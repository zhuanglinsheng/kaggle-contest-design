2026-10-07: Exact limits of a Cole--Hopf verification shortcut

Status: the identities and the restricted no-go statements below are
proved algebraically. They do not establish or refute Proposition 1(ii).
No manuscript assumption or proof has been changed on their basis.

1. Existing Nash equations and the actual equilibrium generator

Fix a positive incentive alpha=theta/c. In reverse time tau=T-t,
put U=(V_i/theta,V_j/theta), z=U_y, and a=lambda S^2. The equations are

  U^i_tau=(a/2)U^i_yy+alpha(z_i^2/2+z_i z_j),
  U^j_tau=(a/2)U^j_yy+alpha(z_j^2/2+z_i z_j).             (T1)

The perceived-gap drift is alpha(z_i+z_j), not the drift of
either component's differentiated linearized equation. Thus

  M=(a/2)partial_yy+alpha(z_i+z_j)partial_y

satisfies

  (partial_tau-M)U^k=-alpha (z_k)^2/2.                     (T2)

2. A constant-diffusion scalar identity

If a>0 is constant and kappa=alpha/a, (T2) gives

  (partial_tau-M)exp(-kappa U^k)=0.                        (T3)

This is a scalar equation along the already selected equilibrium;
its drift still contains both Nash policies. It does not linearize
the entire Nash system into an independent heat equation.

For full disclosure a=sigma^2 and the terminal normalized values
are the two complementary winner indicators. Their exponentials
therefore sum to the constant 1+exp(-kappa). Bounded scalar
uniqueness gives

  exp(-kappa U^i)+exp(-kappa U^j)=1+exp(-kappa).

Writing R=U^i+U^j and d=U^i-U^j yields

  R=1+(2/kappa)log[cosh(kappa d/2)/cosh(kappa/2)].          (T4)

This verifies the existing full-disclosure value relation directly.
With a smooth finite-precision terminal winning probability, the
terminal sum is not constant, even if a happens to be constant.
Hence the same conclusion (T4) cannot be inferred in that game.

3. The precise extra term at variable precision-induced diffusion

If a=a(t)>0 and kappa(t)=alpha/a(t), use forward calendar time.
For E_k=exp(-kappa(t)U^k), the HJB and the chain rule give

  (partial_t+M)E_k=-kappa'(t)U^k E_k.                      (T5)

All gradient-square terms cancel, but this time-derivative term
remains. Along the Riccati path S'=sigma^2-lambda S^2,

  kappa'(t)=-2kappa(t) S'(t)/S(t).

It is positive when S decreases and negative when S increases.
It vanishes only on a stationary posterior path. A martingale
argument omitting (T5) is therefore invalid for general S0.

4. Classification of universal smooth point transforms

This classification concerns transforms that cancel the quadratic
gradient terms FOR EVERY z, not a special identity on an unknown
particular equilibrium trajectory. Assume sufficient smoothness
for the mixed derivatives below and fix alpha,a>0.

First ask whether E=psi(U^i,U^j) can satisfy the heat equation
without the equilibrium drift. Coefficient comparison in (T1)
requires, with kappa=alpha/a,

  psi_ii=kappa psi_i,
  psi_jj=kappa psi_j,
  psi_ij=kappa(psi_i+psi_j).                               (T6)

Differentiate the first identity in j and the last in i:

  psi_iij=kappa^2(psi_i+psi_j),
  psi_iji=kappa^2(2psi_i+psi_j).

Equality implies psi_i=0. Interchanging i and j gives psi_j=0.
Thus only constant smooth point transforms produce an independent
heat equation by this universal coefficient-cancellation method.

If the scalar equation retains the actual equilibrium drift M,
the required conditions instead become

  psi_ii=-kappa psi_i, psi_jj=-kappa psi_j, psi_ij=0.

Their general solution is

  psi=C0+Ci exp(-kappa U^i)+Cj exp(-kappa U^j).             (T7)

At variable a(t), allowing these coefficients to depend on time
does not cancel (T5) as a universal identity: differentiation
introduces the independent functions U^i exp(-kappa U^i) and
U^j exp(-kappa U^j). If kappa' is nonzero, their coefficients
force Ci=Cj=0. Time-dependent coefficient multiples alone
therefore do not repair the varying-diffusion problem.

At a=0 and alpha>0, direct comparison of the z_i^2,z_j^2
coefficients likewise forces psi_i=psi_j=0 in either version.

5. Consequence for the compression task

The full-disclosure relation is directly verifiable by (T3)-(T4).
That verification provides a candidate, not a comparison with
every other prize, deadline and finite precision. Equations
(T5)-(T7) rule out this particular universal point-transform
shortcut to that global comparison. They do NOT rule out
nonlocal transforms, averaged inequalities, or transforms valid
only on a specially proved invariant manifold.

The next admissible short-proof route must handle the averaged
output (for example REAL_OUTPUT_VARIATION_AUDIT.txt), or directly
control the payoff of competitive finite-feedback designs.
Do not cite (T4) alone as proof of their strict exclusion.
