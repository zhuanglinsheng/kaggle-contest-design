2026-10-07: Coarse root-output certificate

Status: the scalar algebra below is proved; the analytic root-output
certificate is NOT proved. This is not a replacement of the precision
estimates, nor a new hypothesis on the organizer's utility.

1. A closed simplification already applied to the manuscript

For U(w)=1+w+12w^2/5, the zero-initial-variance normalized payoff
upper bound can be maximized exactly over the deadline ratio x:

  max_x [(1-(sqrt(x)U)^(-gamma))/gamma-w-(x^2-1)/4]
    = (gamma+4)/(4 gamma)
       [1-U^(-4 gamma/(gamma+4))]-w.

The maximizing x is U^(-2 gamma/(gamma+4)).
For 0<w<1/4,

  d/dw [-log(1-4w)/4-log U]
    =w(1+108w)/[5(1-4w)U]>0.

The limiting maximum as gamma increases to infinity is therefore
strictly negative for w>0. Dividing by w^2 extends continuously to
w=0, with limit -1/10. Uniform convergence then permits a finite,
universal sufficiently large gamma.

At x=1,w=0 the finite-gamma Hessian has
F_xx=-(gamma+4)/4 and determinant gamma/20-19/5.
For gamma>76 it is negative definite. The initial-variance ratio a
can consequently be made small by increasing the constructed b:
the unique zero maximum is preserved locally by the Hessian, and
on its compact complement by strict payoff separation.

This replaces the manuscript's stationary-point exclusion with an
exact deadline maximization and a compact perturbation argument.
It does not replace the preceding equilibrium output estimate.

2. A genuinely weaker possible replacement of that output estimate

Fix a deadline and precision. Let f(w)=B(w)/b(T) denote the normalized
root output, continued in the complex incentive parameter w.
Suppose one could establish, UNIFORMLY over the needed deadlines
and precisions:

  f is analytic on a neighborhood of |w|<=1/2, and |f(w)|<=2 there. (C1)

Continuation means the analytic interior HJB and designer linear PDE,
not an economic equilibrium with a complex or negative prize.
The first coefficient is k=sigma^2 T/(S0+sigma^2 T).
The second coefficient is at most k/2: the exact second-order
precision kernel is bounded by full disclosure, and the verified
full-disclosure small-prize bound yields this upper bound.

Cauchy's coefficient bound would then give, for 0<=w<1/2,

  f(w)<=1+k w+(k/2)w^2+16w^3/(1-2w).                       (C2)

This is only a root-output bound, not a uniform bound on gradients
or their spatial derivatives. Its allowance for third-order terms
is much looser than a fixed 12/5 coefficient throughout the screen.

3. Exact scalar check of the available slack

At k=1 put U=1+w+w^2/2+16w^3/(1-2w).
Then

  1-(1-4w)U^4 = w^2 P(w)/(1-2w)^4,

where P has power coefficients
  [8,-106,991/2,-623,-2274,19743/2,-113697/16,
   -118989/4,159705/2,-21600,-131625,202500].

All 12 Bernstein coefficients of P on [0,1/4] exceed 1/4.
Consequently U^4(1-4w)<1 for 0<w<=1/4.
This yields the same strictly negative limiting payoff comparison
as in Section 1. Near w=0 the gap divided by w^2 tends to -2.
A sufficiently large finite gamma and sufficiently small initial
variance ratio can again be handled by the compact/Hessian argument.

Thus (C1), if proved independently and briefly, would suffice to
replace the existing precision estimates by Cauchy's formula and
a short scalar comparison. It is not currently a theorem.

4. Counterexample screening, not certification

A temporary complex-HJB diagnostic at S0/(sigma^2 T)=1e-4,
|w|=.5, precisions .001,1,100,10000 and four phases
0,pi/4,pi/2,pi found root-output moduli below 1.52.
This did not falsify (C1); it does NOT establish analytic continuation,
uniform boundedness, or reliable behavior between sampled points.

Real-prize existence from Lemma 1 does not imply (C1).
Existing small-prize analyticity does not establish radius 1/2.
Any subsequent argument must prove these two missing properties,
not substitute the diagnostic for them.

5. Exact full-disclosure coefficients: positivity is not a shortcut

This calculation uses the S0/(sigma^2 T) -> 0 limit of the explicit
full-disclosure equilibrium. Set sigma=T=1 and g=2 Phi(z)-1.
With R=U_i+U_j and D=U_i-U_j, its expansion is

  D=g+w^2(g-g^3)/12+O(w^4),
  R=1+w(g^2-1)/4+O(w^3).

The stationary designer profile solves

  (L0-1/2)b+w R' b'+w D'/2=0,
  L0=(partial_zz+z partial_z)/2.

Writing b=beta+w b1+w^2 b2+w^3 b3+... gives

  b1=G,
  (L0-1/2)b2=-g^2 G/2,
  (L0-1/2)b3=z g G^2-(1-3g^2)G/12.

The resolvent is -integral_0^infinity e^(-t/2) P_t dt,
where P_t is Gaussian convolution with variance e^t-1 at z=0.
After x=e^(-t), Gaussian integration gives

  b2(0)/G(0)=(1/pi) integral_0^1
                  asin((1-x)/(2-x)) dx
             =1/3-(sqrt(3)-1)/pi,

  b3(0)/G(0)=1/12
       -(1/(2 pi)) integral_0^1 x/[(2-x)sqrt(3-2x)] dx
       -(1/pi) integral_0^1 (1-x)/[(2-x)sqrt(3-2x)] dx
             =1/12-(sqrt(3)-1)/(2 pi)<0.

For the second identity, integration by parts changes the asin
integral into integral x/[(2-x)sqrt(3-2x)] dx.
The two third-order integrals then combine to
integral [2 sqrt(3-2x)]^(-1) dx=(sqrt(3)-1)/2.

Thus the third coefficient is analytically negative, not merely
negative in a numerical approximation. One cannot infer the
complex-disc maximum from real-prize bounds by assuming positive
Taylor coefficients. This is not a counterexample to analyticity.

Also b2(0)/G(0)<1/8, since 24(sqrt(3)-1)>5 pi.
The exact full-disclosure profile implies continuity of this
coefficient in the initial variance ratio. Together with the
second-order precision-kernel comparison, f2<1/8 holds at every
precision when that ratio is sufficiently small. The constructed
reference point can meet this condition uniformly on its compact
deadline range.

6. A more permissive centered certificate

The unproved analytic-radius requirement can be reduced from 1/2
to 27/100 if, instead of bounding the whole output, one bounds its
centered remainder:

  h(w)=[f(w)-1-k w]/w^2 is analytic through |w|<=27/100,
  and |h(w)|<=2 on that disc.                               (C3)

The value at w=0 is the removable extension f2.
Cauchy's coefficient bound and Section 5 then give

  f(w)<=1+k w+w^2/8+2w^3/(27/100-w) for 0<=w<27/100.        (C4)

At k=1 let U denote this right side. Exact rational arithmetic
verifies U^4(1-4w)<1 on 0<w<=1/4:
multiply the margin by (27/100-w)^4 and divide by w^2;
all Bernstein coefficients are positive on each of
[0,1/16], [1/16,1/8], [1/8,3/16], [3/16,1/4].
The smallest is 281291929387/442918502400000000.
For 1/4<=w<=.252 the payoff sign is immediate as before.
At zero initial variance, the local finite-gamma Hessian satisfies
F_xx=-(gamma+4)/4 and det(D^2F)=(19 gamma+12)/16>0.
Hence the same compact perturbation argument applies.

verify_root_output_scalar_certificates.py verifies these scalar
polynomial identities and coefficient signs with exact fractions.
It does NOT verify (C3).

Numerical Fourier probing at radius .35 found mixed signs in the
output coefficients at precisions 100 and 10000, consistent with
Section 5. At .5 the earlier sampled moduli were below 1.52.
These checks only screen proposed shortcuts for counterexamples.

(C3) is now a sharper research target than (C1): a smaller complex
radius and a generous bound on the centered remainder. Its
uniform validity has not been established by Lemma 1 or by an
independent estimate. The long manuscript estimate is retained.

7. Further reduction: scalar coefficients rather than complex equilibria

An alternative sufficient obligation is the scalar coefficient bound

  |f_n| <= 2(100/27)^(n-2), n>=2,                         (C5)

uniformly over the relevant prior ratios and precisions.
This implies convergence of the scalar root-output Taylor series
at radius 27/100 and the centered remainder estimate in (C4).
The second coefficient can still use the sharper bound 1/8.

For each fixed positive finite precision, the existing classical
real-prize solution can be treated locally by parameter-analytic
semilinear parabolic theory. If this local analyticity is established
along the real prize interval, the convergent scalar series and
the actual root output agree initially and then agree throughout
the interval by analytic continuation. Uniform global existence
of complex Nash solutions is not needed for this argument.

Zero precision needs the specified analytic branch on the required
real interval; full disclosure has its explicit analytic profiles.
Neither of these boundary requirements may be inferred merely
from positive-precision existence in Lemma 1.

Thus (C5) would be a sufficient, weaker route than (C3). A possible
proof is a positive majorant for Gaussian heat-kernel coefficient
trees after root integration, using Q_t>=sigma^2(T-t). Spatial
derivative norms and finite coefficients alone do not prove (C5).
No all-order root coefficient majorant has yet been derived.

8. A verified obstruction to the naive no-signal coefficient majorant

One cannot simply apply the no-signal Gaussian majorant at the
total variance Q_t and declare it uniform in precision.
The first coefficient of the imbalance A already gives a counterexample.
Normalize sigma=T=1 and let the initial prior ratio tend to zero.
With Fourier convention hat(f)(xi)=integral exp(-i xi y) f(y) dy,
the exact first-variation formula gives

  hat(A1)(xi)=-(i xi)/(2 sqrt(pi))
       integral_0^1 Q_s^(-1/2)
         exp[-(1-Q_s/2)xi^2/2] ds.

At zero signal Q_s=1. Thus

  lim_{xi -> 0} |hat(A1)(xi)|/|xi|=1/(2 sqrt(pi)).

At full disclosure Q_s=1-s, and the corresponding limit is

  (1/(2 sqrt(pi))) integral_0^1 (1-s)^(-1/2) ds
    =1/sqrt(pi).

The full-disclosure coefficient is twice as large near zero
frequency. The no-signal Fourier majorant therefore fails already
at first order. Heat smoothing cannot be treated as merely
decreasing the entire nonlinear coefficient sequence.

This does not disprove (C5). It identifies an input that an
all-order root-tree estimate must preserve: smoothing broadens
the spatial Gaussian but increases its low-frequency amplitude
through the Q_s^(-1/2) time integral. An admissible majorant must
handle both effects, rather than keeping only the former.

9. A finite real-parameter alternative to all-order analyticity

All-order coefficient control is NOT necessary. A sufficient replacement
is a real-parameter third-variation bound for the scalar initial output:

  f is C^3 on [0,.252], and f'''(w)<=42 there,              (C6)

uniformly over the reference construction's compact deadline range
and all finite precisions, including the specified no-signal branch.
This is an upper bound, not an absolute-value bound. Negative third
variations help the comparison. Its validity is NOT established here.

With f(0)=1, f'(0)=k<=1 and f''(0)/2<=1/8, the integral Taylor
formula would imply

  f(w)<=1+k w+w^2/8+7w^3.                                 (C7)

This avoids both complex Nash equilibria and infinite coefficient
estimates. The algebraic comparison needs no interval computation:
for 0<w<1/4, the binomial expansion has positive coefficients and

  (1-4w)^(-1/4)
     >1+w+(5/2)w^2+(15/2)w^3
     >1+w+w^2/8+7w^3=:U(w).

Therefore (1-4w)U(w)^4<1. At w>=1/4 the limiting optimized payoff
is automatically negative. Near zero its ratio to w^2 tends to
1/8-5/2=-19/8. The exact deadline maximization in Section 1,
followed by the same finite-curvature and small-prior perturbations,
then yields the strict payoff screen. The local Hessian determinant
is (19 gamma+12)/16, as in Section 6.

Only the real, third-variation statement (C6) is missing for this
route. Positive-precision equilibrium existence does not imply a
UNIFORM bound as precision tends to zero or infinity. Nor does the
negative full-disclosure third coefficient at zero prize establish
(C6) throughout a positive prize interval.

Research priority: derive a one-sided third-variation bound for the
initial designer output directly from the differentiated HJB and
linear output equations. Do not replace this obligation with a
pointwise spatial-gradient bound unless that bound is independently
shorter; do not cite the finite numerical probes as certification.
