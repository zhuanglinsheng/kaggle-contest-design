2026-10-07: A payoff-ratio route to shortening Lemma 3

Status: exact reduction and a conditional short argument, NOT a replacement
proof. No new assumption has been inserted into the manuscript.

1. Object and independence from effort cost

Fix S0, sigma and the selected symmetric equilibria. Put alpha=theta/c.
The normalized HJB and effort policies depend on alpha, not separately
on theta and c. Thus define

  J(alpha,T,lambda)=u(B(0,c alpha,T,lambda;S0))-D(T).

This function is independent of the c used on the right-hand side.
Let V0=max_T J(0,T,lambda); zero-prize output is precision-independent.
For alpha>0 write

  Q(alpha,T,lambda)=[J(alpha,T,lambda)-V0]/alpha.

Then the exact net-payoff difference is

  J(alpha,T,lambda)-c alpha-V0=alpha[Q(alpha,T,lambda)-c].   (R1)

All statements concern precisions at which the selected output is defined.
This reduction does not supply an equilibrium at zero precision.

2. The global prize-exit threshold

Let C_exit=sup Q over positive alpha, positive T and admissible precision.
The independent high-cost argument in Proposition 1(iii) implies that
C_exit is finite. The full-disclosure first variation at the zero-prize
optimal deadline T0 gives

  lim_{alpha down to 0} Q(alpha,T0,infinity)
    =T0 u'(sqrt((S0+sigma^2 T0)/(2 pi)))
       /sqrt(2 pi(S0+sigma^2 T0))=:c_e>0.

Hence C_exit>=c_e. By (R1), no positive-prize design beats V0 when
c>=C_exit, whereas some design beats V0 when c<C_exit.
This statement is about payoff comparison, not global attainment.
At c=C_exit a positive-prize design can tie V0.

3. What would actually close a short proof of case (ii)

The needed payoff-level certificate, for a suitable sigma>sigma_F, is

  sup_{alpha>=epsilon,T,lambda<infinity} Q(alpha,T,lambda)<c_e
  for the fixed positive epsilon needed to apply Lemma 2.              (R2)

It is enough to take the supremum over designs that can compete for
c in [c_e/2,c_e]. Indeed, bounded utility gives
theta+D(T)<=u_infinity-V0. Thus alpha and T have uniform upper bounds.
The energy estimate B<=b(T)+sqrt(2 alpha T), together with
V0>u(b(0)), excludes T approaching zero on this bounded alpha range.
Competitive deadlines therefore lie in a fixed positive compact interval.

Choose epsilon small enough that alpha<epsilon implies
alpha/sigma^2<w_* for Lemma 2 on this deadline interval.
If the supremum in (R2) is c_e-g with g>0, every competitive
finite-precision design at c_e-g<c<c_e must have alpha<epsilon,
by (R1). Lemma 2 strictly improves it by full disclosure.
Meanwhile the full-disclosure entry derivative is c_e/c-1>0, so the
attained full-disclosure maximum beats V0. It is then a joint maximum.

This argument needs only one fixed epsilon, not convergence of the
deadline or a localization statement for every epsilon.

4. Why local derivatives and ordinary energy bounds do not prove (R2)

The energy relaxation gives B<=b(T)+sqrt(2 alpha T). At T=T0 its
utility gain is O(sqrt(alpha)), too large to establish a finite
linear prize-entry threshold. It cannot supply the required certificate.

Also, a first variation equal to c_e does not imply C_exit=c_e.
For illustration only, a smooth scalar gain

  J(alpha)-V0=c_e alpha+a alpha^2-b alpha^3,  a,b>0,

has the same entry slope, but Q is maximized at alpha=a/(2b), with
value c_e+a^2/(4b)>c_e. This is not a counterexample to the contest
model; it demonstrates the invalidity of a local-to-global inference.

The existing long output estimate proves the missing global exclusion.
A genuine shorter proof must establish (R2) independently, using the
actual Nash output. Merely introducing Q, citing the maximum theorem,
or assuming organizer-payoff concavity does not close it.

5. Scope correction: (R2) is sufficient, not the minimal target

Proposition 1(ii) requires a single parameter point sigma>sigma_F,c>0,
not a cost window, arbitrarily small competitive prizes, or deadline
convergence. Let V_infinity be the attained full-disclosure optimum
at that point. The entry variation with c<c_e ensures V_infinity>V0.

Only finite-precision designs with payoff >= V_infinity need exclusion.
Designs whose payoff lies between V0 and V_infinity are irrelevant.
The manuscript's old global comparison against V0 is thus stronger
than required. In particular, proving C_exit=c_e is NOT necessary
for positive-prize full-disclosure joint optimality.

For a short replacement using Lemma 2, first use the payoff budget
and energy estimate to place potentially beating designs in a
positive compact deadline interval. Let W be the weak-prize range
on that interval from Lemma 2. The exact remaining obligation is:

  at ONE sigma>sigma_F and c<c_e, every finite-precision design
  outside W has payoff STRICTLY LESS than V_infinity.                 (R3)

Inside W, Lemma 2 excludes a design with payoff >= V_infinity:
switching it to full disclosure would produce payoff > V_infinity.
Outside W, (R3) excludes it directly. Zero prizes give at most V0,
and zero deadlines give at most u(b(0)), both below V_infinity.

(R3) is pointwise strict in designs; a uniform positive gap over all
finite precisions is not required. This matters because lambda may
approach infinity. A coarse candidate comparison would instead
require a uniform margin and can unnecessarily strengthen the task.

The manuscript lemma has been narrowed to precisely this comparison
role at one parameter point. Its current proof still uses the stronger
V0-based output estimate internally; that estimate has not been
removed because no independent proof of (R3) has yet been established.
Next research should target (R3), not re-prove global prize exit.
