2026-10-07: Real-prize variations of the initial organizer output

Status: exact identities for regular parameter-dependent equilibria,
not a proof of the uniform third-variation bound. No new assumption
or unproved output estimate has been inserted into the manuscript.

1. Normalization and the two different linear operators

Fix T, sigma, S0 and precision. Rescale scores by sigma sqrt(T)
and time by T, so sigma=T=1 and the incentive parameter is
w=theta/(c sigma^2). Hold the normalized prior and precision fixed
when differentiating w. In reverse time tau, let z=U_y, where
U=(V_i/theta,V_j/theta). The normalized equations are

  U_tau=(a/2)U_yy+w F(U_y),
  F_i(z)=z_i^2/2+z_i z_j,
  F_j(z)=z_j^2/2+z_i z_j.

The terminal normalized values are independent of w.
Write p=z_i, n=-z_j, A=p-n and H=p+n.

The Jacobian of F is

  DF(z)=[[A,p],[-n,A]].                                    (S1)

For p,n>0 its eigenvalues are A +/- i sqrt(p n). It is NOT
a cooperative first-order coupling. Therefore scalar comparison
or a Markov-generator interpretation cannot be applied directly
to the coupled Nash parameter variations.

This issue is separate from the scalar organizer equation below.

2. Exact finite hierarchy for Nash prize variations

Define the symmetric bilinear map

  C_i(x,y)=x_i y_i/2+(x_i y_j+x_j y_i)/2,
  C_j(x,y)=x_j y_j/2+(x_i y_j+x_j y_i)/2,

so F(z)=C(z,z). Let U_j=partial_w^j U and z_j=(U_j)_y,
with U_0=U, z_0=z. (Indices here denote derivatives, not players.)
The common variational operator is

  L_N U_j=(U_j)_tau-(a/2)(U_j)_yy-w DF(z)(U_j)_y.

For j=1,2,3, direct differentiation gives

  L_N U_1=C(z,z),
  L_N U_2=4C(z,z_1)+2w C(z_1,z_1),
  L_N U_3=6C(z,z_2)+6C(z_1,z_1)+6w C(z_1,z_2).            (S2)

All U_j for j>=1 have zero terminal data. No fourth or higher
parameter derivative occurs. These identities are conditional only
on the indicated differentiability, not on a complex-prize extension.

3. Exact scalar output hierarchy

Let K(y,t;w) be the conditional remaining best output, with the
current average score set to zero. Its terminal value is
beta(y,S_T)=E|N(y,S_T)|/2, independent of w, and

  K_t+(a_t/2)K_yy+w A K_y+w H/2=0.                         (S3)

This equation follows by separating the average-score drift
w H/2 from the perceived-gap drift w A; the terminal posterior
accounts for the remaining latent-gap uncertainty. In particular,
K(0,0;w) is the normalized initial output, not a contestant value.

Set K_j=partial_w^j K, A_j=partial_w^j A and H_j=partial_w^j H.
Use the forward-time generator L_K=(a_t/2)partial_yy+w A partial_y.
Every K_j, j>=1, has zero terminal data and solves

  (partial_t+L_K)K_j+R_j=0.

The sources needed through order three are

  R_1=A K_y+H/2+w(A_1 K_y+H_1/2),

  R_2=2A K_1,y+2A_1 K_y+H_1
      +w(2A_1 K_1,y+A_2 K_y+H_2/2),

  R_3=3A K_2,y+6A_1 K_1,y+3A_2 K_y+3H_2/2
      +w(3A_1 K_2,y+3A_2 K_1,y+A_3 K_y+H_3/2).            (S4)

For fixed positive finite precision, assuming sufficient regularity
and integrability, Feynman--Kac gives the exact root identity

  f'''(w)=E_w integral_0^1 R_3(Y_t,t;w) dt / b,
  b=sqrt((S0+1)/(2 pi)),                                 (S5)

where f=K(0,0;w)/b and dY=w A(Y,t;w)dt+sqrt(a_t)dW_t.
At zero precision, the same identity is deterministic on a
regular branch. Endpoint/uniform claims require separate control.

Equation (S5) already includes the change in the equilibrium
state distribution: the drift-derivative terms in (S4) account
for it. Differentiating only the integrand of an existing
expectation while holding its law fixed would be incorrect.

4. What is sufficient and what is still missing

Section 9 of ROOT_OUTPUT_ANALYTIC_CERTIFICATE.txt shows that
the one-sided estimate f'''<=42 on 0<=w<=.252 would suffice.
By (S5), it is enough to prove

  E_w integral_0^1 R_3(Y_t,t;w)dt <=42 b                  (S6)

uniformly in the relevant reference deadlines and precisions.
One does NOT need uniform absolute suprema of every term in (S4),
or parameter derivatives above order three. Near full disclosure,
some pointwise source terms may become singular near the deadline;
their expected time integrals are the quantities that matter.

Neither (S2) nor (S5) establishes (S6). The remaining task is a
one-sided, averaged bound involving the first three Nash variations,
not scalar maximum-principle control of the Nash system.

5. Explicit check against a naive elliptic estimate

Freeze p=n=g>0 and a>0 in (S1). The homogeneous variational system
has Fourier modes with growth exponents

  -a xi^2/2 +/- w g xi.

Their largest exponent is w^2 g^2/(2a), which diverges as a decreases
to zero. Thus fixed-precision elliptic stability does NOT by itself
supply the required precision-uniform bound. This is a frozen-
coefficient operator calculation, not a counterexample to the
actual contest equilibrium or its Gaussian terminal data.

Gaussian terminal regularity can offset high-frequency growth;
a valid short proof must preserve that regularity or exploit the
expectation in (S5), instead of quoting an elliptic bound whose
constant deteriorates when public diffusion vanishes.
