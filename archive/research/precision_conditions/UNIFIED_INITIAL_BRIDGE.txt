1. A broader Gaussian strip removes the derivative inputs

Use E_q(z)=q^(-1/2)exp(-z^2/(4q)) and the norm
sup |F(y+ih,t)|/|E_Qt(y+ih)| on |h|<=R sqrt(Q_t).
In T(F,G), shift the source contour to h_s=Q_s h/Q_t. This is
inside the source strip. Gaussian completion of squares gives
the phase factor exp(h^2/(4Q_t))<=exp(R^2/4).
The real part of the derivative kernel has the real norm constant
2sqrt(2)exp(-1/4)<2.21. For the imaginary part, its coefficient
(h-h_s)/(Q_t-Q_s)=h/Q_t is independent of s. Integration of
P(E_Qs^2)/E_Qt over variance gives 2sqrt(Q_t)exp(-y^2/(4Q_t));
therefore its norm constant is 2R. With K=alpha/a_min,
 alpha ||T(F,G)|| <=K exp(R^2/4)(2.21+2R)||F||||G||.
Gaussian domination justifies the contour shifts and variance limits.

The source bound is also explicit. Write u=y/sqrt(Q_t), h=eta sqrt(Q_t),
v=Q_s/Q_t and V=1-v/2. After taking absolute values and dividing by
the target E-weight, the derivative source integrand has factor
 sqrt(u^2+eta^2) exp[-beta u^2+beta eta^2], beta=1/(2V)-1/4.
For R<sqrt(2/3), maximization over u gives
 (2beta)^(-1/2)exp(-1/2+2beta eta^2).
Since 2beta<=3/2, this implies
 ||A1||<=K exp(3R^2/2) exp(-1/2)atanh(sqrt(2/3))/pi
        <.222K exp(3R^2/2).
The elementary remaining integral is
 integral_0^1 dv/[sqrt(v)(1-v/2)sqrt(1/2+v/4)]
     =2sqrt(2)atanh(sqrt(2/3)).

At R=.45 and K<=.35, the centered mild map contracts and preserves
 ||E_H||<=.105(K/.35)^2, ||A||<=.192(K/.35).
Its real restriction preserves the sharper zeroth-order bounds
 ||E_H||<=.04(K/.35)^2, ||A||<=.11(K/.35).
All scalar inequalities are checked in verify_broad_gaussian_strip.py.
No differentiated Nash equation is used. Uniqueness identifies the
analytic fixed point with the real symmetric equilibrium.

At delta let z=w/.252. Every Cauchy coefficient is bounded by its
complex sup norm times (.45)^(-n). The complex sup introduces
exp(.45^2/4), which must not be omitted. Consequently all positive
orders of (E_H+eta A)/2 have the rescaled majorant

 exp(.45^2/4)(.105+eta*.192)/2 <.232.
Only the real zeroth-order caps .04z^2 and .11z are retained.

3. Coupling the early and mature outputs

Set Ebar=.04+e1/2, Jbar=.11+j1/2, Ibar=.11/2+j1/3,
J(s)=.11+j1(1-s), I(s)=integral_0^s J(v)dv.
Couple the equilibrium mean Y with the driftless mean Y0 using
the same Brownian motion. Then
 |Y_t-Y0_t|<=alpha z delta I(s)/sqrt(q),
 E|beta_y(Y0_t,Q_t)|<=sqrt(9/40)/pi<.152.
Also |beta_yy|<=.4/sqrt(q), |G'_Qt|<.243/q and
E G_Qt(Y0_t)=G_Q0(0). The exact split output identity is
 B-b=alpha E integral_0^delta [G_Qt+E_H/2+beta_y A]dt
              +E[K(Y_delta,delta)-beta(Y_delta,q)].
The mature statewise bound (M6b), rather than a bound only at
the initial mean, supplies the final term.

Put L=sqrt(2pi Q0/q)<2.8 and g=(alpha delta/q)/w<.64.
Use integral J I=Jbar^2/2 to obtain an early excess coefficient
 CE <=2.8[Ebar/(2*.252)+.152 Jbar/.252+.4*.64 Jbar^2/2]
           +.6804*.64 Ibar.
The mature excess coefficient satisfies
