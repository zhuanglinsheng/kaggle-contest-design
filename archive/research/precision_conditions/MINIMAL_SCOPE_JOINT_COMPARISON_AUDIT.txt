1. Problem and resolved scope

The old Lemma 3 proved a stronger assertion than Proposition 1(ii)
needs: arbitrary fixed S0 and arbitrarily high sigma. Its normalization
forced S0/(sigma^2 T0) near zero and introduced a difficult low-diffusion
initial layer. Proposition 1 already assumes S0 sufficiently large and
only asks for one parameter point above the finite-feedback risk interval.

The replacement uses exactly that existing assumption. It proves, for
every sufficiently large S0, a point sigma>1,c<c_e where every finite
feedback design is strictly below the attained full-disclosure maximum.
The part-(i) threshold is chosen <=1, without changing that result.
No new utility or delay-cost assumption, numerical optimum, or conditional
attainment assertion has been introduced. The unnecessary stronger
Lemma 3 statement, not the Proposition, has been removed.

2. Proof dependency chain

Fix the innovation share k=.01 in the proof, not in the model:
 b=sqrt(S0/[2pi(1-k)]), M=b u'(b),
 T0 D'(T0)=Mk/2, sigma^2 T0=k S0/(1-k).
This is the exact zero-prize optimum. As S0 grows, T0 decreases to zero
and sigma grows without bound. Its prize-entry threshold satisfies
c_e sigma^2=Mk.

The bounded utility tail and local quadratic delay cost restrict every
competitive design near c=c_e to w+x^2/4<.252, x=T/T0<2. Concavity alone
gives output>.99b. A coarse uniform output bound then suffices:
 B<=b(T)+alpha T G_(S0+sigma^2T)(0)(1+2.9w).

There are only two elementary diffusion checks:
 - If lambda S0^2<.72 sigma^2, S_T>=S0 throughout the contest and
   alpha T/S_T<=2w/99<.01. A simple Gaussian analytic supersolution
   gives an excess coefficient below .01, including no signal.
 - Otherwise a_t>=.72 sigma^2 for the whole contest. A single positive
   Gaussian convolution identity gives the centered-gradient contraction;
   direct verification of the designer linear equation gives coefficient
   below 2.6. The simpler ball E<=6w^2, A<=3w replaces the normalized
   z-dependent bounds. No spatial strip or differentiated Nash estimates.

The joint payoff screen, not the output ranking, excludes strong prizes.
With h=1+k(x-1), r=sqrt(h)+kx(w+2.9w^2)/sqrt(h), its upper bound is
 F_gamma=(1-r^(-gamma))/(gamma k)-w-(x^2-1)/4.
On the zero-relative-cost sublevel, r<1 except at (1,0), by one
explicit polynomial factorization. At a fixed sufficiently large gamma0
(e.g. 10000), the local Hessian is negative definite. F_gamma decreases
pointwise as gamma increases; an increasing open cover of the compact
complement then makes F_gamma strictly negative elsewhere for
some fixed large gamma. The utility-tail assumption supplies this fixed
curvature for all sufficiently large S0; it is not an extra assumption.

Taking c just below c_e preserves exclusion on x<=1/2 and w>=w0.
Every remaining competitive design has prior ratio in [99/2,198] and
w<w_*, so the existing Lemma 2 strictly improves it by full disclosure.
All other designs already lie below the zero-prize reference. Therefore
the comparison is global over prizes, deadlines and finite precisions.

Finally the full-disclosure objective attains its maximum on a compact
competitive set. Its positive entry derivative c_e/c-1>0 puts that
maximum strictly above the zero-prize reference, so its prize and
deadline are positive. Lemma 3 excludes every finite-precision rival;
zero prize and zero deadline cannot tie. This proves Proposition 1(ii).
