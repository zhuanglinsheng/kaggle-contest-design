DIRECT VERIFICATION OF THE FULL-DISCLOSURE BENCHMARK
2026-10-07

Status: an exact comparison identity and an obstruction to pointwise
verification. This is NOT a closed replacement proof of Proposition 1(ii).
The manuscript has not been changed on the strength of this calculation.

1. Exact identity using existing equilibria

Fix theta,T,c,sigma, and write alpha=theta/c. For each existing equilibrium,
write A=(m_i-m_j)/alpha and H=(m_i+m_j)/alpha. Let F_infty(y,t) be the
conditional remaining best output in the full-disclosure equilibrium, with
the current average score normalized to zero. It solves the LINEAR equation

  F_t + sigma^2 F_yy/2 + alpha A_infty F_y + alpha H_infty/2 = 0,
  F(y,T)=|y|/2.

Let P_S be Gaussian convolution with variance S and set
Fbar=P_{S_t}F_infty. The Riccati identity S_t'+lambda S_t^2=sigma^2 gives

  Fbar_t + lambda S_t^2 Fbar_yy/2
    = -alpha P_{S_t}(A_infty F_infty,y + H_infty/2).

Consequently define the exact residual

  R_lambda = alpha [ A_lambda Fbar_y + H_lambda/2
                     -P_{S_t}(A_infty F_infty,y+H_infty/2) ].

Dynkin's formula along the finite-precision equilibrium gives

  B_lambda-B_infty = E_lambda integral_0^T R_lambda(Y_t,t) dt.       (V1)

Here Fbar(0,0)=B_infty, and its terminal value is the conditional best
terminal gap contribution, E|N(Y_T,S_T)|/2. The accumulated average effort
accounts for the remaining part of B_lambda. Spatial localization and
stopping at T-epsilon are justified by finite expected effort energy and
the at-most-linear growth of F_infty; the final interval contribution
vanishes by Cauchy--Schwarz. These arguments apply to an existing regular
equilibrium, not to an arbitrary selection of Nash equilibria.

For joint design, (V1) must be compared at every competitive theta,T.
Knowing that its integral is nonpositive only at the candidate optimal
theta,T does not rule out another finite-feedback design at different
theta,T. No new existence proof is required, but this global design
comparison is still required.

2. The pointwise supersolution shortcut is false

The tempting sufficient condition R_lambda<=0 at every state/time cannot
be used: it fails even at arbitrarily small positive prizes. The failure
can be proved from exact first variations, without a nonlinear PDE solver.

Hold sigma,T,S0 fixed with S0>0. Set Q_t=S_t+sigma^2(T-t). Analytic small
prize equilibria have, at fixed finite lambda and fixed t<T,

  H_lambda=2G_{Q_t}+O(alpha^2),
  A_lambda=alpha A_{1,lambda}+O(alpha^2),

where G_q is the centered Gaussian density of variance q. Differentiating
the HJB at alpha=0 gives the exact first-variation formula

  A_{1,lambda}(y,t)
    = - integral_t^T P_{Q_t-Q_r} partial_y(G_{Q_r}^2)(y) dr.        (V2)

There is no first-order variation of H. The explicit full-disclosure
strategy gives, with q=sigma^2(T-t),

  A_{1,infty}(y,t) = [2Phi(y/sqrt(q))-1]G_q(y)/sigma^2.

The zero-prize designer continuation is beta(y,q)=E|N(y,q)|/2, whose
derivative is Phi(y/sqrt(q))-1/2. Therefore

  R_lambda(y,t)/alpha^2
    -> A_{1,lambda}(y,t) beta_y(y,Q_t)
       -P_{S_t}[A_{1,infty} beta_y(.,q)](y)                       (V3)

as alpha decreases to zero, at each fixed finite lambda.

At t=0, Q=S0+sigma^2 T is independent of lambda. For every r>0, the
Riccati solution satisfies S_r -> 0 as lambda -> infinity, and hence
Q_r -> sigma^2(T-r). In (V2), Q_r>=sigma^2(T-r) and
Q-Q_r/2>=Q/2; the integrand has an integrable bound proportional to
(T-r)^(-1/2), for fixed y. Dominated convergence thus gives

  A_{1,lambda}(y,0) -> P_{S0} A_{1,infty}(y,0).

Put K=sigma^2 T, Q=S0+K, and let

  U ~ N(mu,v),  mu=y sqrt(K)/Q,  v=S0/Q,
  delta=E[1-Phi(U)]=1-Phi(mu/sqrt(1+v)),
  epsilon=E[(1-Phi(U))^2],  zeta=1-Phi(y/sqrt(Q)).

Gaussian multiplication now evaluates the limit of the coefficient in
(V3), divided by G_Q(y), as

  [2delta-2zeta+4zeta delta-4epsilon]/(2sigma^2).                  (V4)

To determine its sign, take y to positive infinity. With independent
standard normals X,Z independent of U,

  epsilon=P(X>U,Z>U)
          <=P((X+Z)/2>U)=1-Phi(mu/sqrt(v+1/2)).

Since v+1/2<v+1, normal tail estimates imply epsilon=o(delta).
Also zeta=o(delta): its normal argument divided by the argument defining
delta equals sqrt((Q+S0)/K)>1. Thus (V4) is asymptotic to
delta/sigma^2>0. Choose a large but FIXED y for which it is positive,
then a sufficiently large but FINITE lambda, then a sufficiently small
positive alpha. This order of choices proves R_lambda(y,0)>0.

Continuity gives a nearby positive residual at t>0 as well. Positive
finite precision has a nondegenerate innovation diffusion, so the states
cannot simply be discarded from a global pointwise verification.

This counterexample does NOT show B_lambda>B_infty. The root comparison
in (V1) involves the expectation of the entire time integral, and positive
local residuals may be outweighed by negative residuals elsewhere.

3. What is still required

The valid direct-verification task is to prove the SIGN OF THE EXPECTED
INTEGRAL in (V1), uniformly over the competitive prize/deadline set and
every finite precision. Pointwise nonpositivity must not be assumed.
Existing weak-prize ranking establishes an integrated comparison in its
stated regime, but using it does not by itself replace the separate
global competitive-design localization in the current case (ii) proof.

Numerical diagnostic files are not sign certificates. In particular,
large initial Riccati diffusion is stiff and can invalidate coarse time
steps. The analytic argument (V2)--(V4), not a finite-difference sign,
establishes the obstruction above.
