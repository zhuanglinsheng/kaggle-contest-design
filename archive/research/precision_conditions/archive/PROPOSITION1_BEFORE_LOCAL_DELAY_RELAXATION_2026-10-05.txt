\proof{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop-joint-design}}
We work with the exact equilibria of Theorem~\ref{thm-equilibrium-effort}. Write $\alpha=\theta/c$ and $w=\alpha/\sigma^2$.
We establish the three regions through explicit sufficient subsets,
then prove prize exit and precision separation. The benchmarks used
only in this proof are
\[
T_0(\sigma)=\argmax_{T\geq0}
\left\{u\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{S_0+\sigma^2T}{2\pi}}\right)-D(T)\right\},
\qquad c_e(\sigma)=\frac{2T_0(\sigma)D'(T_0(\sigma))}{\sigma^2}.
\]
Let $\sigma_L,\sigma_U$ solve
$\sigma_L^2T_0(\sigma_L)=S_0/3$ and
$\sigma_U^2T_0(\sigma_U)=3S_0$.
Their existence and ordering are proved below.
For $\sigma_L\leq\sigma\leq\sigma_U$, the full-disclosure subset is
\begin{equation}\label{eq-full-disclosure-region}
\frac9{16}c_e(\sigma)\leq c<c_e(\sigma).
\end{equation}
On the same risk range, $c\geq c_e(\sigma)$ gives only zero-prize
optima. The cost-dependent finite-feedback subset is constructed
below. These are sufficient subsets, not definitions of the exact
precision boundary.
In the extensions below, explicitly stated weaker utility or delay-cost
assumptions replace the corresponding common shape bounds; other
assumptions are retained unless a relaxation is specified.
Let
\[
J(\theta,T,\lambda;\sigma)
:=u\!\left(\mathcal B(0,\theta,T,\lambda;S_0)\right)-\theta-D(T)
\]
denote the organizer's exact payoff.
We first derive the exact output identity used below. For $q>0$, define
\[
\beta(y,q):=\sqrt{\frac{q}{2\pi}}e^{-y^2/(2q)}
+y\left[\Phi(y/\sqrt q)-\frac12\right]
=\frac12\mathbb E|Y|,
\qquad Y\sim\mathcal N(y,q).
\]
It satisfies
\[
\beta_y=\Phi(y/\sqrt q)-\frac12,\qquad
\beta_{yy}=\frac{e^{-y^2/(2q)}}{\sqrt{2\pi q}},\qquad
\beta_q=\frac12\beta_{yy}.
\]
Because $Q_t'=-\lambda S_t^2$, the function $\beta(y,Q_t)$ solves the backward equation associated with the posterior-state diffusion without strategic drift. Moreover, conditional normality gives
$\mathbb E[|y_T|/2\mid I_T]=\beta(\tilde y_T,S_T)$.
Applying It\^o's formula and using
$\max\{x_T^i,x_T^j\}=(x_T^i+x_T^j)/2+|y_T|/2$
therefore yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq-best-output-drift-identity}
\mathcal B=\beta(\tilde y_0,Q_0)
+\mathbb E\!\int_0^T\left\{
\frac{m_i+m_j}{2}
+(m_i-m_j)\left[\Phi(\tilde y_t/\sqrt{Q_t})-\frac12\right]
\right\}dt.
\end{equation}
The policies inside the expectation are evaluated at $(\tilde y_t,t)$. The stochastic integrals have zero expectation because $\beta_y$ and the equilibrium policies are bounded on the fixed horizon.

Adding the players' equilibrium payoffs gives, from every state and date,
\begin{equation}\label{eq-design-energy-budget}
\mathbb E\!\left[\int_t^T(m_i^2+m_j^2)\,ds\mid I_t\right]
=2\alpha\left[1-U_i(\tilde y_t,t)-U_j(\tilde y_t,t)\right]
\leq2\alpha,
\qquad U_k=V^k/\theta.
\end{equation}
In particular $0\leq U_i+U_j\leq1$. Coupling with the same initial scores and innovation paths but zero effort, and applying Cauchy--Schwarz to cumulative efforts, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq-design-basic-output-bound}
0\leq\mathcal B-\sqrt{\frac{S_0+\sigma^2T}{2\pi}}
\leq\sqrt{2\alpha T}.
\end{equation}

\emph{A uniform weak-incentive comparison.}
We next establish the estimate used for part (iii). Rescale time by
$T$, state and output by $\sigma\sqrt T$, and precision by setting
\[
w=\frac{\theta}{c\sigma^2},\qquad
s=\frac{S_0}{\sigma^2T},\qquad
\ell=\lambda\sigma^2T^2.
\]
Write $\widehat{\mathcal B}(w,s,\ell)=
\mathcal B(0,\theta,T,\lambda;S_0)/(\sigma\sqrt T)$ and let
$\widehat{\mathcal B}(w,s,\infty)$ be its high-precision limit.
There is a numerical $w_*>0$ such that, for every
$s\geq1/4$, every finite $\ell\geq0$, and $0<w<w_*$,
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathcal B}(w,s,\infty)
>\widehat{\mathcal B}(w,s,\ell).\label{eq-uniform-disclosure-ranking}
\end{equation}
The lower bound on $s$ is part of the scope of the argument below;
the joint-design conditions will put every relevant deadline in this range.

In normalized time, let
$\bar S_t$ solve
\[
\bar S_t'=1-\ell\bar S_t^2,\qquad \bar S_0=s,
\]
and put $q=1+s$, $Q_t=\bar S_t+1-t$, and $A_t=q-Q_t$.
At full disclosure, $Q_t^\infty=1-t$ and $A_t^\infty=s+t$.
For each fixed finite $s,\ell$, the analytic constructions below and the
linear organizer equation give, for sufficiently small $|w|$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq-uniform-output-series}
\widehat{\mathcal B}(w,s,\ell)
=b_0(s)+wb_1(s)+\sum_{n\geq2}w^nb_n(s,\ell),
\qquad
b_0(s)=\sqrt{\frac q{2\pi}},\qquad
b_1(s)=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi q}}.
\end{equation}
We control bounded precisions by their second-order gap and the
unbounded precision tail by the exact nonlinear terminal layer. We do not
assume a common relative bound for all higher-order coefficient differences.
Let $P_{t,r}^{\ell}$ denote the Gaussian semigroup
with variance $Q_t-Q_r$, and write
$U_{\ell}^k=\sum_{n\geq0}w^nU_{\ell,n}^k$ for the two normalized player
values. Their coefficients are defined recursively by
\begin{align*}
U_{\ell,0}^i(x,t)&=\Phi(x/\sqrt{Q_t}),
&U_{\ell,0}^j(x,t)&=\Phi(-x/\sqrt{Q_t}),\\
U_{\ell,n}^i(t)
&=\int_t^1P_{t,r}^{\ell}
\left\{\frac12\sum_{a+b=n-1}U_{\ell,a,x}^iU_{\ell,b,x}^i
+\sum_{a+b=n-1}U_{\ell,a,x}^iU_{\ell,b,x}^j\right\}dr,
\end{align*}
for $n\geq1$, with the analogous expression for player $j$.
To link these coefficients to output, let
$R_{\ell,n}=U_{\ell,n}^i+U_{\ell,n}^j$,
$\Delta_{\ell,n}=U_{\ell,n}^i-U_{\ell,n}^j$, and
$h_t^{\ell}(x)=\Phi(x/\sqrt{Q_t})-1/2$. If
$Z_{\ell}=\sum_{n\geq1}w^nZ_{\ell,n}$ is the effort-induced part of expected
best output, equation~\eqref{eq-best-output-drift-identity} and Feynman--Kac
give
\begin{align*}
Z_{\ell,1}(t)
&=\int_t^1P_{t,r}^{\ell}
\left\{\frac12\Delta_{\ell,0,x}
+h_r^{\ell}R_{\ell,0,x}\right\}dr,\\
Z_{\ell,n}(t)
&=\int_t^1P_{t,r}^{\ell}\left\{
\frac12\Delta_{\ell,n-1,x}
+h_r^{\ell}R_{\ell,n-1,x}
+\sum_{a+b=n-1}R_{\ell,a,x}Z_{\ell,b,x}
\right\}dr,
\qquad n\geq2,
\end{align*}
where the last sum has $a\geq0$ and $b\geq1$. Thus
$b_n(s,\ell)=Z_{\ell,n}(0,0)$ for $n\geq1$.

For the high-precision range $\ell s^2\geq1$, convergence and the individual
coefficient bound can be verified directly in a Gaussian function space.
The Riccati equation gives $\ell\bar S_t^2\geq1$, so
$Q_t-Q_r\geq r-t$ and $Q_r\geq1-r$. For player gradients and organizer
gradients, respectively, use
\[
\|f\|_G:=\sup_{x,t<1}\sqrt{Q_t}e^{x^2/(4Q_t)}|f(x,t)|,
\qquad
\|g\|_H:=\sup_{x,t<1}\sqrt{Q_t}e^{x^2/(8Q_t)}|g(x,t)|.
\]
Define $\mathscr A f=\int_t^1\partial_xP_{t,r}^{\ell}f_r\,dr$ and
$\mathscr M(f,g)=\int_t^1\partial_xP_{t,r}^{\ell}(f_rg_r)\,dr$.
To check the operator bounds, put $v=Q_t-Q_r$. If
$|F_r(x)|\leq M Q_r^{-1}e^{-x^2/(2kQ_r)}$, conditional Gaussian
integration, with $W=v+kQ_r$, gives
\[
|\partial_xP_vF_r(x)|\leq
\frac{\sqrt k M}{\sqrt{Q_rW}}e^{-x^2/(2W)}
\left\{\frac{|x|}{W}+\sqrt{\frac{2kQ_r}{\pi vW}}\right\}.
\]
For a product of two $G$-bounded functions take $k=1$; for a product of a
$G$-bounded and an $H$-bounded function take $k=4/3$. For the linear
operator take $k=2$ and $M=\sqrt{Q_r}\|f\|_G$.
Multiplying by the target Gaussian weight and using
$\int_t^1[(1-r)(1-t)]^{-1/2}dr=2$ and
$\int_t^1[(r-t)(1-t)]^{-1/2}dr=2$ yields
\[
\|\mathscr M(f_1,f_2)\|_G\leq C_G\|f_1\|_G\|f_2\|_G,
\quad
\|\mathscr A f\|_H\leq C_G\|f\|_G,
\quad
\|\mathscr M(f,g)\|_H\leq C_H\|f\|_G\|g\|_H,
\]
where
\[
C_G=2\{\sqrt{2/e}+\sqrt{2/\pi}\}<4,
\qquad
C_H=2\{\sqrt{8/(3e)}+(4/3)\sqrt{2/\pi}\}<5.
\]
\emph{Nonperturbative control of the player gradients.}
For finite $\ell$ with $\ell s^2\geq1$, the exact equilibrium satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq-exact-gradient-pairing}
\frac{|p_i-p_j|}{p_i+p_j}\leq\tanh(w/2),\qquad
\|p_i+p_j\|_G<\frac43,\qquad
\|p_i-p_j\|_G\leq\frac{2w}{3},
\quad 0<w\leq\frac12,
\end{equation}
where $p_i=U_{i,x}$ and $p_j=-U_{j,x}$. These are bounds on the real
equilibrium, not on its Taylor coefficients or on differences between
information regimes.

To prove them, use $Q$ as the increasing backward-time variable, starting
at $Q_*=\bar S_1>0$. Put $d=U_i-U_j$, $H=p_i+p_j=d_x$,
$A=p_i-p_j$, and $k=w/(\ell\bar S^2)$. The Riccati equation gives
\[
0<k\leq w,\qquad
k_Q=\frac{2w}{\bar S}\frac1{\ell\bar S^2}
\left(\frac1{\ell\bar S^2}-1\right)\leq0.
\]
The gradient equations are
\[
(p_i)_Q=\tfrac12(p_i)_{xx}+k[A(p_i)_x-p_i(p_j)_x],\qquad
(p_j)_Q=\tfrac12(p_j)_{xx}+k[A(p_j)_x+p_j(p_i)_x].
\]
Their gradients are positive by Theorem~\ref{thm-equilibrium-effort},
and their masses are one by the boundary values. Reflection symmetry
therefore gives $0<d<1$ on $x>0$ and $p_i(0,Q)=p_j(0,Q)$.
Write $k_*=k(Q_*)$. For a differentiable function $b(Q)$, put
$m=\exp(kd+b)$ and $F=p_i-mp_j$. Substitution, retaining
$m_x=mkH$, gives the scalar equation
\[
F_Q=\tfrac12F_{xx}+(kA-mkp_j)F_x
+[k(m-1)(p_j)_x+mp_jk^2H]F
-mp_j(k_Qd+b_Q).
\]
For $b=k_*-k$, the last source is nonpositive on $x>0$;
$F\leq0$ initially and at $x=0$. For $b=-k_*$ the source is
nonnegative and these boundary inequalities reverse. The coefficients
are bounded for each finite precision, and $F\to0$ at spatial infinity,
so scalar comparison yields
\[
kd-k_*\leq\log(p_i/p_j)\leq kd+k_*-k
\qquad(x>0).
\]
Both bounds lie in $[-k_*,k_*]$. Reflection extends this interval to
all states, giving $|A|/H\leq\tanh(k_*/2)\leq w/2$.
This argument does not assume that the gradients are unimodal.

The sum of the two mild equations is
\[
H=2G_Q+\frac w2\mathscr M(A,H),\qquad
G_Q(x)=\frac{e^{-x^2/(2Q)}}{\sqrt{2\pi Q}}.
\]
On any backward-time subinterval, its Gaussian norm $M$ consequently
satisfies
\[
M\leq\frac2{\sqrt{2\pi}}+\frac{C_Gw^2}{4}M^2
<\frac45+w^2M^2.
\]
Initially $M=2/\sqrt{2\pi}<4/5$. A first crossing of $4/3$ would give
$M<4/5+(4/3)^2/4=56/45<4/3$, a contradiction.
The local Gaussian mild construction starts from $G_{Q_*}$; since
$Q_*>0$, a bounded Gaussian norm permits its continuation on the
entire finite clock interval. Bounded-gradient uniqueness identifies
this construction with the equilibrium of
Theorem~\ref{thm-equilibrium-effort}. This proves
equation~\eqref{eq-exact-gradient-pairing} uniformly over finite
high precisions. It does not identify the infinite-precision limit.

The same bounds control the organizer's exact response. The sum equation
gives $\|H-2G_Q\|_G\leq16w^2/9$, and hence
\[
\max_k\|p_k-G_Q\|_G\leq\frac w3+\frac{8w^2}{9}
\leq\frac{7w}{9}.
\]
For the effort-induced output $Z$, write $g=Z_x$ and
$F=\Phi(x/\sqrt{Q_t})p_i+[1-\Phi(x/\sqrt{Q_t})]p_j$.
Then $0<F\leq H$, and the exact linear equation is
$g=w\mathscr A F+w\mathscr M(A,g)$.
Its second operator has norm at most
$(2C_H/3)w^2<5/6$ for $w\leq1/2$. Thus its convergent Neumann
series, identified with the organizer equation by uniqueness at each
finite precision, yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq-exact-organizer-gradient-bound}
\|Z_x\|_H\leq
\frac{(4C_G/3)w}{1-(2C_H/3)w^2}\leq32w.
\end{equation}
Neither estimate uses a small-incentive expansion.

\emph{Analytic bounds for small incentives.}
Let $a_0=1/\sqrt{2\pi}<2/5$.
Their mild equations have quadratic terms
$w\mathscr M(p_i,p_i)/2-w\mathscr M(p_i,p_j)$ and
$w\mathscr M(p_i,p_j)-w\mathscr M(p_j,p_j)/2$, respectively.
On the vector $G$-norm ball of radius $2a_0$, these equations are a
contraction for $|w|\leq1/100$, with Lipschitz constant less than $12/125$.
The quadratic coefficient recursion and the Catalan bound give
\[
\max\{\|p_{i,n}\|_G,\|p_{j,n}\|_G\}\leq a_0(48/5)^n.
\]
The organizer source is
$F=\Phi(x/\sqrt{Q_t})p_i+[1-\Phi(x/\sqrt{Q_t})]p_j$.
Writing $g=Z_x$, its mild equation is
$g=w\mathscr A F+w\mathscr M(p_i-p_j,g)$.
The second term has operator norm less than $2/25$ on the same prize
range. Its coefficient recursion therefore gives
$\|g_n\|_H\leq4a_0(68/5)^{n-1}$ for $n\geq1$.
At the root, Gaussian integration gives bounds
$\sqrt{2/q}\|f\|_G$ and
$4\|f\|_G\|g\|_H/\sqrt{3q}$ for the time integrals of a linear source
and a product source, respectively. Combining these with the preceding
coefficient recursion proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq-high-precision-output-majorant}
|b_n(s,\ell)|\leq\frac{2a_0}{\sqrt q}(68/5)^{n-1},
\qquad n\geq1,\quad\ell s^2\geq1.
\end{equation}
Thus truncation after order $N\geq1$ has output error at most
\[
\frac{2a_0}{\sqrt q}
\frac{(68/5)^N|w|^{N+1}}{1-(68/5)|w|}.
\]
For each finite precision, bounded-gradient HJB uniqueness and the linear
organizer equation identify these convergent series with exact equilibrium
output. For fixed $0<t<r<1$, $Q_t\to1-t$ and the Gaussian kernels converge.
The same operator bounds control omitted time intervals by integrals of
$(r-t)^{-1/2}$ and $(1-r)^{-1/2}$; their Gaussian envelopes control spatial
tails. Dominated convergence in the coefficient recursions, followed by
the summable bound in equation~\eqref{eq-high-precision-output-majorant},
therefore gives the high-precision output limit. At the root, the limiting propagation
variance is $q-(1-r)=s+r$, not $r$: the initial disclosed gap must still
be averaged over its prior distribution. This argument proves the
individual high-precision bounds; the comparison of two precisions requires
a separate difference estimate.

\emph{Second-order comparison on bounded precision sets.}
Since $R_{\ell,0,x}=0$ and $\Delta_{\ell,1}=0$, the order-two organizer
source is $h_t^\ell R_{\ell,1,x}$. Gaussian multiplication gives
\[
R_{\ell,1}(t)=-\int_t^1\frac{G_{Q_t-Q_r/2}}{2\sqrt{\pi Q_r}}\,dr.
\]
To evaluate the root, put $A=A_t$, $B=A_r$, and $V=Q_t-Q_r/2$.
The product $G_AG_V$ is a Gaussian density of variance
$D=AV/(A+V)$ times $[2\pi(A+V)]^{-1/2}$.
Gaussian integration by parts yields
$\mathbb E[Yh_t^\ell(Y)]=D/\sqrt{2\pi(Q_t+D)}$ for $Y\sim\mathcal N(0,D)$.
Consequently,
\[
b_2(s,\ell)=\int_0^1\int_t^1K_q(A_t,A_r)\,dr\,dt,\qquad
K_q(A,B)=\frac{A}{\pi\sqrt{2\pi}\sqrt{q-B}(q+B)
\sqrt{q^2+qB-2A^2}}.
\]
At $A=0$ this expression is understood by continuity.
The revealed fraction $A_t/(s+t)$ is nondecreasing. Indeed, writing
$a_t=s+t$ and $H_t=\ell a_t\bar S_t^2-(a_t-\bar S_t)$ gives
\[
(A_t/a_t)'=H_t/a_t^2,\qquad H_t'=2\ell a_t\bar S_t\bar S_t'.
\]
For $\ell>0$, $H_0>0$; at a first zero one would have
$\bar S_t'=\bar S_t/a_t>0$ and $H_t'>0$, a contradiction.
For $\ell=0$, $A_t=0$. The kernel increases strictly with $A$.
Along a ray $(A,B)=\rho(a,b)$, put $x=\rho b/q$, $z=\rho a/q$.
Direct differentiation gives
\[
2\rho\partial_\rho\log K_q(\rho a,\rho b)
=\frac{2(1+x^3+xz^2-3x^2z^2)}
{(1-x^2)(1+x-2z^2)}>0.
\]
For $x\le1/3$ the numerator is positive; otherwise its minimum over
$0\le z\le x$ is $(1-x)(3x^3+x^2+x+1)>0$.
Taking $a=s+t$, $b=s+r$, $\rho=A_r/b<1$, the revealed-fraction
inequality gives
$K_q(A_t,A_r)\le K_q(\rho a,\rho b)<K_q(a,b)$.
Thus $\Delta_2(s,\ell):=b_2(s,\infty)-b_2(s,\ell)>0$ at every finite precision.

\emph{The exact high-precision terminal layer.}
We first compare full disclosure with an auxiliary problem of constant
posterior variance $\eta>0$, innovation variance one, horizon one, and
initial perceived-gap variance $s-\eta$. This is a proof device, not a
different data-generating process. Take $0<\eta<s/2$.
Write $\mathcal L_0=(\partial_{zz}+z\partial_z)/2$ and $G=G_1$.
The full-disclosure player profiles $R_w,d_w$ are determined by
\[
R_w=f_w(d_w),\qquad K_w(d_w)=K_w(1)[2\Phi(z)-1].
\]
\[
f_w(d)=1+\frac2w\log\frac{\cosh(wd/2)}{\cosh(w/2)}.
\]
\[
K_w(d)=\frac{d+\sinh(wd)/w}{2}.
\]
The identities $f_w''=(w/2)(1-f_w'^2)$ and $K_w''/K_w'=wf_w'$
verify their HJB equations
\[
\mathcal L_0R_w+w\{3R_w'^2/4-d_w'^2/4\}=0,\qquad
\mathcal L_0d_w+(w/2)R_w'd_w'=0.
\]
They have the required boundary values and positive gradients.
Small-$w$ uniqueness in the Gaussian fixed point above identifies them
with its full-disclosure coefficient limit.
The organizer profile $\beta_w$, with asymptotic slopes $\pm1/2$, solves
\[
(\mathcal L_0-1/2)\beta_w+wR_w'\beta_w'+wd_w'/2=0.
\]
At zero incentive $\beta_0=\beta$, where
$\beta(z)=G(z)+z[\Phi(z)-1/2]$.
The player gradient ODEs are
$p_i'=(-z+w(2p_j-p_i))p_i$ and
$p_j'=(-z+w(p_j-2p_i))p_j$.
Their integrable Gaussian bounds imply, by integration, Gaussian
$e^{-z^2/2}$ tails for both gradients and their differentiated equations.
Hence $R_w-1,d_w-(2\Phi-1)$ belong to Gaussian-weighted $H^2$.
Solving for $\beta_w-\beta$ uses the Gaussian source
$-w(R_w'\beta'+d_w'/2)$, so it belongs to the same space and is $O(w)$.
All these quantities are analytic for small complex $w$.

For the auxiliary problem use logarithmic time $v$ and state $z$,
where remaining variance is $\eta e^v$.
Its exact player and organizer perturbation is
$E=(\phi,\psi,\chi)$, initially
$(1-R_w,2\Phi-1-d_w,\beta-\beta_w)$.
Addition and subtraction of the original HJB give
\[
E_v=\mathcal A_wE+\mathcal N_w(E),\qquad
\mathcal N_w(E)=w(3\phi'^2/4-\psi'^2/4,\ \phi'\psi'/2,\ \phi'\chi').
\]
The player block of $\mathcal A_w$ is
\[
\binom{\mathcal L_0\phi+
w(3R_w'\phi'/2-d_w'\psi'/2)}
{\mathcal L_0\psi+w(d_w'\phi'+R_w'\psi')/2};
\]
the organizer block is
$(\mathcal L_0-1/2+wR_w'\partial_z)\chi+
w(\phi'\beta_w'+\psi'/2)$.
The exact change $e=\phi-f_w'(d_w)\psi$,
$x=K_w'(d_w)\psi/K_w(1)$ makes the player block triangular:
\[
e_v=\mathcal L_{E,w}e,\quad
x_v=\mathcal L_0x+(w/2)(2\Phi-1)'e',\qquad
\mathcal L_{E,w}=\mathcal L_0+wR_w'\partial_z.
\]
The first component is even and the second odd.
Conjugating $\mathcal L_{E,w}$ by
$m_w^{1/2}$, $m_w=\exp(z^2/2+2wR_w)$, gives
\[
-\sqrt{m_w}\mathcal L_{E,w}(g/\sqrt{m_w})
=\left[-\frac12\partial_{zz}+\frac{z^2}{8}+\frac14+
\frac{w^2}{4}(d_w'^2-R_w'^2)\right]g.
\]
The added potential is positive and equals $w^2G^2+O(w^4)$.
The unperturbed ground state has squared normalized density $G$.
The simple slow eigenvalue is therefore $\nu(w)=-\kappa(w)$, where
\[
\kappa(w)=\frac12+\frac{w^2}{2\pi\sqrt3}+O(w^4),\qquad
\frac12<\kappa(w)\le\frac12+\frac{w^2K_w(1)^2}{2\pi}<\frac{11}{20}
\]
for $0<w\le1/2$. The variational upper bound follows by testing the
unperturbed ground state and using
$0<d_w'^2-R_w'^2\le2K_w(1)^2/\pi$;
$K_w(1)\le21/20$ on this interval follows from the series for $\sinh(w)/w$.
The remaining even player spectrum is at most $-3/2$, the odd heat
spectrum at most $-1$, and the organizer spectrum at most $-1$.
The positive potential, the harmonic-oscillator eigenbasis, and
bounded first-derivative couplings give these statements without
assuming stability of the nonlinear solution.

Here are the nonlinear estimates needed to use this spectrum.
Work in $\mathcal H=L^2(e^{z^2/2}dz)^3$ with the even--odd--even parity,
and $X=D((-\mathcal A_0)^{7/8})$.
The preceding weighted $H^2$ bounds place $E(0)$ in $X$, with norm
$O(|w|)$, uniformly in a small complex disk.
Conjugation to the harmonic oscillator and one-dimensional Sobolev
embedding give
$\|f'\|_\infty+\|f'\|_{\mathcal H}\le C\|f\|_X$.
Thus $\|\mathcal N_w(E)\|_{\mathcal H}\le C|w|\|E\|_X^2$, with the
corresponding local Lipschitz bound.
The common second-order domain is unchanged by the bounded-coefficient
first-order perturbation. In the Hermite basis, the derivative-resolvent
bound is controlled by $\sup_{n\ge0}\sqrt{n+1}/|\zeta+(n+1)/2|$.
Outside a disk of radius $1/8$ about $-1/2$, this bound and a Neumann
series exclude spectrum in $\operatorname{Re}\zeta\ge-3/4$ for small
complex $w$. The enclosed eigenvalue remains simple, with
$9/20<\operatorname{Re}\kappa<3/5$ after reducing the disk.
Hermite resolvent estimates and spectral projection therefore give,
uniformly in a small complex $w$ disk,
\[
\|e^{v\mathcal A_w}\|_{\mathcal H\to X}
\le C(1+v^{-7/8})e^{-9v/20},\qquad
\|e^{v\mathcal A_w}(I-\Pi_w)\|_{\mathcal H\to X}
\le C(1+v^{-7/8})e^{-7v/10}.
\]
The same estimates without the singular factor hold from $X$ to $X$.
They follow by contour integration of the sectorial resolvent and
interpolation on the fixed domain; the smoothing and spectral
decomposition framework is given in \citet[Sections 3.2, 5.2, and 7.1]{Lorenzi2005}.
The displayed domain, derivative order, and spectral separation verify
its hypotheses here. The kernel exponent $7/8<1$ makes the Duhamel map
a contraction on a ball of radius $C|w|$ in
$\sup_{v\ge0}e^{2v/5}\|E(v)\|_X$.
Consequently $\|E(v)\|_X\le C|w|e^{-2v/5}$, analytically in $w$.

Normalize the slow right vector by $\int\phi_w=1$ and its left
functional by $\ell_w(e_w)=1$.
The exact nonlinear amplitude is
\[
A(w)=\ell_wE(0)+\int_0^\infty e^{\kappa(w)r}
\ell_w\mathcal N_w(E(r))\,dr.
\]
The integral is $O(w^3)$ and locally uniformly convergent since
$2(2/5)>\operatorname{Re}\kappa$.
The initial projection is $w/\sqrt\pi+O(w^2)$:
$R_w=1-w\Phi(1-\Phi)+O(w^2)$ and
$\int\Phi(1-\Phi)=1/\sqrt\pi$.
Hence $A(w)>0$ for small positive $w$.
Projection of the Duhamel equation gives
\[
\|E(v)-A(w)e^{-\kappa(w)v}e_w\|_X\le C|w|e^{-7v/10}.
\]
The slow organizer response satisfies
\[
(\nu-\mathcal L_0+1/2-wR_w'\partial_z)\chi_w
=w(\phi_w'\beta_w'+\psi_w'/2),\qquad
\chi_w=-wG\beta+O(w^2).
\]
The first-order identity follows from
$\mathcal L_0(G\beta)=G'(\Phi-1/2)$.

The sign of this response can also be established without a Taylor
remainder: {\color{blue}$\chi_w(z)<0$ for every $z$ when $0<w\le9/20$.}
Here $e=\phi_w-f_w'(d_w)\psi_w$ is the positive scalar ground state,
not the full eigenvector. Its equation gives
$m_we'(z)=-2\kappa\int_0^z m_we<0$ for $z>0$.
The odd component $x=K_w'(d_w)\psi_w/K_w(1)$ satisfies
\[
(-\kappa-\mathcal L_0)x=(w/2)(2\Phi-1)'e'.
\]
The positive half-line resolvent applies since $\kappa<1$.
The comparison function $-(3w/5)(2\Phi-1)e$ gives, for $0<w\le1/2$,
\[
|\psi_w|\le(63/100)w(2\Phi-1)e,\qquad
(737/800)e\le\phi_w\le e\quad(z>0).
\]
Indeed, applying $-\kappa-\mathcal L_0$ to this comparison function
gives $(3w/5)e'\{(2\Phi-1)'-w(2\Phi-1)R_w'\}$, and
$w(2\Phi-1)R_w'/(2\Phi-1)'\le21/160$.
Thus $|\psi_w|\le(7/10)w(2\Phi-1)\phi_w$.
{\color{blue}Since $R_w=f_w(d_w)$, $f_w'(d)\ge0$ for $d\ge0$ and
$f_w''\le w/2$ imply $0\le R_w(z)-R_w(0)\le w/4$.
Put $m_\phi=e^{z^2/2+3w[R_w-R_w(0)]}$,
and $I(z)=\int_0^z m_\phi\phi_w$.}
Integration of the sum-component equation yields
\[
m_\phi\phi_w'=-2\kappa I+wm_\phi d_w'\psi_w
-2w^2\int_0^z m_\phi R_w'd_w'\psi_w.
\]
Since $R_w'd_w'\le(147/400)w$, the positive last term in the first
equation is at most $(1029/2000)w^4I$. Hence $\phi_w'<0$ and
$|\phi_w'|\ge[2\kappa-(1029/2000)w^4]I/m_\phi$.
The negative half-line solution also has $\psi_w'(0)<0$.
{\color{blue}To retain the favorable derivative of the leading odd
response, put $g=2\Phi-1$, $k=K_w(1)$, and
$\epsilon=-x-(w/2)ge$. The product rule gives
\[
(-\kappa-\mathcal L_0)\epsilon=-\tfrac12w^2gR_w'e'>0.
\]
Both boundary values are zero. Since $R_w'\le wkG$,
$wgR_w'/g'\le21/160$, and
$(31/100)(1-21/160)>21/80\ge k/4$, comparison with
$(31/100)w^3ge$ in the positive half-line resolvent gives
\[
0<\epsilon\le(31/100)w^3ge\qquad(0<w\le1/2).
\]
To bound its derivative, use its equation rather than differentiate
this inequality. Put $m_0=e^{z^2/2}$ and
$m=e^{z^2/2+2w[R_w-R_w(0)]}$. Then
\[
(m_0\epsilon')'=-m_0(2\kappa\epsilon+w^2gR_w'|e'|),
\qquad m|e'|=2\kappa\int_0^z me.
\]
The positive half-line solution has $\epsilon'(0)>0$. Discarding
this initial flux and integrating gives
\[
-\epsilon'(z)\le m_0(z)^{-1}
\left[2\kappa\int_0^z m_0\epsilon
+w^2\int_0^z m_0gR_w'|e'|\right]
\le\frac{167}{200}e^{w^2/2}w^3g|e'|.
\]
For the last bound, the first integral contributes at most
$(31/100)w^3g e^{2w[R_w-R_w(0)]}|e'|$.
For the second, use $R_w'\le wkG$ and
$e(0)-e(z)\le m(z)|e'(z)|\sqrt{\pi/2}\,g(z)$, obtained by
integrating the ground-state flux; its contribution is at most
$(k/2)w^3g^2e^{2w[R_w-R_w(0)]}|e'|$.

The reconstruction multiplier also varies with state:
\[
\psi_w'=\frac{k}{K_w'(d_w)}(x'-wR_w'x),\qquad
x'=-(w/2)(g'e+ge')-\epsilon'.
\]
From $d_w\le kg$ we have $R_w'\le wk^2gG$. The ground-state flux
gives $|e'|\ge ze\,e^{-z^2/2-w^2/2}$, hence
$gGe/|e'|\le e^{w^2/2}/\pi<8/21$.
Using $e^{w^2/2}\le8/7$ and
$(167/200)(8/7)+(441/400)(231/400)(8/21)<6/5$, we obtain
\[
\psi_w'\le C(w)wg|e'|,\qquad
C(w)=\frac{21}{20}(1/2+6w^2/5)\le\frac{21}{25}.
\]
Finally, $\phi_w=e+f_w'(d_w)\psi_w$, $f_w''\ge0$,
$\psi_w<0$, and $f_w'\le w/2$ imply
$|\phi_w'|\ge[1-C(w)w^2/2]|e'|$.
Consequently
\[
\psi_w'\le\frac{168}{179}wg|\phi_w'|
\le wg|\phi_w'|\qquad(z>0,\ 0<w\le1/2).
\]}

To bound the output derivative, write $H=\beta_w-\beta$.
It solves
$(1/2-\mathcal L_{E,w})H=(w/2)d_w'[1+f_w'(d_w)(2\Phi-1)]$.
The source $F=d_w'[1+f_w'(d_w)(2\Phi-1)]/2$ is positive and even.
Since $d_w''=-(z+wR_w')d_w'$,
$f_w'(d_w)\le wd_w/2$, and
$\partial_zf_w'(d_w)\le wd_w'/2$, its derivative satisfies
\[
F'(z)\le\tfrac12d_w'z[-1+2wK_w(1)/\pi]
\le-(13/40)zd_w'<0\quad(z>0,\ w\le1/2).
\]
The odd-drift diffusion semigroup preserves even decreasing functions,
as follows from its scalar derivative equation; its bounded source
and discount therefore give $H\ge0,H'\le0$.
{\color{blue}A sharper bound retains the effort contribution $H>wG$.
To verify it, put $d=d_w$
and $a=f_w'(d)=\tanh(wd/2)$. The equation for $H-wG$ is
\[
(1/2-\mathcal L_{E,w})(H-wG)
=w(F-G)+w^2R_w'G'.
\]
For $z\ge0$, its right side divided by $wG$ equals
\[
\frac{k-K_w'(d)+ak[g-2wzG]}{K_w'(d)}
\ge\frac{k-1-\sinh^2(wd/2)+(1-w)d\tanh(wd/2)}{K_w'(d)}>0.
\]
Indeed $g\ge2zG$, $kg=K_w(d)\ge d$, and
$\sinh(wd)\le d\sinh w\le2(1-w)d$ for $0\le d\le1$,
$w\le1/2$. The same bound holds by symmetry on the negative half-line.
The bounded-source, positive-discount resolvent therefore gives $H>wG$.
Since $H'\le0$, the output ODE now yields
\[
\beta_w''+2wR_w'\beta_w'
=G+H-zH'-wd_w'\ge[1-w(2K_w(1)-1)]G.
\]
Integration from zero, using $R_w(z)-R_w(0)\le w/4$, gives
\[
\beta_w'(z)\ge\tfrac12(1-11w/10)(1-w^2/2)(2\Phi(z)-1)
\qquad(0<w\le1/2).
\]
Consequently the organizer source is pointwise negative:
\[
\phi_w'\beta_w'+\psi_w'/2
\le-\frac1{600}(2\Phi-1)|\phi_w'|<0
\quad(z>0,\ 0<w\le9/20).
\]}
It is even, and at zero equals $\psi_w'(0)/2<0$.
Finally, on $L^2(m_wdz)$ the operator
$1/2-\kappa-\mathcal L_{E,w}$ has lowest eigenvalue $1/2$.
Its convergent positive-semigroup resolvent gives
\[
\chi_w=w\int_0^\infty e^{(\kappa-1/2)t}
e^{t\mathcal L_{E,w}}(\phi_w'\beta_w'+\psi_w'/2)\,dt<0.
\]
This argument does not assume that the evolved prior remains unimodal.
It quantifies the linear response, not the nonlinear remainder or the
full-precision ranking threshold $w_*$.

For $Y\sim\mathcal N(0,s)$,
\[
\mathbb E[G(Y)\beta(Y)]=\frac{\sqrt{1+2s}}{2\pi(1+s)}.
\]
Weighted $H^1$ controls both $L^1$ and $L^\infty$; splitting $s\le1$
and $s\ge1$ gives
$\sqrt{1+s}|\mathbb Ef(Y)|\le C\|f\|_{H^1}$.
Thus the negative response is uniform after multiplication by
$\sqrt{1+s}$.

At $w=0$ the organizer fast component has derivative $-e^{-v}G$.
Cauchy's estimate in the fixed complex disk consequently sharpens its
remainder to
\[
\|\chi(v)-A(w)e^{-\kappa(w)v}\chi_w+we^{-v}G\|_X
\le Cw^2e^{-7v/10}.
\]
For horizon one, put $h=1+\eta$, $v=\log(h/\eta)$, and average the
auxiliary profile at $Y\sim\mathcal N(0,(s-\eta)/h)$.
Differentiating $\sqrt h\,\mathbb E\beta_w(Y)$ in $\eta$ using its profile
equation gives the background increment
$w\eta G_q(0)+O(w^2\eta/\sqrt q)$.
The displayed fast term contributes exactly $-w\eta G_q(0)$.
The positive amplitude and negative slow response therefore imply
\[
\widehat{\mathcal B}(w,s,\infty)
-\widehat{\mathcal B}_{a}(w,s,\eta)
\ge\frac{C_-w^2\eta^{\kappa(w)}
-C_+w^2(\eta^{7/10}+\eta)}{\sqrt q}.
\]
Here $C_-,C_+>0$ are numerical constants; subsequent $C$ may vary.
Since $\kappa(w)<11/20$, this difference is strictly positive for a
common small $\eta$, provided $\eta<s/2$.

\emph{Returning to the original variance path.}
This last comparison concerns an auxiliary path, so we must bound its
difference from the Riccati path. Assume $\eta=\ell^{-1/2}<\min(s/2,1/8)$.
On the variance clock $Q$, both problems have the same heat propagator.
Their incentive weights are
\[
\gamma_o(Q)=\begin{cases}
\eta^2/\bar S_{t(Q)}^2,&\bar S_1\le Q\le q,\\
0,&\eta\le Q<\bar S_1,
\end{cases}
\qquad
\gamma_a(Q)=\mathbf1_{[\eta,1+\eta]}(Q).
\]
Both weights lie in $[0,1]$ and integrate to one.
Extending below $\bar S_1$ with zero weight is pure heat propagation,
which gives the original Gaussian terminal data.
The player and organizer equations on this clock are
\[
\partial_QU_k=\tfrac12U_{k,yy}+w\gamma(Q)N_k(U_y),\qquad
\partial_QZ=\tfrac12Z_{yy}
+w\gamma(Q)\{F+(p_i-p_j)Z_y\},
\]
with $F=\Phi(y/\sqrt Q)p_i+[1-\Phi(y/\sqrt Q)]p_j$.
Both outputs are evaluated at the same root $(q,0)$.

If $t_c$ solves $Q_{t_c}=1+\eta$, the explicit Riccati formula gives
$0<t_c<\eta$, and equal weight masses give
$\|\gamma_o-\gamma_a\|_{L^1}=2t_c<2\eta$.
For $Q\le1/2$ one has $t\ge1/2$ and
$\bar S_t-\eta\le4\eta e^{-1/\eta}$.
Thus the low-clock difference is exponentially small, including the
interval $[\eta,\bar S_1]$.
Put $\delta\gamma=\gamma_o-\gamma_a$, and restrict to real
$0<w\le1/100$. The Gaussian bounds above give
$\|p_{b,k}-G_Q\|_G\le5w$ and $\|Z_{b,y}\|_H\le2w$
on either active path. Pure heat continuation of the auxiliary path
beyond $1+\eta$ widens these envelopes by factors at most $\sqrt2$
and 2. On the whole common clock we may therefore use
\[
|p_{b,k}-G_Q|\le8wQ^{-1/2}e^{-y^2/(4Q)},\qquad
|a_b|\le16wQ^{-1/2}e^{-y^2/(4Q)},\qquad
|g_a|\le4wQ^{-1/2}e^{-y^2/(8Q)},
\]
where $a_b=p_{b,i}-p_{b,j}$ and $g_b=Z_{b,y}$ for $b=o,a$.
These are gradient bounds, not assumptions on the prior.

The derivative kernel can be checked without differentiating its input.
If $|f(y)|\le M u^{-r}e^{-y^2/(2ku)}$, conditional Gaussian integration
and maximization of the remaining spatial weight give, for $Q>u$ and $K>k\ge1$,
\[
\sqrt Q\sup_y e^{y^2/(2KQ)}|\partial_yP_{Q-u}f(y)|
\le M\left\{
\frac{k\sqrt{K/[e(K-k)]}\,u^{1/2-r}}{\sqrt Q}
+\frac{k\sqrt{2/\pi}\,u^{1-r}}{\sqrt{Q(Q-u)}}\right\}.
\]
Indeed the convolution envelope has variance $Q+(k-1)u$;
its derivative is bounded by its conditional absolute first moment.
No derivative of $\gamma_b$ is used.

First consider $\eta\le Q\le1/2$, and write $E_\eta=e^{-1/\eta}$.
Apart from the initial interval of length
$\varepsilon=\bar S_1-\eta\le4\eta E_\eta^2$,
$|\delta\gamma|\le8E_\eta$.
Let $D_p,D_g$ be the maximum gradient differences in the original
$G,H$ norms restricted to this interval.
For the player quadratic source, the derivative kernel has total
weight at most 4 and mismatch weight at most $40E_\eta$.
The latter includes the initial interval through
$\varepsilon/\eta+2\sqrt{\varepsilon/\eta}\le8E_\eta$.
Since each player gradient has $G$ norm less than $4/5$, subtraction
of the two mild equations gives
\[
D_p\le w\{(48/5)D_p+(192/5)E_\eta\}\le50wE_\eta.
\]
For the organizer linear source the corresponding weights are at most
6 and $64E_\eta$; for its product source they are at most 6 and
$60E_\eta$. Using $\|g_a\|_H\le2w$ on this interval gives
\[
D_g\le w\{(6+24w)D_p+(48/5)D_g
+(256/5+192w)E_\eta\}\le70wE_\eta.
\]
Both absorptions hold for $w\le1/100$.

For $Q\ge1/2$ define only the difference envelopes
\[
A_p(Q)=\max_k\sup_y\sqrt Qe^{y^2/(8Q)}|p_{o,k}-p_{a,k}|,
\qquad
A_g(Q)=\sup_y\sqrt Qe^{y^2/(16Q)}|g_o-g_a|,
\]
and put $I_p=\int_{1/2}^q\gamma_o A_p$,
$I_g=\int_{1/2}^q\gamma_o A_g$.
The wider envelopes leave room for the spatial factor in the derivative
kernel. In particular $A_p(1/2)\le50wE_\eta$ and
$A_g(1/2)\le70wE_\eta$.
Writing $k(Q,u)=1+(Q-u)^{-1/2}$, the same kernel gives
\[
A_p(Q)\le2A_p(1/2)+24w\int_{1/2}^Q k(Q,u)
\{\gamma_o(u)A_p(u)+|\delta\gamma(u)|\}\,du,
\]
\[
A_g(Q)\le3A_g(1/2)+w\int_{1/2}^Q k(Q,u)
\left[\gamma_o(u)\{(6+32w)A_p(u)+64wA_g(u)\}
+(6+256w^2)|\delta\gamma(u)|\right]du.
\]
Any weight in $[0,1]$ of total mass at most one satisfies
$\int_u^q\gamma(Q)k(Q,u)dQ\le3$.
Multiplying by $\gamma_o(Q)$ and applying Tonelli, then using
$\|\delta\gamma\|_1<2\eta$, therefore yields
\[
I_p\le2A_p(1/2)+72wI_p+144w\eta
\le8A_p(1/2)+600w\eta\le1000w\eta,
\]
\[
I_g\le3A_g(1/2)+21wI_p+192w^2I_g+42w\eta
\le4A_g(1/2)+22wI_p+44w\eta\le600w\eta.
\]
These estimates apply first on finite clock intervals and extend by
the same strict absorptions; no small difference is assumed in advance.

Finally, $P_{q-u}G_u(0)=G_q(0)$ and the equal weight masses imply
$\int_\eta^q\delta\gamma(u)P_{q-u}G_u(0)du=0$ exactly.
Subtracting the full organizer equations consequently gives
\[
Z_o(q,0)-Z_a(q,0)=w\int_\eta^qP_{q-u}
\left[\gamma_o\{\delta F+a_o\delta g+\delta a\,g_a\}
+\delta\gamma\{F_a-G_u+a_ag_a\}\right](0)du.
\]
Here $\delta F=\Phi\delta p_i+(1-\Phi)\delta p_j$;
$\delta a$ and $\delta g$ are the corresponding path differences.
The root convolution of $u^{-1/2}e^{-y^2/(2ku)}$ equals
$\sqrt k/\sqrt{q+(k-1)u}$.
The low-clock bounds thus give a root contribution at most
$w^2(127E_\eta+24\eta)/\sqrt q\le200w^2\eta/\sqrt q$.
For the remaining integral the global envelopes give
\[
\sqrt q\,|\delta Z_{\rm high}|
\le w\{(2+16w)I_p+32wI_g\}
+16w^2\|\delta\gamma\|_{L^1([1/2,q])}.
\]
Combining these estimates proves directly
\begin{equation}\label{eq-real-variance-path-error}
|Z_o(q,0)-Z_a(q,0)|\le3000w^2\eta/\sqrt q.
\end{equation}
The bound is uniform for $s\ge1/4$ and
$\eta<\min(s/2,1/8)$ and does not require complex-parameter stability.
Since $\kappa(w)<11/20$, this error is smaller than the slow
contribution above after choosing a common sufficiently small $\eta$.
We have therefore proved exact strict sorting on the original path for
all sufficiently large $\ell$, uniformly when $\eta<s/2$.

\emph{Uniform bounded-precision comparison, including arbitrarily short deadlines.}
Choose a numerical $L>\max(64,\eta_*^{-2})$, where $\eta_*$ is the
tail threshold just proved. For $s\ge1/4$, $0\le\ell\le L$,
the terminal variance is bounded below by
$m=\bar S_1(1/4,L)>0$.
The Fourier norm with moving radius $\rho(\tau)=1-\tau/2$ and total
initial bound $M_0=4e^{1/(2m)}/\sqrt{2\pi m}$ satisfies
$M'\le(\rho'+2|w|M)N$.
For $|w|<1/(32M_0)$, the bootstrap $M\le4M_0$ makes the derivative
coefficient at most $-1/4$.
A further radius gap controls the flux-Jacobian derivative in the cutoff
difference equation; its discarded tails tend to zero.
Gronwall consequently gives a unique symmetric analytic solution and
analytic parameter dependence, including zero diffusion.
These bounds are independent of the upper value of $s$:
a large diffusion coefficient only adds Fourier dissipation.
The positivity and HJB verification at zero are the same as the
no-signal construction given below.

To retain the scale $q^{-1/2}$ we also need spatial localization.
In a smaller analytic strip the original gradient equations have drift
$w(p_i-p_j)$ and reactions $-wp_{j,y}$, $wp_{i,y}$, all bounded by
$C|w|$. Their complex Feynman--Kac paths differ from the Gaussian paths
by at most $C|w|$ and stay in the strip.
For a terminal Gaussian of variance $v\ge m$,
$(x+d)^2\ge x^2/2-d^2$ bounds the path integrand by $CG_{2v}$.
Convolution therefore gives $|p_k(x+iy,t)|\le CG_{2Q_t}(x)$.
The organizer source has the same envelope.
At the root its expectation is bounded by
$CG_{q+3Q_t}(0)\le C/\sqrt q$; thus $|Z(w,s,\ell)|\le C|w|/\sqrt q$.
Cauchy's estimate and the individual full-disclosure bound give
\[
\widehat{\mathcal B}(w,s,\infty)-\widehat{\mathcal B}(w,s,\ell)
=w^2\Delta_2(s,\ell)+O(|w|^3/\sqrt q)
\]
uniformly on $s\ge1/4$, $\ell\le L$.

An explicit lower bound avoids a separate argument at $s=\infty$.
Take $L\ge16$ and put $m_L=L^{-1/2}\le1/4$.
Riccati comparison gives $\bar S_t\ge m_L$ for $s\ge1/4$ and
$\ell\le L$. Restrict the kernel integral to
$t\in[1/4,3/8]$, $r\in[1/2,5/8]$, and write
$u=1-t$, $v=1-r$, $a=q-u$, $b=q-v$.
The finite-precision revealed fraction is at most
$1-m_L/(q-v)\le1-m_L/q$.
On the final ray segment $1-m_L/(2q)\le\rho\le1$, put
$U=q-\rho a$, $V=q-\rho b$. Then $U\le7/8$, $V\le5/8$, and
\[
\sqrt q\,K_q(\rho a,\rho b)
=\frac{1-U/q}{\pi\sqrt{2\pi}\sqrt V(2-V/q)
\sqrt{4U-V-2U^2/q}}>\frac1{80}.
\]
Here $q\ge5/4$, $1-U/q\ge3/10$, and
$\pi\sqrt{2\pi}<8$. In the ray derivative above, its numerator
is at least $1-x$, while
$1+x-2z^2\le4U/q\le7/(2q)$. Hence
$\partial_\rho\log K_q\ge q/7$ on this segment.
Integrating along the segment and then over the rectangle of area
$1/64$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq-bounded-precision-gap-explicit}
\sqrt q\,\Delta_2(s,\ell)
>\frac{1}{72000\sqrt L},
\qquad s\ge1/4,\quad0\le\ell\le L.
\end{equation}
The omitted kernel differences are nonnegative by the preceding ordering.
Choose $w_*$ smaller than this bound divided by twice the uniform Taylor-remainder
constant, and smaller than the high-precision threshold.
This proves equation~\eqref{eq-uniform-disclosure-ranking} on exactly its
stated range, without a uniform relative high-order coefficient bound.

\emph{The no-prize and full-disclosure regions.}
Let
\[
b(T):=\sqrt{\frac{S_0+\sigma^2T}{2\pi}},
\qquad
F_0(T):=u(b(T))-D(T).
\]
The function $b$ is strictly concave and increasing, so $F_0$ is strictly
concave. The utility tangent bounds its gross return by
$u(b(0))+u'(b(0))\sigma\sqrt{T/(2\pi)}$, whereas convex delay grows at
least linearly. Thus $F_0(T)\to-\infty$ and has the unique maximizer
$T_0$ used in the proposition. Its derivative at zero is
\[
F_0'(0)=\frac{\sigma^2u'(b(0))}{2\sqrt{2\pi S_0}}-D'(0).
\]
Under $D'(0)=0$ and $\sigma>0$, this derivative is strictly positive,
so $T_0>0$ without an additional condition in part (i).
If $T_0>0$, its first-order condition is
\begin{equation}\label{eq-no-prize-deadline-foc}
\frac{u'(b(T_0))\sigma^2}
{2\sqrt{2\pi(S_0+\sigma^2T_0)}}=D'(T_0).
\end{equation}

To order the risk regions, keep $(u,D,S_0)$ fixed and write
$q=\sigma^2T_0(\sigma)$ and
$A(q)=u'(\sqrt{(S_0+q)/(2\pi)})/[2\sqrt{2\pi(S_0+q)}]$
only in this argument. The first-order condition is
\[
\sigma^2A(q)=D'(q/\sigma^2).
\]
The function $A$ is positive, continuous, and strictly decreasing,
while $D'$ is continuous and nondecreasing. Increasing $\sigma$
at a fixed $q$ strictly increases the left side and weakly decreases
the right side. Since their difference strictly decreases with $q$,
the unique root $q(\sigma)$ strictly increases with $\sigma$.
Continuity follows from the continuous, strictly monotone root equation.
If $q\geq\varepsilon>0$ along a sequence $\sigma\downarrow0$, then
\[
D'(\varepsilon/\sigma^2)\leq\sigma^2A(\varepsilon)\longrightarrow0,
\]
contradicting $D'(1)>0$. Hence $q(\sigma)\to0$.
If $q\leq M<\infty$ along a sequence $\sigma\to\infty$, then
\[
\sigma^2A(M)\leq D'(M/\sigma^2)\longrightarrow D'(0)=0,
\]
which is impossible. Thus $q(\sigma)\to\infty$.
This proves existence, uniqueness, and the ordering
$0<\sigma_L<\sigma_U$ of the two risk boundaries.
They depend only on $(u,D,S_0)$, not on $c$ or an optimal design.

{\color{blue}The prize-entry benchmark also has a definite direction on this risk
range. Put $b_q=\sqrt{(S_0+q)/(2\pi)}$. Direct differentiation gives
\[
\frac{qA'(q)}{A(q)}
=-\frac{q}{2(S_0+q)}
\left(1-\frac{b_qu''(b_q)}{u'(b_q)}\right)
\leq-\frac{(1+\gamma_0)q}{2(S_0+q)}<-1
\quad(q\geq S_0/3).
\]
Thus $qA(q)$ strictly decreases. The no-prize first-order condition
also reads $qA(q)=T_0D'(T_0)$. Since $TD'(T)$ strictly increases,
$T_0$ strictly decreases with $q$ on this range. Consequently
\[
c_e(\sigma)=\frac{2T_0^2D'(T_0)}q
\]
strictly decreases with $q$, and hence with $\sigma\geq\sigma_L$.
This argument requires no second derivative of $D$.}

For completeness, if the assumption $D'(0)=0$ is relaxed, a primitive
high-cost condition also identifies when no contest should be launched.
Put $b_0=b(0)$. If
\begin{equation}\label{eq-no-contest-cost-bound}
D'(0)>\frac{\sigma^2u'(b_0)}{2\sqrt{2\pi S_0}},\qquad
c\ge\frac{[u'(b_0)]^2}
{2D'(0)-\sigma^2u'(b_0)/\sqrt{2\pi S_0}},
\end{equation}
every joint optimum has $\theta=T=0$.
Indeed, for $T>0$, strict concavity of the square root and
equation~\eqref{eq-design-basic-output-bound} give
\[
\mathcal B-b_0<\frac{\sigma^2T}{2\sqrt{2\pi S_0}}
+\sqrt{2\theta T/c}.
\]
The concave utility tangent, $D(T)\ge D'(0)T$, and
$u'(b_0)\sqrt{2\theta T/c}\le\theta+[u'(b_0)]^2T/(2c)$ imply
\[
J(\theta,T,\lambda)-u(b_0)
<T\left\{\frac{\sigma^2u'(b_0)}{2\sqrt{2\pi S_0}}
-D'(0)+\frac{[u'(b_0)]^2}{2c}\right\}\le0.
\]
The first strict inequality holds also at the cost-bound equality.
At $T=0$, the payoff is $u(b_0)-\theta$, so only zero prize is optimal.
The zero-prize, zero-deadline design itself attains the global maximum,
at every precision.
The energy upper bound passes to the high-precision limit; this excludes
all positive-deadline designs without assuming weak incentives.

The marginal return to introducing a prize can be obtained independently
of the uniform output expansion. Fix any finite precision and let
$Q_t=S_t+\sigma^2(T-t)$, $Q_0=S_0+\sigma^2T$. At zero prize, the limiting
normalized value gradients are
$U_{i,y}=-U_{j,y}=G_{Q_t}$, where $G_v$ is the centered Gaussian density
with variance $v$. The zero-effort perceived gap has variance $Q_0-Q_t$,
so Gaussian convolution gives
$\mathbb E[G_{Q_t}(\tilde y_t)]=G_{Q_0}(0)$.
The first variation of equation~\eqref{eq-best-output-drift-identity}
therefore yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq-prize-entry-derivative}
\left.\partial_\theta\mathcal B(0,\theta,T,\lambda;S_0)
\right|_{\theta=0}
=\frac{T}{c\sqrt{2\pi(S_0+\sigma^2T)}}.
\end{equation}
For fixed positive precision, differentiability follows from the local
mild HJB expansion; at zero precision it follows from the analytic
construction below. In both cases, the first-order integrand is bounded
on the fixed horizon because $Q_t\geq S_T>0$.
Consequently, the organizer's prize derivative at $(0,T_0)$ is
\[
\left.\partial_\theta\{u(\mathcal B)-\theta-D(T_0)\}\right|_{\theta=0}
=\frac{2T_0D'(T_0)}{c\sigma^2}-1,
\]
where the equality follows from
equation~\eqref{eq-no-prize-deadline-foc}.
In particular, the entirely exogenous inequalities
\[
\frac{c\sigma^4}{2S_0}
<D'\!\left(\frac{S_0}{\sigma^2}\right)
\leq\frac{\sigma^2u'(\sqrt{S_0/\pi})}{4\sqrt{\pi S_0}}
\]
are sufficient for a positive prize to improve on every no-prize design.
Indeed, the second inequality gives
$F_0'(S_0/\sigma^2)\geq0$ and hence
$T_0\geq S_0/\sigma^2$. Since $D'$ is nondecreasing, the first inequality
then implies $2T_0D'(T_0)/(c\sigma^2)>1$.
A sufficiently small positive prize at $T_0$ and any fixed finite
precision strictly improves the best no-prize payoff. It also dominates
all zero-deadline designs. This argument establishes prize entry, not
the ranking of feedback precisions.

We first establish a finite high-cost bound for prize exit, without
using a universal disclosure-difference bound.
This argument requires only increasing, concave utility and increasing,
convex delay costs, not the common shape bounds.
In this argument only, put $L=u'(b_0)>0$, $d=D'(T_0)>0$, and
$c_{\min}=2L^2/d$. The utility tangent and
equation~\eqref{eq-design-basic-output-bound} give
\[
J\le u(b_0)+L\sigma\sqrt{T/(2\pi)}
+L\sqrt{2\theta T/c}-\theta-D(T).
\]
Use $D(T)\ge D(T_0)+d(T-T_0)$ and the Young inequalities
$L\sqrt{2\theta T/c}\le\theta/2+L^2T/c$ and
$L\sigma\sqrt{T/(2\pi)}\le dT/4+L^2\sigma^2/(2\pi d)$.
Define the derived prize bound
\[
\Theta_+:=2\{dT_0-D(T_0)+L^2\sigma^2/(2\pi d)\}>0.
\]
For $c\ge c_{\min}$ these inequalities give
\[
J\le u(b_0)+\Theta_+/2-dT/4-\theta/2.
\]
Every design attaining at least $F_0(T_0)>u(b_0)$ thus has
$\theta\le\Theta_+$ and $T\le T_+:=2\Theta_+/d$.
The same output bound gives
\[
F_0(T_0)-u(b_0)
\le L\{\sigma/\sqrt{2\pi}+\sqrt{2\Theta_+/c_{\min}}\}\sqrt T.
\]
Consequently all payoff-relevant deadlines lie in the fixed
positive interval
\begin{equation}\label{eq-high-cost-deadline-interval}
T_-:=\left[
\frac{F_0(T_0)-u(b_0)}
{L\{\sigma/\sqrt{2\pi}+\sqrt{2\Theta_+/c_{\min}}\}}
\right]^2
\leq T\leq T_+.
\end{equation}
These bounds depend only on the fixed environment, not on the optimal
design or on $c$ within this half-line. No utility upper bound is used.

On this interval, exact equilibrium output is analytic in
$w=\theta/(c\sigma^2)$ near zero, uniformly over all precisions.
For $\lambda\geq\sigma^2/S_0^2$, including full disclosure, this follows
from equation~\eqref{eq-high-precision-output-majorant}; no difference
between two precisions is needed. For the remaining compact precision
interval, terminal posterior variances have a positive lower bound.
Here is a direct construction that also includes zero precision.
Normalize time and innovation variance to one, write
$p_i=U_{i,x}$ and $p_j=-U_{j,x}$, and use reversed time $\tau$.
The Fourier norm
\[
M(\tau)=\sum_{k=i,j}\int_{\mathbb R}
e^{\rho(\tau)|\xi|}|\widehat p_k(\xi,\tau)|\,d\xi,
\qquad \rho(\tau)=1-\tau/2,
\]
has uniformly bounded Gaussian terminal data, say $M(0)\leq M_0$.
The gradient equations have conservative quadratic fluxes
$p_i^2/2-p_ip_j$ and $p_ip_j-p_j^2/2$.
With $N$ denoting the same norm with the factor $|\xi|$, convolution and
nonnegative diffusion give
\[
M'(\tau)\leq\{-1/2+2|w|M(\tau)\}N(\tau).
\]
Choose $|w|$ so that $2|w|M_0<1/4$, with the Fourier transform normalized
so that convolution is submultiplicative. Frequency truncation then
bounds $M$ by $M_0$ for the whole horizon. To compare truncations, use
the smaller radius $\rho_\delta=\rho-\delta(1+\tau)$,
where $0<\delta<1/8$, and denote the difference norms by $M_d,N_d$.
The product rule and $|\xi|e^{-\delta|\xi|}\leq1/(e\delta)$ give
\[
M_d'\leq-\tfrac14N_d+C_\delta|w|M_0M_d
+C_\delta e^{-\delta n/2},
\]
for cutoffs of order at least $n$, after reducing the prize disk if
necessary. Their initial difference has the same exponentially small
bound. Gronwall's inequality proves uniform convergence on the smaller
strip; omitting the cutoff error proves uniqueness there.
The truncated equations are analytic in complex $w$, so convergence on
smaller disks preserves analyticity. For real $w$, the
scalar transport--diffusion equations preserve nonnegative gradients,
and HJB verification identifies the resulting policies with equilibrium
policies, also at zero precision.

The organizer's source is
$F=\Phi(x/\sqrt{Q_t})p_i+[1-\Phi(x/\sqrt{Q_t})]p_j$.
Both this source and the perceived-gap drift $w(p_i-p_j)$ are bounded
on a fixed complex strip: $Q_t$ is bounded below in the compact
parameter range. In the complex drift equation driven by the real
innovation Brownian motion, a sufficiently small $|w|$ keeps the
imaginary part of the state inside that strip for the whole horizon.
Picard iteration thus gives analytic dependence on $w$, and bounded
Feynman--Kac integrands permit expectation to pass through the analytic
limit. This proves the asserted uniform analyticity of output without
requiring positive diffusion at $\lambda=0$.

Cauchy's derivative estimate and
equation~\eqref{eq-prize-entry-derivative} now give a fixed $r>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq-prize-derivative-bound-dimensional}
\partial_\theta\mathcal B
\leq\frac{2T}{c\sqrt{2\pi(S_0+\sigma^2T)}},
\qquad T\in[T_-,T_+],\quad \theta/(c\sigma^2)\leq r.
\end{equation}
Take
\[
c_H>\max\left\{
c_{\min},\ \frac{\Theta_+}{\sigma^2r},\
\frac{2LT_+}{\sqrt{2\pi S_0}}
\right\}.
\]
For $c\geq c_H$, all payoff-relevant prizes satisfy $\theta\leq\Theta_+$.
Along the entire prize segment $[0,\Theta_+]$ at any deadline in
equation~\eqref{eq-high-cost-deadline-interval}, nonnegative effort and
concavity yield
\[
\partial_\theta\{u(\mathcal B)-\theta-D(T)\}
\leq\frac{2LT_+}{c\sqrt{2\pi S_0}}-1<0.
\]
Hence every positive payoff-relevant prize is strictly dominated by zero
prize at the same deadline. The unique best deadline is $T_0$.
This zero-prize design itself attains the global value, regardless of
precision; existence of equilibria at irrelevant large prizes is not
needed for this conclusion.

{\color{blue}
\emph{Global joint attainment near prize entry.}
Here the common delay-growth condition can be relaxed to increasing,
convex delay costs.
For the following narrower-risk extension, it is enough to
assume $-Bu''(B)\ge9u'(B)$ for $B\ge\sqrt{S_0/(2\pi)}$.
If
\begin{equation}\label{eq-entry-joint-positive-band}
\frac{S_0}{2}\le\sigma^2T_0\le2S_0,
\qquad
 c\ge\frac{9T_0D'(T_0)}{5\sigma^2},
\end{equation}
the original three-parameter problem attains a global maximum.
If also $c<2T_0D'(T_0)/\sigma^2$, every optimum has
$\theta^*>0$ and $T^*>0$.
The deadline $T_0$ is the external no-prize benchmark defined in
Proposition~\ref{prop-joint-design}, not a joint-design variable.
No restriction on average delay costs is required. The conclusion does
not rank disclosure regimes and remains valid if $D'(0)=0$ is relaxed.

For the proof, use only within this calculation
$a=S_0/(\sigma^2T_0)$, $x=T/T_0$, $d=D'(T_0)$,
$b=\sqrt{(S_0+\sigma^2T_0)/(2\pi)}$, and
$r=c\sigma^2/(2T_0d)$.
Here $a\in[1/2,2]$ and $d>0$. The attainment argument below applies
to all $r\ge9/10$; $r<1$ will be used only to prove a positive prize.
The no-prize first-order condition gives
$bu'(b)=2dT_0(1+a)$. Integrating the curvature bound gives
\[
\sup u-u(b)\le\frac{bu'(b)}8
=\frac{dT_0(1+a)}4.
\]
Any design that weakly improves the best no-prize payoff must therefore
satisfy $\theta+D(T)-D(T_0)\le dT_0(1+a)/4$.
Convexity of $D$ gives the compact payoff-relevant triangle
\[
\theta+dT\le\frac{dT_0(a+5)}4.
\]
For $T>0$, its cumulative incentive exposure satisfies
\[
\frac{\theta T}{c(S_0+\sigma^2T)}
\le\frac{x(a+5-4x)}{8r(a+x)}<\frac14.
\]
To check strictness, it is enough to take $r=9/10$.
The required inequality is
$20x^2-(5a+16)x+9a>0$; its discriminant is
$25a^2-560a+256<0$ on $a\in[1/2,2]$,
since it is decreasing there and equals $-71/4$ at $a=1/2$.
The strict exposure bound holds uniformly on the compact triangle;
at $T=0$ exposure is zero.

The no-signal construction establishing
equation~\eqref{eq-zero-precision-continuity} consequently applies
throughout this payoff-relevant set. Its continuity at zero precision,
positive-precision parameter continuity, and uniform high-precision
convergence on bounded prize--deadline sets make the exact objective
continuous after compactifying precision. It attains its maximum
on this triangle for every $r\ge9/10$. Designs outside the triangle have payoff below
the no-prize benchmark regardless of precision and cannot improve it.
For $r<1$, equation~\eqref{eq-prize-entry-derivative} and the
no-prize first-order condition give, at $T_0$,
\[
\left.\partial_\theta J(\theta,T_0,\lambda;\sigma)
\right|_{\theta=0}
=\frac{2T_0D'(T_0)}{c\sigma^2}-1>0.
\]
A small positive prize thus strictly improves every no-prize design.
All zero-deadline designs have payoff at most $u(\sqrt{S_0/(2\pi)})$,
which is strictly below the best no-prize payoff because $T_0>0$.
Hence every global optimum has a positive prize and deadline.
}

\emph{A weaker curvature requirement with convex marginal delay costs.}
If $D'$ is convex, the preceding global positive-prize result remains
valid with $-Bu''(B)\ge7u'(B)$ for
$B\ge\sqrt{S_0/(2\pi)}$. This includes quadratic delay costs.
To prove it, keep the preceding proof's notation and put
$k=2/(\eta-1)$ when $-Bu''(B)\ge\eta u'(B)$, $\eta\ge7$.
Convexity of $D'$ and $D'(0)=0$ give
$D'(t)\le dt/T_0$ for $t\le T_0$ and the reverse inequality
for $t\ge T_0$. Integrating on either side of $T_0$ yields
\[
D(T)-D(T_0)\ge\frac{dT_0}{2}(x^2-1).
\]
The utility-tail bound therefore restricts every payoff-relevant
design to
\[
\frac{\theta}{dT_0}\le\frac{1-x^2}{2}+k(a+1),
\qquad
\frac{\theta T}{c(S_0+\sigma^2T)}
\le\frac{x[(1-x^2)/2+k(a+1)]}{2r(a+x)}.
\]
For $0\le x\le1$, the last expression decreases with $a$:
the numerator of its $a$ derivative, apart from positive factors,
is $(x-1)(x+1+2k)/2$.
Taking $a=1/2$, $k=1/3$, and $r=9/10$ thus bounds it by
$x(1-x^2/2)/[(9/5)(1/2+x)]$.
This is strictly below $1/4$, since
\[
20x^3-22x+9
=(x-3/5)^2(20x+24)+\frac{9-10x}{25}>0.
\]
For $x\le4/5$, the last summand is at least $1/25$;
for $4/5\le x\le1$, the polynomial is increasing and its value
at $4/5$ is $41/25$.
For $x\ge1$, the expression instead increases with $a$.
Taking $a=2$ bounds it by
$x(3/2-x^2/2)/[(9/5)(2+x)]$ on the relevant set.
Its derivative has the sign of $3-3x^2-x^3<0$, so its maximum
is $5/27<1/4$ at $x=1$. On $0\le x\le1$, the polynomial bound also gives
\[
\frac14-\frac{x(1-x^2/2)}{(9/5)(1/2+x)}
=\frac{20x^3-22x+9}{72(1/2+x)}\ge\frac1{2700}.
\]
Thus the exposure stays uniformly below $1/4$ on the entire relevant set.
The quadratic budget also gives
$T\le T_0\sqrt{1+2k(a+1)}$ and
$\theta\le dT_0[1/2+k(a+1)]$.
The strict exposure bound, compactness, and the positive prize
derivative consequently complete the same attainment and positivity
argument as above. No precision ordering has been used.

\emph{Joint design under utility and delay-cost shape conditions.}
We now prove the full-disclosure and zero-prize conclusions in
part (iii) using the common utility and delay-growth bounds,
without imposing a
prize or deadline bound. Positive-prize conclusions under weaker
utility curvature and narrower risk bounds were proved at
equation~\eqref{eq-entry-joint-positive-band}.
Only within this proof, write
\[
a=\frac{S_0}{\sigma^2T_0},\qquad d=D'(T_0),\qquad
b=\sqrt{\frac{S_0+\sigma^2T_0}{2\pi}},\qquad
c_e=\frac{2T_0d}{\sigma^2},\qquad r=\frac c{c_e}.
\]
Here $1/3\leq a\leq3$ and $d>0$.
Let $\rho=\min(w_*,1/4)$ and choose
$\gamma_0\geq\max\{45,1+8/\rho\}$.
This single choice also implies the curvature bounds $9$ and $32$
used for the positive-prize and finite-precision conclusions.
The no-prize first-order condition gives $bu'(b)=2dT_0(a+1)$.
Integrating the utility curvature bound and using the delay elasticity
bound gives
\[
\sup u-u(b)\leq\frac{2dT_0(a+1)}{\gamma_0-1},
\qquad D(T_0)\leq\frac{dT_0}{\gamma_0}.
\]
Every design with payoff at least $F_0(T_0)$ therefore satisfies
\[
\theta+D(T)-D(T_0)\leq\sup u-u(b).
\]
Convexity of $D$, together with the preceding two bounds, implies
\[
T\leq T_0\left[1+\frac{2(a+1)}{\gamma_0-1}\right],
\qquad
\theta\leq dT_0\left[\frac{2(a+1)}{\gamma_0-1}
+\frac1{\gamma_0}\right].
\]
For all $r\geq9/16$, these bounds yield
\begin{equation}\label{eq-shape-joint-design-budget}
\frac{\theta}{c\sigma^2}
\leq\frac{4/(\gamma_0-1)+1/(2\gamma_0)}{9/16}
<\rho\leq w_*,
\qquad
T\leq\frac54T_0.
\end{equation}
Indeed $4/(\gamma_0-1)\leq\rho/2$ and
$1/(2\gamma_0)<\rho/16$.
Consequently $s\geq4/15>1/4$ at every relevant positive deadline,
and cumulative exposure is at most $w<1/4$.
The no-signal construction below supplies equilibrium and continuity
at zero precision on this domain. Positive-precision continuity and
the uniform high-precision limit also proved below then apply.
These derived bounds put all relevant designs in a compactified box;
the continuous objective attains its global maximum there.
Equation~\eqref{eq-uniform-disclosure-ranking} strictly excludes every
positive-prize finite-precision optimum.
If $9c_e/16\leq c<c_e$, equation~\eqref{eq-prize-entry-derivative}
gives a positive-prize candidate with payoff above $F_0(T_0)$.
Since $T_0>0$, zero-deadline payoffs are also strictly lower.
This proves the positive-prize, full-disclosure conclusion in part (iii).

To establish the global zero-prize boundary, first set $c=c_e$.
All potentially improving positive-prize designs have just been
reduced to full disclosure with $w<\min(w_*,1/4)$.
Equation~\eqref{eq-exact-full-weak-envelope}, with its correction
$1+w/4$ enlarged to $1+w/2$, gives
\[
\mathcal B(0,\theta,T,\infty;S_0)
\leq\sqrt{\frac{S_0+\sigma^2T}{2\pi}}
+\frac{\theta T}{c_e\sqrt{2\pi(S_0+\sigma^2T)}}(1+w/2).
\]
For $T>0$, put $x=T/T_0$, $m=a+x$,
$N=m+xw+xw^2/2$, and $K=3a+1+2x$ only in this calculation.
The output ratio is at most $N/\sqrt{m(a+1)}$.
The common utility curvature bound implies curvature at least five,
and hence, on either side of $b$,
\[
u(B)-u(b)\leq\frac{bu'(b)}4[1-(B/b)^{-4}],
\qquad B\geq\sqrt{S_0/(2\pi)}.
\]
Convex delay costs then give
\[
\frac{J(\theta,T,\infty;\sigma)-F_0(T_0)}{dT_0}
\leq\frac{P}{2N^4},\qquad
P=(a+3-2x-4w)N^4-(a+1)^3m^2.
\]
We show $P<0$ for $1/3\leq a\leq3$, $x>0$, and $w>0$.
Expand $P=\sum_{j=0}^9c_jw^j$. The first three coefficients are
\[
c_0=-m^2K(x-1)^2,\qquad
c_1=-4m^3(x-1)(2x-a),
\]
\[
c_2=2m^2x[a^2-5a+(2a+4)x-8x^2]<0,
\]
because the bracket is at most
$(9a^2-36a+4)/8\leq-7/8$.
The next two coefficients are negative:
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_3&=2mx[-10x^3-(a+1)x^2+a(3a-11)x-4a^2],\\
c_4&=\frac{x^2}{2}[-34x^3-(19a+29)x^2
+a(12a-74)x+3a^2(a-13)].
\end{aligned}
\]
Writing $L=a+3-2x\leq6$, the remaining coefficients satisfy
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_5&=x^2[(2L-4)x^2+(3L-24)mx-6m^2]\leq-4x^4,\\
c_6&=x^3[(3L/2-8)x+(L/2-12)m]\leq-8x^4,\\
c_7&=x^3[(L/2-6)x-2m]<0,\\
c_8&=x^4(L/16-2)<0,\qquad c_9=-x^4/4<0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the bounds for $c_5,c_6$ use $m\geq x$.
If $c_1\leq0$, these signs already give $P<0$.
Otherwise $x$ lies strictly between $a/2$ and one.
Completing the square gives
\[
c_0+c_1w+c_2w^2
=-m^2K\left[x-1+\frac{2m(2x-a)}K w\right]^2
+\frac{2m^2}K f(a,x)w^2,
\]
where
\[
f(a,x)=2a^4-(a^3+14a^2+5a)x
+(2a^2+4a+4)x^2-12ax^3-8x^4.
\]
For every $a\geq1/3$, this function strictly decreases with $x\geq0$:
\[
\partial_x f
\leq-a^3-14a^2-5a+(4a^2+8a+8)x-36ax^2
\leq-\frac{8a^4+122a^3+37a^2-8a-4}{9a}<0.
\]
The numerator is increasing for $a\geq1/3$ and equals $167/81$
at $a=1/3$.
If $a\leq2$, then $x>a/2$ and
$f(a,x)<f(a,a/2)=-3a^2(4a+1)/2<0$.
If $2<a\leq3$, then $x>1$ and
\[
f(a,x)<f(a,1)=2a^4-a^3-12a^2-13a-4\leq-16.
\]
The last polynomial is convex on $[2,3]$ and has endpoint
values $-54$ and $-16$.
Thus $P<0$ also when $c_1>0$.
Every positive-prize full-disclosure design is strictly dominated by
$(0,T_0)$; the strict precision comparison excludes all remaining
potentially improving positive-prize designs.
At $T=0$, a positive prize only reduces payoff.
Hence $c=c_e$ has the unique optimal prize--deadline pair $(0,T_0)$.
For $c>c_e$, keeping effective incentives $\theta/c$ fixed raises
prize expenditure relative to $c_e$ without changing equilibrium
output. Every positive-prize design remains strictly dominated.
This establishes the claimed global boundary, not merely a local
prize-entry test.

The shape region is nonempty. Fix $S_0,t_1>0$, take
$u(B)=1-e^{-kB}$ with $k\sqrt{S_0/(2\pi)}\geq\gamma_0$, and set
\[
K=\frac{\sqrt{S_0}u'(\sqrt{S_0/\pi})}{4\sqrt\pi},
\qquad D(T)=\frac K{\gamma_0}(T/t_1)^{\gamma_0}.
\]
At $\sigma^2=S_0/t_1$, the no-prize first-order condition holds at
$T_0=t_1$, and strict concavity makes it the unique no-prize optimum.
The risk condition is then strict at both ends, and the effort-cost
interval has positive length. Keeping this utility and delay function
fixed, sufficiently low innovation risk also meets part (ii), while
high effort costs meet part (i). The three sufficient regimes thus
coexist in one fixed utility--delay environment as risk and effort
costs vary. This example verifies nonemptiness, not a moderate bound
on the universal shape threshold.

\emph{An alternative full-disclosure parameter window.}
The following sufficient region does not require globally high delay
elasticity. Suppose, for some $\gamma\geq\gamma_0$,
$-Bu''(B)\geq\gamma u'(B)$ for $B\geq\sqrt{S_0/\pi}$, and
\[
\frac{3\sigma^2u'(\sqrt{S_0/\pi})}{16\sqrt{\pi S_0}}
\leq D'(S_0/\sigma^2)
\leq\frac{\sigma^2u'(\sqrt{S_0/\pi})}{4\sqrt{\pi S_0}},
\]
\[
D(S_0/\sigma^2)\leq
\frac{\sqrt{S_0}u'(\sqrt{S_0/\pi})}{4\gamma\sqrt\pi}.
\]
If
\begin{equation}\label{eq-full-disclosure-primitive-window}
\frac{\sqrt{S_0}u'(\sqrt{S_0/\pi})}{4\sqrt\pi\,\sigma^2}
\leq c<\frac{3\sqrt{S_0}u'(\sqrt{S_0/\pi})}{8\sqrt\pi\,\sigma^2},
\end{equation}
every joint optimum has a positive prize and deadline and full disclosure.
In the proof only, put
$K=\sqrt{S_0}u'(\sqrt{S_0/\pi})/(4\sqrt\pi)>0$ and
$\tau=S_0/\sigma^2$. The displayed primitive conditions give directly
\[
\frac{3K}4\le\tau D'(\tau)\le K,\qquad
D(\tau)\le\frac K\gamma.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq-full-disclosure-primitive-window} gives
$K/\sigma^2\le c<3K/(2\sigma^2)$.

Let $g(T)=u'(b(T))b'(T)$ be the no-prize marginal innovation value.
It satisfies $g(\tau)=K/\tau\ge D'(\tau)$, so $T_0\ge\tau>0$.
Convexity therefore gives
\[
c_{\rm entry}:=\frac{2T_0D'(T_0)}{\sigma^2}
\ge\frac{2\tau D'(\tau)}{\sigma^2}
\ge\frac{3K}{2\sigma^2}>c.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq-prize-entry-derivative} therefore gives a positive
prize candidate strictly better than every zero-prize and zero-deadline design.

To exclude remote strong incentives, compare the best no-prize value
with the feasible no-prize deadline $\tau$. Integrating the utility-tail
condition gives
\[
\sup u-u(b(\tau))
\le\frac{b(\tau)u'(b(\tau))}{\gamma-1}
=\frac{4K}{\gamma-1}.
\]
This finite tail bound proves that utility is bounded above in this region.
Every design whose payoff is at least $F_0(T_0)$ therefore satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq-payoff-relevant-budget}
\theta+D(T)\le\sup_{B\ge0}u(B)-F_0(T_0)=:H_0.
\end{equation}
Since $u(b(T_0))-D(T_0)\ge u(b(\tau))-D(\tau)$,
the payoff-relevant budget in
equation~\eqref{eq-payoff-relevant-budget} satisfies
\[
H_0\le\frac{4K}{\gamma-1}+D(\tau)
\le\frac{4K}{\gamma-1}+\frac K\gamma
<\frac{5K}{\gamma-1}.
\]
Every payoff-relevant design satisfies $\theta+D(T)\le H_0$.
Using $c\sigma^2\ge K$ and the convex tangent at $\tau$ therefore gives
\[
\frac\theta{c\sigma^2}<\frac5{\gamma-1},\qquad
T\le\tau+\frac{4K}{(\gamma-1)D'(\tau)}
\le\left(1+\frac{16}{3(\gamma-1)}\right)\tau<4\tau.
\]
The deadline bound also holds for $T<\tau$; hence each relevant positive
deadline has $s>1/4$.
The already chosen $\gamma_0$ satisfies
$5/(\gamma_0-1)<\min(w_*,1/4)$.
Every relevant prize is then in the exact sorting range of
equation~\eqref{eq-uniform-disclosure-ranking}.
Its exposure $\theta T/[c(S_0+\sigma^2T)]$ is also below $1/4$,
so the selected no-signal boundary is covered by the construction below.
Holding prize and deadline fixed, any finite precision is strictly
dominated by full disclosure.

The budget confines $(\theta,T)$ to a compact set.
The full-disclosure output limit proved above is continuous on its
positive interior and at both axes, using
equation~\eqref{eq-design-basic-output-bound}.
Consequently the objective attains its maximum on the full-disclosure
face. The strictly profitable positive-prize candidate excludes both
axes; exact sorting excludes every finite precision.
This proves the alternative parameter window, including global attainment and
$\theta^*>0,T^*>0,\lambda^*=\infty$.

To identify the sufficient risk range, let $t_aD'(t_a)=3K/4$,
$t_1D'(t_1)=K$, and $D(t_D)=K/\gamma$.
These roots are unique: $D$ and $tD'(t)$ are continuous and strictly
increasing from zero to infinity. The latter statement uses
$D'(t)\ge D(t)/t>0$ for $t>0$, not differentiability of $D'$.
Thus $t_a<t_1$, and the admissible natural innovation times are
$\tau\in[t_a,t_+]$, where $t_+=\min(t_1,t_D)$.
This interval is nonempty exactly when $D(t_a)\le K/\gamma$, and has
positive length under strict inequality. Requiring the stronger
$D(t_1)\le K/\gamma$ would unnecessarily exclude valid risks.
For example, if $D(T)=dT^{7\gamma/8}$ with $d>0$, then
$D(t_a)=6K/(7\gamma)<K/\gamma<D(t_1)=8K/(7\gamma)$.
This fixed delay function admits a positive-length risk region although
the stronger complete-window test fails.

The alternative full-disclosure region, the finite-precision region,
and the high-cost no-prize region coexist for fixed exogenous primitives.
Choose any $S_0,t_1>0$, $\gamma\ge\max(\gamma_0,32)$,
and $u(B)=1-e^{-kB}$ with $k\sqrt{S_0/(2\pi)}\ge\gamma$.
Compute $K$ from this utility, and put
$h=t_1/(2\gamma)$, $a_0=K/(4\gamma t_1)$, $a_1=K/t_1-a_0$.
A fixed $C^1$, increasing convex delay function with $D'(0)=0$ is
\[
D(T)=a_0
\begin{cases}
T^2/(2t_1),&T\le t_1,\\
T-t_1/2,&T>t_1
\end{cases}
+
\begin{cases}
0,&T\le t_1-h,\\
a_1(T-t_1+h)^2/(2h),&t_1-h<T\le t_1,\\
a_1(T-t_1+h/2),&T>t_1.
\end{cases}
\]
It has $D'(t_1)=K/t_1$ and
$D(t_1)=K(3/(8\gamma)-1/(16\gamma^2))<K/\gamma$.
The sufficient risk interval and cost interval therefore have positive
length. Delay is linear after $t_1$, so no sustained high delay
elasticity is assumed.
The same utility meets part (ii), which yields its low-risk finite
precision interval; that risk bound may be reduced below
$\sqrt{S_0/t_1}/2$.
Inside the full-disclosure risk window $T_0>0$, so part (i) also gives a
high-cost no-prize region in this same environment.
The three regimes are therefore compatible, not conditions obtained
by changing primitives separately for each case.
The sufficient full-disclosure window may nevertheless be narrow:
convexity gives
$D(t_+)\ge(3K/4)(t_+/t_a-1)$, hence its upper-to-lower risk ratio is
$\sqrt{t_+/t_a}\le\sqrt{1+4/(3\gamma)}$.
We make no claim that these sufficient bounds exhaust the regimes.

Finally, the cost comparison stated in the proposition follows directly
from global optimality. Dividing each player's HJB and payoff by
$\theta$ shows that policies and output depend on $\theta,c$ only through
$\alpha=\theta/c$, with zero prize interpreted by continuity. Write the
organizer's gross value as
$A(\alpha,T,\lambda)=u(\mathcal B(0,c\alpha,T,\lambda;S_0))-D(T)$;
$A$ does not depend on $c$. If optima at $c_1<c_2$ have effective prizes
$\alpha_1,\alpha_2$, their two optimality inequalities give
\[
A_1-c_1\alpha_1\geq A_2-c_1\alpha_2,
\qquad A_2-c_2\alpha_2\geq A_1-c_2\alpha_1.
\]
Adding yields $(c_2-c_1)(\alpha_1-\alpha_2)\geq0$.
If zero prize is optimal at $c_1$, every positive effective prize at
$c_2$ incurs the additional strictly positive cost
$(c_2-c_1)\alpha$. It is therefore strictly inferior to zero prize.
This comparison allows both the deadline and precision to change.

\emph{Value-function control at high precision.}
The next argument remains within the finite-precision games; it does not assume existence or continuity of a separate full-disclosure equilibrium. Fix $\theta,T>0$ and take $\lambda\geq\sigma^2/S_0^2$, so $a_t=\lambda S_t^2\geq\sigma^2$ and $a_t$ decreases in $t$. Put
\[
W=U_i+U_j,\qquad \Delta=U_i-U_j,\qquad
f(d)=1+\frac2w\log\frac{\cosh(wd/2)}{\cosh(w/2)},\qquad
K_w(d)=\frac{d+\sinh(wd)/w}{2}.
\]
Symmetry and interiority give $\Delta(-y,t)=-\Delta(y,t)$ and $\Delta_y>0$. Direct addition and subtraction of the HJB equations yield
\[
W_t+\frac a2W_{yy}+\frac\alpha4(3W_y^2-\Delta_y^2)=0,\qquad
\Delta_t+\frac a2\Delta_{yy}+\frac\alpha2W_y\Delta_y=0.
\]
Define the defect $E=W-f(\Delta)$ and the auxiliary generator
\[
\mathcal A=\partial_t+\frac a2\partial_{yy}+b\partial_y,
\qquad b=\alpha f'(\Delta)\Delta_y.
\]
Let $\mathbb E^b_{y,t}$ denote expectation under the auxiliary diffusion generated by $\mathcal A$ and started from $y$ at $t$.
Because $f''=(w/2)(1-f'^2)$, exact differentiation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq-design-defect-equations}
\mathcal A E=-\frac{3\alpha}{4}E_y^2
-\frac\alpha4\left(\frac a{\sigma^2}-1\right)(1-f'^2)\Delta_y^2,
\qquad
\mathcal A\Delta=-\frac\alpha2E_y\Delta_y+\frac\alpha2f'\Delta_y^2.
\end{equation}
At $T$, $E=1-f(2\Phi(y/\sqrt{S_T})-1)\geq0$. Dynkin's formula applied to the first equation in~\eqref{eq-design-defect-equations} gives
\[
E(y,t)=\mathbb E^b_{y,t}\!\left[E(Y_T,T)
+\int_t^T\left\{\frac{3\alpha}{4}E_y^2
+\frac\alpha4\left(\frac a{\sigma^2}-1\right)
(1-f'^2)\Delta_y^2\right\}ds\right]\geq0.
\]
The payoff bound $W\leq1$ and $f\geq0$ then imply $0\leq E\leq1$.

Equation~\eqref{eq-design-defect-equations} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq-design-defect-energy}
\mathbb E^b_{y,t}\int_t^T E_y^2\,ds
\leq\frac{4E(y,t)}{3\alpha},\qquad
\mathbb E^b_{y,t}\int_t^T\Delta_y^2\,ds
\leq\frac2{\sigma^2}+\frac{4\alpha}{3\sigma^4}.
\end{equation}
For the second inequality, $\Delta f'(\Delta)\geq0$, $|\Delta|\leq1$, and Young's inequality give
\[
\mathcal A(\Delta^2)
=a\Delta_y^2-\alpha\Delta E_y\Delta_y
+\alpha\Delta f'(\Delta)\Delta_y^2
\geq\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\Delta_y^2
-\frac{\alpha^2}{2\sigma^2}E_y^2.
\]
Dynkin's formula, $\Delta(Y_T,T)^2\leq1$, and the first energy estimate then yield the stated bound.
The same bounds hold conditionally at stopping times.

Fix $\delta>0$ with $\delta<T$, and let
$\zeta_\lambda=a_\delta/\sigma^2-1\to0$.
For $k=2w$ and $A=w^2e^{2w}$, equations~\eqref{eq-design-defect-equations} imply, on $[\delta,T]$,
\[
\mathcal A\{e^{kE}+A\zeta_\lambda\Delta^2\}
\geq\frac{\sigma^2w^2}{2}(1-A\zeta_\lambda)E_y^2\geq0
\]
once $A\zeta_\lambda\leq1$. Since $b(y,t)y\geq0$, comparison of the reflected absolute-value process with driftless reflected Brownian motion bounds expectations of nonnegative decreasing functions of $|y|$ by their driftless counterparts. The terminal function $e^{kE(\cdot,T)}-1$ is even, decreasing on the positive half-line, and has integral $C_w\sqrt{S_T}$ for a finite constant $C_w$. With $g_\lambda(y)=e^{kE(y,T)}-1$, Dynkin's formula, $|\Delta|\leq1$, and the maximum density of a centered Gaussian with variance at least $\sigma^2(T-t)$ give
\[
e^{kE(y,t)}-1
\leq \mathbb E^b_{y,t}g_\lambda(Y_T)+A\zeta_\lambda
\leq\frac{\|g_\lambda\|_{L^1(\mathbb R)}}
{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi(T-t)}}+A\zeta_\lambda.
\]
Since $\|g_\lambda\|_{L^1}=C_w\sqrt{S_T}$ and
$\log(1+x)\leq x$, this yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq-design-defect-small}
0\leq E(y,t)\leq
\frac1k\left\{\frac{C_w\sqrt{S_T}}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi(T-t)}}
+A\zeta_\lambda\right\},\qquad \delta\leq t<T.
\end{equation}
Thus $E\to0$ uniformly in $y$ on time intervals bounded away from zero and $T$.

We next identify $\Delta$ without assuming convergence of policy derivatives. Let $\mathbb P^b$ be the auxiliary law and define
\[
Z_{t,T}:=\mathcal E\!\left(-\int_t^T\frac{b(Y_s,s)}{\sqrt{a_s}}\,dW_s^b\right),
\qquad
\frac{d\mathbb P^0}{d\mathbb P^b}\bigg|_{\mathcal F_T}=Z_{t,T}.
\]
Under $\mathbb P^0$, $Y$ has zero drift. Since $|f'|\leq1$, $a\geq\sigma^2$, and $b=\alpha f'(\Delta)\Delta_y$, equation~\eqref{eq-design-defect-energy} implies, also conditionally at stopping times,
\[
\mathbb E^b_\tau\int_\tau^T\frac{b^2}{a}\,ds
\leq\frac{\alpha^2}{\sigma^2}
\left(\frac2{\sigma^2}+\frac{4\alpha}{3\sigma^4}\right).
\]
Thus the stochastic integral in $Z_{t,T}$ has a BMO norm bounded independently of $\lambda$. Its stochastic exponential satisfies a reverse H\"older inequality: for some $q>1$,
\[
\sup_{\lambda}\sup_{t\leq T}
\sup_{y\in\mathbb R}\mathbb E^b_{y,t}[Z_{t,T}^{\,q}]<\infty;
\]
see \citet[Theorem~3.1]{Kazamaki1994}. The conditional bounds in equation~\eqref{eq-design-defect-energy}, applied successively at stopping times, also give uniform moments of every finite order for
$\int E_y^2ds$ and $\int\Delta_y^2ds$.

Fix a time interval separated from zero and $T$. Equation~\eqref{eq-design-defect-small} and the first inequality in equation~\eqref{eq-design-defect-energy} give
\[
\mathbb E^b_{y,t}\int_t^T E_y^2\,ds=o(1)
\]
uniformly on that interval. Interpolating this vanishing first moment with the uniform higher moments and then applying H\"older's inequality with the preceding reverse H\"older bound transfers the estimate to $\mathbb P^0$. The second energy estimate transfers in the same way and remains bounded. Hence
\[
\mathbb E^0_{y,t}\int_t^T E_y^2\,ds=o(1),\qquad
\mathbb E^0_{y,t}\int_t^T\Delta_y^2\,ds=O(1),
\]
uniformly for $y\in\mathbb R$ and $t$ in such time intervals.

Since $K_w''/K_w'=wf'$, the driftless generator satisfies
\[
\left(\partial_t+\frac a2\partial_{yy}\right)K_w(\Delta)
=-\frac\alpha2K_w'(\Delta)E_y\Delta_y
+\frac w2(a-\sigma^2)f'(\Delta)K_w'(\Delta)\Delta_y^2.
\]
The absolute expected integral of the first term is bounded by a constant times
\[
\left(\mathbb E^0_{y,t}\int_t^T E_y^2ds\right)^{1/2}
\left(\mathbb E^0_{y,t}\int_t^T\Delta_y^2ds\right)^{1/2}=o(1),
\]
while that of the second is $O(\zeta_\lambda)$. The difference between the terminal function $K_w(2\Phi(y/\sqrt{S_T})-1)$ and $K_w(1)\operatorname{sign}(y)$ has $L^1(\mathbb R)$ norm $O(\sqrt{S_T})$. Convolution with the driftless Gaussian kernel therefore makes this terminal error uniformly $o(1)$ whenever $t$ stays away from $T$. Since the driftless variance from $t$ to $T$ converges to $\sigma^2(T-t)$, we obtain, uniformly in space and away from the two time endpoints,
\begin{equation}\label{eq-design-value-limit}
\Delta(y,t)\longrightarrow d_w(y,t),\qquad
W(y,t)\longrightarrow f(d_w(y,t)),\qquad
K_w(d_w(y,t))
=K_w(1)\left[2\Phi\!\left(\frac y{\sigma\sqrt{T-t}}\right)-1\right].
\end{equation}
All estimates are uniform when $\theta,T$ vary over compact subsets of $(0,\infty)$.

\emph{A uniform high-precision output bound.}
Let $Y_\delta=\tilde y_\delta$. Its martingale part is normal with variance
$S_0+\sigma^2\delta-S_\delta$, while equation~\eqref{eq-design-energy-budget} bounds the expected absolute drift before $\delta$ by $\sqrt{2\alpha\delta}$. Applying the same budget conditionally at $\delta$ and then conditional Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\begin{align*}
\mathcal B(0,\theta,T,\lambda;S_0)
-\sqrt{\frac{S_0+\sigma^2T}{2\pi}}
&\leq\sqrt{2\alpha\delta}\\
&\quad+\sqrt{2\alpha(T-\delta)}\,
\mathbb E\sqrt{1-W(Y_\delta,\delta)}.
\end{align*}
Using equation~\eqref{eq-design-value-limit} and then letting
$\delta\downarrow0$ replaces the final expectation by
$\mathbb E\sqrt{1-f(d_w(Y_0,0))}$, where
$Y_0\sim\mathcal N(0,S_0)$. The passage to the initial distribution follows from the preceding drift bound and the convergence of the Gaussian variance to $S_0$.

For $d>0$, write $x=wd/2$. Then
\[
\frac{wf'(d)K_w(d)}{K_w'(d)}
=1+(x\tanh x-1)\operatorname{sech}^2x<\frac98.
\]
For $0\le x\le1$ the added term is nonpositive. For $x\ge1$ it is at
most $4(x-1)e^{-2x}\le2e^{-3}<1/8$; the last inequality follows from
$e^3>1+3+9/2+27/6+81/24>16$.
Integrating from $d$ to one and using equation~\eqref{eq-design-value-limit} gives
\[
1-f(d_w(y,0))
\leq-\frac9{8w}\log\left|2\Phi\!\left(\frac y{\sigma\sqrt T}\right)-1\right|.
\]
Define the universal constant
\[
C_{\rm G}:=\frac32\int_{\mathbb R}
\sqrt{-\log|2\Phi(z)-1|}\,dz.
\]
The singularity at zero and the Gaussian tails are integrable. Moreover,
$C_{\rm G}<5$. Jensen's inequality applied to $e^{-x^2/2}$ on
$[0,z]$ gives $2\Phi(z)-1\ge\sqrt{2/\pi}\,z e^{-z^2/6}$.
Consequently, Cauchy--Schwarz gives, for $0\le a<b\le1$,
\[
\int_a^b\sqrt{-\log(2\Phi(z)-1)}\,dz
\le\sqrt{(b-a)
\left[z\left(1+\tfrac12\log(\pi/2)-\log z\right)
+\frac{z^3}{18}\right]_a^b},
\]
where $z\log z=0$ at zero. For $z\ge a\ge1$, the inequality
$-\log(1-x)\le x+x^2/[2(1-x)]$ yields
\[
-\log(2\Phi(z)-1)
\le\frac{2\Phi(a)}{2\Phi(a)-1}[1-\Phi(z)].
\]
Since $[z(1-\Phi(z))-\varphi(z)]'=1-\Phi(z)$, another
Cauchy--Schwarz bound gives
\[
\int_a^b\sqrt{-\log(2\Phi(z)-1)}\,dz
\le\sqrt{(b-a)\frac{2\Phi(a)}{2\Phi(a)-1}
\left[z(1-\Phi(z))-\varphi(z)\right]_a^b}.
\]
On $[3,\infty)$, the Mills bound and
$\int_3^\infty e^{-z^2/4}dz\le(2/3)e^{-9/4}$ give
\[
\int_3^\infty\sqrt{-\log(2\Phi(z)-1)}\,dz
\le\sqrt{\frac4{27}\frac{2\Phi(3)}{2\Phi(3)-1}\varphi(3)}.
\]
These bounds on $[0,1/2]$, $[1/2,1]$, $[1,3/2]$, $[3/2,2]$,
$[2,3]$, and $[3,\infty)$ are respectively less than
$0.6952$, $0.3969$, $0.2581$, $0.1498$, $0.1290$, and $0.0363$.
They can be verified with $3.14159<\pi<3.14160$: the even and odd
Taylor truncations at orders 60 and 61 bound $e^{-z^2/2}$ and its
integral, while the first 40 terms of
$\log x=2\sum_{n\ge0}[(x-1)/(x+1)]^{2n+1}/(2n+1)$ bound the
logarithms, with the remainder bounded by the corresponding geometric
tail. Their sum is $1.6653<5/3$, so symmetry gives $C_{\rm G}<5$.

The density of $Y_0/(\sigma\sqrt T)$ is bounded by
$1/\sqrt{2\pi S_0/(\sigma^2T)}$. Substitution in the conditional bound above therefore yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq-design-exact-upper}
\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\mathcal B(0,\theta,T,\lambda;S_0)
\leq\sqrt{\frac{S_0+\sigma^2T}{2\pi}}
+\frac{C_{\rm G}\sigma^2T}{\sqrt{2\pi S_0}}
<\sqrt{\frac{S_0+\sigma^2T}{2\pi}}
+\frac{5\sigma^2T}{\sqrt{2\pi S_0}}.
\end{equation}
The cutoff estimates are uniform when prize and deadline range over positive compact sets. Equation~\eqref{eq-design-basic-output-bound} controls sequences approaching $\theta=0$ or $T=0$.

The same estimates establish the existence of the high-precision output limit. Fix $0<\delta<T/2$. On $[\delta,T-\delta]$, equation~\eqref{eq-design-value-limit} and interior parabolic estimates imply locally uniform convergence of the policy gradients. The state drifts therefore converge there, and coupling the state diffusions gives convergence of the output accumulated on this middle interval. Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz and equation~\eqref{eq-design-energy-budget} bound the expected contribution of either omitted interval of length $\delta$ by $\sqrt{2\alpha\delta}$; the terminal filtering error is $O(\sqrt{S_T})$. Letting first $\lambda\to\infty$ and then $\delta\downarrow0$ proves output convergence.

This convergence is uniform when $(\theta,T)$ ranges over a compact subset of $(0,\infty)^2$, because all constants above are then uniform. To include the axes of any bounded prize--deadline set, observe from equation~\eqref{eq-design-basic-output-bound} that the effort-induced output gain is at most $\sqrt{2\theta T/c}$. It is therefore uniformly small whenever $\theta$ or $T$ is small, under both finite precision and the limiting policy. Covering the remaining parameter set by a positive compact rectangle proves uniform convergence on every bounded prize--deadline set.

0<\mathcal B(0,\theta,T,\infty;S_0)

\emph{The no-signal boundary and attainment.}
We first control the degenerate boundary $\lambda=0$ whenever
$\theta T/[c(S_0+\sigma^2T)]\leq1/4$. In particular, this includes every design with $\theta/(c\sigma^2)\leq1/4$. Fix $T>0$, put $Q_0=S_0+\sigma^2T$, scale state by $\sqrt{Q_0}$ and time by $T$, reverse time, and set $\epsilon=\theta T/(cQ_0)$. For $\lambda TQ_0\leq1/101$, the normalized value gradients
\[
p=U_{i,x},\qquad r=-U_{j,x}
\]
satisfy
\[
p_\tau=\frac{a(\tau)}2p_{xx}
+\epsilon\partial_x(p^2/2-pr),\qquad
r_\tau=\frac{a(\tau)}2r_{xx}
+\epsilon\partial_x(pr-r^2/2),
\]
where $a(\tau)=\lambda S_{T(1-\tau)}^2T/Q_0\geq0$ and $a\equiv0$ without a signal. Since $S_t\leq S_0+\sigma^2t\leq Q_0$, integrating the variance equation gives
\[
v:=S_T/Q_0=1-\frac{\lambda}{Q_0}\int_0^T S_t^2dt
\geq1-\lambda TQ_0\geq100/101.
\]
Both reversed-time initial gradients are Gaussian densities with variance $v$. For the Fourier norm
\[
\|p\|_\rho=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{\rho|\xi|}
|\widehat p(\xi)|\,d\xi,
\]
put $M=\|p\|_\rho+\|r\|_\rho$ and
$N=\|p_x\|_\rho+\|r_x\|_\rho$.
At radius one their total norm is
\[
M(0)=\frac{2e^{1/(2v)}}{\sqrt{2\pi v}}
\operatorname{erfc}\!\left(-\frac1{\sqrt{2v}}\right)<\frac{23}{10}.
\]
For an explicit bound, $1/\sqrt{2v}<5/7$ and the alternating series for $\operatorname{erf}$ give $\operatorname{erfc}(-1/\sqrt{2v})<17/10$. Using $\pi>25/8$ and $e^{101/100}<11/4$, the square of the displayed norm is less than $321079/62500<529/100$.
Writing $P=\|p\|_\rho$, $R=\|r\|_\rho$, $N_p=\|p_x\|_\rho$, and $N_r=\|r_x\|_\rho$, symmetry gives $r(x,\tau)=p(-x,\tau)$, hence $P=R$ and $N_p=N_r$. The equations and symmetric Fourier cutoffs preserve this reflection, including for complex $\epsilon$. The convolution product inequality therefore bounds the nonlinear terms by
\[
(P+2R)N_p+(2P+R)N_r=\frac32MN.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac{dM}{d\tau}\leq(\rho'+3|\epsilon|M/2)N.
\]
With $\rho(\tau)=1-9\tau/10$, a bootstrap yields $M(\tau)\leq M(0)<23/10$ and $\rho(\tau)\geq1/10>0$, since $3|\epsilon|M/2<69/80<9/10$. Apply this estimate first to symmetric Fourier-cutoff equations. Let $z^N=(p^N,r^N)$ and $\widetilde z^{\widetilde N}=(\widetilde p^{\widetilde N},\widetilde r^{\widetilde N})$ solve two such systems, with cutoff levels $N,\widetilde N$ and coefficients $(a,\epsilon)$ and $(\widetilde a,\widetilde\epsilon)$. To control the derivative in their difference equation, fix $0<\delta<1/10$ and use the shrinking radius $\rho_d(\tau)=\rho(\tau)-\delta$. Write $M_d=\|z^N-\widetilde z^{\widetilde N}\|_{\rho_d}$ and $N_d=\|\partial_x(z^N-\widetilde z^{\widetilde N})\|_{\rho_d}$, with component norms summed. The flux Jacobian is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}p-r&-p\\r&p-r\end{smallmatrix}\right)$; at each symmetric intermediate state its column norms are at most $3M/2$. Its spatial derivative and the coefficient-difference terms are bounded using the extra radius $\delta$. Thus
\[
M_d'\leq\left\{-\frac9{10}+\frac32\max\{|\epsilon|,|\widetilde\epsilon|\}\max\{M,\widetilde M\}\right\}N_d
+C_\delta M_d
+C_\delta\bigl(|a(\tau)-\widetilde a(\tau)|
+|\epsilon-\widetilde\epsilon|\bigr)
+R_{N,\widetilde N}(\tau),
\]
where $C_\delta$ is independent of the cutoffs and
$\|R_{N,\widetilde N}\|_{L^1(0,1)}\to0$ as
$N,\widetilde N\to\infty$. The latter follows from the radius gap $\delta$, which makes the discarded Fourier tails exponentially small. The coefficient of $N_d$ is at most $-3/80$ when both exposures are at most $1/4$, so that term can be dropped. For common coefficients, Gronwall's inequality shows that the cutoff solutions are Cauchy; their limit is a smooth analytic solution. For any fixed $0<\rho_*<1/10-\delta$, the same argument gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq-zero-precision-continuity}
\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq1}\|z(\tau)-\widetilde z(\tau)\|_{\rho_*}
\leq C_{\rho_*}
\left(\|z(0)-\widetilde z(0)\|_{1}
+\|a-\widetilde a\|_{L^1(0,1)}
+|\epsilon-\widetilde\epsilon|\right).
\end{equation}
In particular, the solution is unique in this symmetric bounded analytic class.

As $\lambda\downarrow0$, the variance equation gives
$a\to0$ uniformly and $S_T\to S_0+\sigma^2T$, so the Gaussian initial gradients converge in the radius-one norm. Equation~\eqref{eq-zero-precision-continuity} then gives uniform convergence of the equilibrium gradients and hence of the policies. The state equations are ordinary differential equations at $\lambda=0$; their globally Lipschitz drifts and the policy convergence give convergence of the state paths and expected output. This proves continuity at zero and defines the selected no-signal equilibrium.

Finally, the gradient signs needed for admissibility do not rely on diffusion. The equations for $p,r$ are scalar transport--diffusion equations with respective drifts $\epsilon(p-r)$ and potentials $-\epsilon r_x$ and $\epsilon p_x$. All coefficients and spatial derivatives are bounded by the analytic estimates. The scalar positivity principle for positive Gaussian initial data gives $p,r>0$, including at $a=0$, where the representation is along characteristics. In conservation form their flux coefficients are bounded, so their initial unit masses are preserved. Integrating $p$ from $-\infty$ and $r$ from $+\infty$ reconstructs bounded values with the required Gaussian terminal data and HJB equations. The completion-of-the-square verification in Theorem~\ref{thm-equilibrium-effort} remains valid with $a=0$ (It\^o's formula reduces to the chain rule). For positive precision, bounded-gradient uniqueness identifies this analytic solution with the exact equilibrium.

On positive compact precision intervals, the uniform parabolic estimates and bounded-gradient uniqueness from Theorem~\ref{thm-equilibrium-effort} give continuous dependence on parameters. Together with the analytic estimate near zero and equation~\eqref{eq-design-basic-output-bound} at $\theta=0$ or $T=0$, this proves continuity of $\mathcal B$ on every bounded design box satisfying $\theta T/[c(S_0+\sigma^2T)]\leq1/4$.

We sharpen the output guarantee using the exact no-signal equilibrium.
Put $Q=S_0+\sigma^2T$ and $\epsilon=\theta T/(cQ)\leq1/4$.
With state normalized by $\sqrt Q$ and reversed time $\tau=(T-t)/T$,
its gradients solve
\[
p_\tau=\epsilon\{(p-r)p_x-pr_x\},\qquad
r_\tau=\epsilon\{(p-r)r_x+rp_x\},\qquad p(x,0)=r(x,0)=G_1(x).
\]
The preceding Fourier estimates also apply to complex $\epsilon$ with
$|\epsilon|$ in place of $\epsilon$. At terminal variance one, the initial
norm $M_0<23/10$ permits the open disk
$|\epsilon|<3/(5M_0)>1/4$. The cutoff solutions are holomorphic
there and converge locally uniformly by the difference estimate.
Thus $p(x,\tau)=\sum_{n\geq0}(\epsilon\tau)^np_n(x)$ converges throughout
the required exposure range. Symmetry gives $r(x,\tau)=p(-x,\tau)$ and
\[
(n+1)p_{n+1}
=\sum_{a+b=n}\{1-(-1)^a-(-1)^b\}p_ap_b',\qquad p_0=G_1.
\]
Each coefficient is an entire Gaussian times a real polynomial of
parity $n$. To check its sign at zero, define $q_n(y)=p_n(iy)$ for even
$n$, and $q_n(y)=p_n(iy)/i$ for odd $n$; this evaluates coefficient
functions, not the full equilibrium outside its analytic strip.
For even $n$, their recurrence is the sum of $q_aq_b'$ over even pairs
plus three times that sum over odd pairs, divided by $n+1$.
For odd $n$, it is the sum over all pairs, divided by $n+1$.
Starting from $q_0=(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{y^2/2}$, differentiation and
multiplication preserve nonnegative Taylor coefficients. Hence
$p_{2n}(0)\geq0$, $p_{2n+1}(0)=0$, and $p(0,\tau)\geq(2\pi)^{-1/2}$.
The equilibrium posterior mean stays at zero, and the contestants exert
equal effort. Their terminal gap has variance $Q$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq-design-zero-candidate}
\mathcal B(0,\theta,T,0;S_0)
=\sqrt{\frac Q{2\pi}}+
\frac\theta{c\sqrt Q}\int_0^T p\!\left(0,\frac{T-t}{T}\right)dt
\geq\sqrt{\frac Q{2\pi}}+
\frac{\theta T}{c\sqrt{2\pi Q}}.
\end{equation}
This bound uses the nonlinear equilibrium, not a leading-order policy.

We now prove the low-innovation comparison, first for the curvature-22
extension on $3c_F/4\le c\le2c_F$.
This argument needs only increasing, convex delay costs, so the common
delay-growth bound can also be relaxed for the finite-precision result.
Write $b_0=\sqrt{S_0/(2\pi)}$, $R_0=\sup u-u(b_0)$, and
$G_0=u((17/16)b_0)-u(b_0)$. The marginal-utility condition gives
$u'(kx)\leq k^{-22}u'(x)$ for $k\geq1$, $x\geq b_0$. Integrating the tail
at $b_0$ gives $0<R_0\le b_0u'(b_0)/21<\infty$.
After the change of variable $B=kx$ it also yields
\[
\sup u-u(kb_0)\leq k^{-21}R_0.
\]
The first four terms of the binomial expansion give
$(17/16)^7\geq1+7/16+21/256+35/4096>61/40$, and $(61/40)^3>7/2$.
Consequently,
\[
G_0>\frac57R_0>\frac{R_0}{\sqrt2},
\]
where the last inequality follows from $2\cdot5^2>7^2$.

For $E>0$, define $C(E)=\min_{t>0}\{E/t+D(t)\}$.
Strict convexity of $E/t+D(t)$ gives a unique minimizer, satisfying
$E=t^2D'(t)$. Thus $C$ is continuous, strictly increasing from zero to
infinity: the zero limit follows by choosing $t=\sqrt E$, and
$C(E)\leq M$ implies $E\leq M D^{-1}(M)$, which excludes bounded cost as
$E\to\infty$. Convexity and $D(0)=0$ also give
$C(kE)\geq\sqrt{k}C(E)$ for $k\geq1$: substitute $t=\sqrt{k}r$ and use
$D(\sqrt{k}r)\geq\sqrt{k}D(r)$ before taking the minimum.
Define the effort-cost benchmark $c_F>0$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq-delay-cost-condition}
\min_{t>0}\left\{\frac{c_FS_0}{4t}+D(t)\right\}=R_0.
\end{equation}
The preceding properties of $C$ establish existence and uniqueness of
$c_F$, which depends only on $(u,D,S_0)$.
Let $t_u$ be the unique solution of
$D(t_u)+t_uD'(t_u)=R_0$, and put $E_u=t_u^2D'(t_u)$.
Then $C(E_u)=R_0$ and $c_F=4E_u/S_0$.
For every $3c_F/4\leq c\leq2c_F$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq-finite-region-cost-bounds}
C(cS_0/16)\leq C(E_u/2)\leq R_0/\sqrt2<G_0.
\end{equation}
Set
\[
e_0:=\frac{cS_0}{16}.
\]
Let $T_c>0$ be the unique minimizer implementing $C(e_0)$, so
\[
K_c:=\frac{e_0}{T_c}+D(T_c)<G_0.
\]
Set $\theta_c:=e_0/T_c$, $\lambda_c:=0$, and
\[
M_c:=G_0-K_c>0.
\]
We use this positive candidate margin to bound the expenditure of a
potentially optimal design, rather than comparing it only with zero
net gain. For $0<r\le1$, nonnegativity of $D$ gives
\[
C(rE_u)=\min_{t>0}\{rE_u/t+D(t)\}\ge rC(E_u)=rR_0.
\]
With $r=c/c_F\in[3/4,1]$, the candidate therefore satisfies
\[
\frac{C(cS_0/4)+M_c}{R_0}
>r+\frac57-\frac{\sqrt r}{2}>1.
\]
The last function is increasing, and its value at $3/4$ exceeds one
because $13>7\sqrt3$. For $r\ge1$, the same conclusion follows from
$C(cS_0/4)\ge R_0$ and $M_c>0$. Thus throughout the cost interval,
\begin{equation}\label{eq-finite-region-improved-budget}
R_0-M_c<C(cS_0/4).
\end{equation}

Define
\[
a_\sigma:=
\frac{11u'\!\left(\sqrt{S_0/(2\pi)}\right)\sigma^2}
{2\sqrt{2\pi S_0}}.
\]
Convexity and $D(0)=0$ imply that $D(T)/T$ is nondecreasing on $(0,\infty)$. If $a_\sigma<D(T_c)/T_c$, then $a_\sigma T-D(T)\leq0$ for $T\geq T_c$, whereas $a_\sigma T-D(T)\leq a_\sigma T_c$ for $0\leq T\leq T_c$. Therefore,
\begin{equation}\label{eq-delay-conjugate-limit}
\sup_{T\geq0}\{a_\sigma T-D(T)\}\longrightarrow0
\qquad\text{as }\sigma\downarrow0.
\end{equation}
The risk bound can be chosen uniformly over the cost interval. Let $t_8$
solve $t_8^2D'(t_8)=E_u/8$, and put
$\delta_F=5/7-1/\sqrt2>0$. Monotonicity of the minimizing deadline
gives $t_8\leq T_c\leq t_u$, and
$M_c>\delta_FR_0$. Hence a valid positive preliminary risk bound is
\[
\sigma_F^2=
\frac{2\sqrt{2\pi S_0}}{11u'(b_0)t_u}
\min\{D(t_8),\delta_FR_0\}.
\]
It depends only on $(u,D,S_0)$. For $0<\sigma<\sigma_F$,
$a_\sigma<\min\{D(T_c),M_c\}/T_c$ holds throughout the cost interval.
The supremum in equation~\eqref{eq-delay-conjugate-limit} is then strictly
below $M_c$.

Fix $0<\sigma<\sigma_F$. Since $e_0/(cS_0)=1/16$, the exposure is at most $1/16$, and equation~\eqref{eq-design-zero-candidate} applies at $(\theta_c,T_c,0)$. With $\delta:=\sigma^2T_c/S_0$, its right-hand side divided by $\sqrt{S_0/(2\pi)}$ is
\[
\sqrt{1+\delta}+\frac{1}{16\sqrt{1+\delta}}
\geq\frac{17}{16}.
\]
The inequality follows because the left-hand side is increasing for $\delta\geq0$. Therefore, with $J_c(\sigma):=J(\theta_c,T_c,\lambda_c;\sigma)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq-design-candidate-gap}
J_c(\sigma)\geq u\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{S_0}{2\pi}}\right)+M_c.
\end{equation}

We next bound the entire full-disclosure boundary. Concavity of the square root, equation~\eqref{eq-design-exact-upper}, and the tangent bound for $u$ imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq-design-full-envelope}
\sup_{\theta,T\geq0}\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}J(\theta,T,\lambda;\sigma)
\leq u\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{S_0}{2\pi}}\right)+
\sup_{T\geq0}\left\{
\frac{11u'\!\left(\sqrt{S_0/(2\pi)}\right)\sigma^2}{2\sqrt{2\pi S_0}}T-D(T)
\right\}.
\end{equation}
By the choice of $\sigma_F$, the last supremum is strictly below $M_c$. Equations~\eqref{eq-design-candidate-gap}--\eqref{eq-design-full-envelope} therefore show that the finite-precision candidate strictly dominates every full-disclosure design, including those that reoptimize both the prize and the deadline.

It remains to establish attainment on the original unbounded design set. If a design has payoff at least $J_c(\sigma)$, then
\[
\theta+D(T)
\leq \sup_{B\geq0}u(B)-J_c(\sigma)\le R_0-M_c.
\]
Every potentially optimal prize--deadline pair therefore lies in the compact set
\[
\mathcal K:=\{(\theta,T)\in[0,\infty)^2:
\theta+D(T)\leq R_0-M_c\}.
\]
The set is compact because strict monotonicity and convexity imply $D(T)\to\infty$. Equation~\eqref{eq-finite-region-improved-budget} implies, for every $T>0$,
\[
R_0-M_c-D(T)<\frac{cS_0}{4T}.
\]
Since $\theta\leq R_0-M_c-D(T)$ on $\mathcal K$, every design in $\mathcal K$ therefore satisfies $\theta T<cS_0/4$, and hence $\theta T/[c(S_0+\sigma^2T)]\leq1/4$.
The preceding no-signal construction consequently supplies the selected equilibrium and continuity at $\lambda=0$ throughout the payoff-relevant set. Uniform high-precision convergence on $\mathcal K$, together with the strict gap above, gives a finite $\bar\lambda$ such that no design with $(\theta,T)\in\mathcal K$ and $\lambda>\bar\lambda$ attains $J_c(\sigma)$. Designs outside $\mathcal K$ have payoff below $J_c(\sigma)$. The objective is continuous on the compact set $\mathcal K\times[0,\bar\lambda]$ and therefore attains its global maximum there.

Finally, the right-hand side of equation~\eqref{eq-design-full-envelope} also bounds every zero-prize design, because its no-effort output omits the nonnegative $5\sigma^2T/\sqrt{2\pi S_0}$ term used to obtain that envelope. Hence no zero-prize design reaches equation~\eqref{eq-design-candidate-gap}; a zero deadline yields only $u\!\left(\sqrt{S_0/(2\pi)}\right)$. Thus every global maximizer has $\theta^*>0$, $T^*>0$, and $0\leq\lambda^*\leq\bar\lambda<\infty$.

{\color{blue}
\emph{A cost-dependent risk threshold under the common shape assumptions.}
We now prove part (ii) using one fixed cumulative-prize candidate.
Retain $b_0,R_0,C,E_u,c_F$ above, and write
$L=u'(b_0)$ and $K=\sqrt{2\pi S_0}$ only in this proof.
Set $E_5=E_u/5=c_FS_0/20$, let $T_5$ minimize $C(E_5)$,
and choose $(\theta_5,T_5,\lambda_5)=(E_5/T_5,T_5,0)$.
For $c\geq c_F/2$, its exposure is at most $1/10<1/4$.
Equation~\eqref{eq-design-zero-candidate} gives
\[
\mathcal B_5\geq b_0\left(1+\frac{c_F}{20c}\right),
\qquad J_5\geq u(b_0)+M(c),
\]
where
\[
M(c):=u\!\left(b_0\left[1+\frac{c_F}{20c}\right]\right)
-u(b_0)-C(E_5).
\]
Indeed, with $\delta=\sigma^2T_5/S_0$ and
$\eta=c_F/(20c)\leq1/10$, the output bound divided by $b_0$ is
$\sqrt{1+\delta}+\eta/\sqrt{1+\delta}\geq1+\eta$;
its derivative in $\delta$ is positive.

The common delay assumption implies $D(zt)\leq z^{31}D(t)$ for
$0<z\leq1$. Implementing $kE$ at deadline $k^{1/32}t_E$ gives
\[
C(kE)\leq k^{31/32}C(E),\qquad
C(E_5)<\frac{43}{200}R_0,
\]
where the strict comparison is $(1/5)^{31}<(43/200)^{32}$.
Put $a=\gamma_0-1\geq32$. The utility-tail bound gives
\[
M(c)\geq\left\{1-\left(1+\frac{c_F}{20c}\right)^{-a}\right\}R_0
-C(E_5).
\]
For $c\leq ac_F/5$, the gain in braces exceeds $11/50$.
To verify the uniform strict bound, $a\log(1+1/(4a))$ increases
with $a$, and
\[
(1+1/128)^{32}>
\sum_{j=0}^3\binom{32}{j}128^{-j}
=\frac{84059}{65536}>\frac{50}{39}.
\]
Hence $M(c)>R_0/200$ throughout $c_F/2\leq c\leq ac_F/5$.
At $c=c_F$, the stronger comparison
\[
(21/20)^{32}>
\sum_{j=0}^3\binom{32}{j}20^{-j}
=\frac{223}{50}>4
\]
gives $M(c_F)>107R_0/200>R_0/2$.
The function $M$ is continuous and strictly decreasing, with limit
$-C(E_5)$ as $c\to\infty$. Thus there is a unique
$\bar c>ac_F/5>6c_F$ at which $M(\bar c)=0$.
This is the candidate's profitability limit, not a claimed regime boundary.

Define the delay conjugate $H(v)=\sup_{T\geq0}\{vT-D(T)\}$.
Superlinear delay makes $H$ finite and continuous; $H(0)=0$.
For $v>0$, $D'(0)=0$ makes its maximizing deadline positive,
so $H$ is strictly increasing. Evaluating at any fixed positive
deadline gives $H(v)\to\infty$; hence $H^{-1}$ is well defined.
Let $V_0=\max_T F_0(T)$ and let $V_\infty$ denote the fully
reoptimized full-disclosure payoff. The exact envelopes give
\begin{equation}\label{eq-cost-risk-payoff-envelopes}
V_0\leq u(b_0)+H\!\left(\frac{L\sigma^2}{2K}\right),
\qquad
V_\infty\leq u(b_0)+H\!\left(\frac{11L\sigma^2}{2K}\right).
\end{equation}
For $c_F/2\leq c<\bar c$, put
\[
s(c):=\sqrt{\frac{2K}{L}H^{-1}(M(c))}.
\]
Then $\sigma<s(c)$ makes the candidate strictly better than every
zero-prize design, while $\sigma<s(c)/\sqrt{11}$ makes it strictly
better than every full-disclosure design.

There is a second route to excluding full disclosure. Its pointwise
strong-prize envelope, with the prize payment retained, is
\[
J_\infty(\theta,T)\leq u(b_0)+a_\sigma T-\theta-D(T),
\qquad a_\sigma=11L\sigma^2/(2K).
\]
The ratio $D(T)/T$ strictly increases from zero to infinity.
Let $\tau_\sigma$ solve $D(\tau_\sigma)/\tau_\sigma=a_\sigma$.
If a positive-prize full-disclosure design reaches $V_0>u(b_0)$,
then $T<\tau_\sigma$ and $\theta<a_\sigma\tau_\sigma$.
When $c\geq11L\tau_\sigma/K$, this implies
$w=\theta/(c\sigma^2)<1/2$. The exact weak-prize bound
\eqref{eq-exact-full-weak-envelope} and concavity of $u$ then give
\[
J_\infty\leq F_0(T)+\theta
\left(\frac{9LT}{8cK}-1\right)
\leq V_0+\theta\left(\frac9{88}-1\right)<V_0,
\]
a contradiction. Thus full disclosure chooses zero prize whenever
\[
\sigma\leq z(c):=
\sqrt{\frac2cD\!\left(\frac{cK}{11L}\right)}.
\]
This inequality is exactly equivalent to $c\geq11L\tau_\sigma/K$.
It remains to connect this exclusion to prize entry, rather than
imposing a separate candidate-profitability cutoff at high costs.
First, the two exclusion bounds overlap throughout the lower cost band.
At $c=c_F$, put $t_b=c_FK/(11L)$. The utility-tail bound and
$E_u=t_u^2D'(t_u)$ give
\[
\frac{t_b}{t_u}
=\frac{4t_uD'(t_u)K}{11LS_0}
<\frac{4R_0K}{11LS_0}
\leq\frac4{11a}\leq\frac1{88},
\qquad \frac{T_5}{t_u}\geq\frac1{\sqrt5}>\frac13.
\]
The second bound follows from
$T_5^2D'(T_5)=t_u^2D'(t_u)/5$ and monotonicity of $D'$.
Thus $t_b<T_5$. At $\sigma=z(c_F)$,
$a_\sigma=D(t_b)/t_b<D(T_5)/T_5$. Splitting the conjugate
at $T_5$ gives
\[
H(a_\sigma)\leq a_\sigma T_5<D(T_5)
\leq C(E_5)<M(c_F).
\]
Consequently $z(c_F)<s(c_F)/\sqrt{11}$.
Since $z$ increases and $s$ decreases, the same strict ordering
holds for $c_F/2\leq c\leq c_F$.

Extend $d(c)=s(c)/\sqrt{11}$ by zero at $c\geq\bar c$ and choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq-cost-dependent-risk-threshold}
\sigma_F(c):=\max\{d(c),z(c)\},\qquad c\geq c_F/2.
\end{equation}
This threshold is continuous and strictly positive everywhere on
its domain. If $\sigma<\sigma_F(c)$, either a certified candidate
strictly beats the entire full-disclosure boundary ($\sigma<d(c)$),
or full disclosure itself chooses zero prize ($\sigma<z(c)$).
In the latter case, finite precision is optimal whenever a positive
prize is globally worthwhile, not merely when this one candidate pays.

\emph{A common existence and prize-exit argument.}
For $c_F/2\leq c\leq c_F$, the ordering just proved gives
$\sigma_F(c)=d(c)$. Thus the risk condition guarantees a strict
candidate improvement and a payoff-relevant budget
$\theta+D(T)\leq R_0-M(c)$. Concavity of $C$ and $C(0)=0$ give
\[
C(cS_0/4)\geq(c/c_F)R_0\geq R_0/2>R_0-M(c).
\]
For $c\geq c_F$ and every $\sigma>0$, all designs reaching $V_0$
instead satisfy $\theta+D(T)\leq\sup u-V_0<R_0$.
Since $C(cS_0/4)\geq R_0$, they also have $\theta T<cS_0/4$.
Both budgets define compact prize--deadline sets uniformly inside
the certified zero-end domain. The established continuity results
on the precision compactification $[0,\infty]$ therefore give
global attainment throughout the region in part (ii).

\emph{Global attainment above a primitive cost bound.}
Write $u_\infty=\sup_{B\geq0}u(B)$ and $b_0=\sqrt{S_0/(2\pi)}$.
These are proof notation only. The utility curvature bound implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq-design-tail-scaling}
u_\infty-u(kb)\leq k^{1-\gamma_0}[u_\infty-u(b)],
\qquad k\geq1,\quad b\geq b_0.
\end{equation}
Indeed, integration of $u''/u'\leq-\gamma_0/B$ gives
$u'(kx)\leq k^{-\gamma_0}u'(x)$, which can then be integrated
over $x\geq b$. In particular $u_\infty<\infty$.
Recall $C(E)=\min_{t>0}\{E/t+D(t)\}$. Its minimizer is unique,
since its first-order condition is $E=t^2D'(t)$; the right side
strictly increases from zero to infinity. Thus $C$ is continuous,
strictly increasing, concave, and unbounded, with $C(0)=0$.
Define $c_b$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq-design-attainment-cost}
\frac9{230}C(c_bS_0/4)
=u_\infty-u(31b_0/25).
\end{equation}
The positive right side makes this root unique and strictly positive.
The definition of $c_F$ and equation~\eqref{eq-design-tail-scaling}
give
\[
C(c_bS_0/4)\leq\frac{230}{9}(25/31)^{\gamma_0-1}R_0
<R_0=C(c_FS_0/4),
\]
where the strict inequality holds for $\gamma_0\geq45$.
Hence $c_b<c_F$.

Fix $c\geq c_b$, put $E=cS_0/4$ and $r=24/25$.
The delay-growth bound gives $D(zt)\leq z^{\gamma_0}D(t)$ for
$0<z\leq1$. Rescaling a minimizing deadline for $C(E)$ therefore yields
\[
C(rE)\leq r^{\gamma_0/(\gamma_0+1)}C(E)
\leq(1-9/230)C(E).
\]
The last inequality is the concave-power tangent at one, using
$\gamma_0/(\gamma_0+1)\geq45/46$.
Let $t$ minimize $C(rE)$ and choose
$\lambda=0$, $\alpha=6(S_0+\sigma^2t)/(25t)$, and $\theta=c\alpha$.
Its exposure $\alpha t/(S_0+\sigma^2t)=6/25$ lies strictly below
$1/4$. The exact no-signal bound in
equation~\eqref{eq-design-zero-candidate} gives $\mathcal B\geq31b_0/25$.
For this candidate,
\[
u_\infty-J\leq u_\infty-u(31b_0/25)
+6c\sigma^2/25+C(rE)
\leq C(E)+6c\sigma^2/25
< C(E)+c\sigma^2/4.
\]
In contrast, every design with exposure at least $1/4$ has
\[
\theta+D(T)\geq c\sigma^2/4+cS_0/(4T)+D(T)
\geq c\sigma^2/4+C(E),
\]
and is strictly inferior to the candidate, since $u(\mathcal B)<u_\infty$.
The better of this candidate and the no-prize design supplies a
payoff floor. All competitive designs lie in a compact
prize--deadline budget, with exposure uniformly below $1/4$.
The established continuity at both axes and on the precision
compactification therefore gives global attainment for all
$c\geq c_b$ and $\sigma>0$.

\emph{The continuous prize-exit boundary.}
Let $V_0(\sigma)=u(b(T_0))-D(T_0)$, where
$b(T)=\sqrt{(S_0+\sigma^2T)/(2\pi)}$, and put
$R(\sigma)=u_\infty-V_0(\sigma)>0$.
The unique no-prize optimum has $T_0>0$; its value is continuous
and strictly increasing in $\sigma$, by comparison at its positive
deadline. The following curve is used only to prove attainment:
\begin{equation}\label{eq-design-local-attainment-cost}
c_A(\sigma)=4\max_{0\leq T\leq D^{-1}(R(\sigma))}
\frac{T[R(\sigma)-D(T)]}{S_0+\sigma^2T}.
\end{equation}
It is positive and continuous. Its unique maximizing deadline $t_A$
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq-design-attainment-deadline}
R-D(t_A)=\frac{t_AD'(t_A)(S_0+\sigma^2t_A)}{S_0}.
\end{equation}
Uniqueness follows because the derivative numerator
$S_0(R-D(T))-TD'(T)(S_0+\sigma^2T)$ strictly decreases.
Any design reaching $V_0$ satisfies $c\alpha+D(T)\leq R$ and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq-design-exposure-budget}
\frac{\alpha T}{S_0+\sigma^2T}\leq\frac{c_A(\sigma)}{4c}.
\end{equation}
Thus joint optima exist for $c\geq c_A(\sigma)$; for $c>c_A(\sigma)$,
the bound has a strict margin, also locally in the parameters.

We show that at $c=c_A$ a positive prize strictly beats $V_0$.
If $q_0=\sigma^2T_0\geq S_0/3$, put
$U=b(T_0)u'(b(T_0))$. The utility-tail bound and the no-prize
first-order condition give
\[
R\leq\frac{U}{\gamma_0-1}
+\frac{Uq_0}{2\gamma_0(S_0+q_0)},\qquad
c_e\sigma^2=\frac{Uq_0}{S_0+q_0}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\frac{c_A}{c_e}\leq\frac{4R}{c_e\sigma^2}
\leq\frac{16}{\gamma_0-1}+\frac2{\gamma_0}<1.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq-prize-entry-derivative} supplies a profitable
small prize at $T_0$.
If $q_0\leq S_0/3$, instead choose $T=t_A$, $\lambda=0$,
and $\alpha=(S_0+\sigma^2t_A)/(5t_A)$.
Equations~\eqref{eq-design-zero-candidate} and
\eqref{eq-design-attainment-deadline} give
\[
\mathcal B\geq6b_0/5,\qquad
\theta=\frac45[R-D(t_A)],\qquad
D(t_A)\leq R/(\gamma_0+1).
\]
Since $b(T_0)\leq2b_0/\sqrt3$, equation~\eqref{eq-design-tail-scaling}
implies
\[
u_\infty-u(\mathcal B)
\leq(25/27)^{22}R<9R/46,
\qquad \theta+D(t_A)\leq37R/46.
\]
Thus $J>V_0$ in this case as well.

Normalizing the contestants' payoffs shows that
$A(\alpha,T,\lambda;\sigma)
=u(\mathcal B(0,c\alpha,T,\lambda;S_0))-D(T)$ is independent of $c$.
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq-sharp-prize-exit-cost}
c_0(\sigma)=\sup_{\substack{\alpha,T>0,\ \lambda\in[0,\infty]\\
\alpha T/(S_0+\sigma^2T)<1/4}}
\frac{A(\alpha,T,\lambda;\sigma)-V_0(\sigma)}{\alpha}.
\end{equation}
The high-cost zero-prize argument bounds this supremum by a finite
cost. Local prize entry gives $c_0\geq c_e>0$, and the strict
improvement at $c_A$ just proved gives $c_0>c_A$.
If $c<c_0$, its defining quotient supplies a profitable positive
prize. If $c\geq c_0$, equation~\eqref{eq-design-exposure-budget}
puts every competitive design strictly inside the defining domain.
No positive prize can improve on $V_0$; for $c>c_0$, each is
strictly worse. This proves part (i), including ties at equality.
Since $c_0>c_A$, joint attainment holds throughout a neighborhood
of $c_0$ for every fixed $\sigma$.

For continuity, the quotient domain is strictly interior, so every
fixed candidate remains feasible at nearby risks; hence $c_0$ is
lower semicontinuous. To prove upper semicontinuity, suppose
$\sigma_n\to\sigma_*$ and choose a cost
$C>c_0(\sigma_*)$ below the limiting upper values.
Candidates with $A-C\alpha>V_0$ have uniformly bounded $\alpha,T$.
Equation~\eqref{eq-design-basic-output-bound}, the utility tangent,
and $V_0(\sigma_*)>u(b_0)$ also bound their deadlines away from zero.
Since $C>c_0(\sigma_*)>c_A(\sigma_*)$, their exposure is uniformly
below $1/4$. Pass to a subsequence on the precision compactification.
A positive limit of $\alpha$ would give a quotient at least $C$,
contrary to the definition of $c_0(\sigma_*)$.
For $\alpha\to0$, the output expansions proved above give, uniformly
on these positive risk and deadline compact sets and all precisions,
\begin{equation}\label{eq-design-uniform-entry-expansion}
A=F_0(T;\sigma)+\alpha
\frac{u'(b(T))T}{\sqrt{2\pi(S_0+\sigma^2T)}}+O(\alpha^2),
\qquad F_0(T;\sigma)=u(b(T))-D(T).
\end{equation}
Uniformity follows from the individual-output analytic bounds at
high precision, the Fourier estimates on bounded precision
intervals, and bounded $u''$ on the resulting output compact set.
The energy bound and uniqueness of $T_0$ imply $T\to T_0(\sigma_*)$.
Since $F_0\leq V_0$, the limiting upper quotient is at most
$c_e(\sigma_*)\leq c_0(\sigma_*)<C$, again a contradiction.
Therefore $c_0$ is continuous.

If $c<c_0$ and $\sigma<\sigma_F(c)$, the attained value is strictly
above $V_0$. In the candidate branch it is also strictly above
$V_\infty$; in the other branch $V_\infty=V_0$.
Thus in either branch every joint optimum has a positive prize,
a positive deadline, and finite precision. This single comparison
proves the full classification in part (ii).
The earlier candidate-only risk certificate was
$\min\{s(c),\sigma_F(c)\}$ on $c<\bar c$; it implies $c<c_0$

at $6c_F$, hence $c_0(\sigma)>6c_F$.

\emph{A nonempty precision boundary within the attained region.}
The connected open domain promised in the proposition is

Attainment holds throughout $\mathcal W$. Since $c_0>c_A$, it

of its points. The region $c>c_0(\sigma)$ is nonempty and contains
only zero-prize optima. For the precision boundary, consider
the positive-prize subdomain
$\mathcal U=\{(c,\sigma):\sigma>0,\ c_A(\sigma)<c<c_0(\sigma)\}$.
It is open and connected because both bounding curves are
continuous and strictly ordered. Every point admits joint optima
with positive prize and deadline. Write $V$ for the joint value and
$V_\infty$ for the value with precision restricted to infinity.
Both values are continuous locally on $\mathcal U$: the budget in
equation~\eqref{eq-design-exposure-budget} has a uniform strict
margin in a compact neighborhood, so both maxima can be taken
over a common compact domain with continuous output.
Thus $\mathcal F=\{(c,\sigma)\in\mathcal U:V>V_\infty\}$ is open.
Every optimum in $\mathcal F$ has finite precision, whereas at
$V=V_\infty$ a full-disclosure design is jointly optimal.

The path $c(\sigma)=[3c_0(\sigma)+c_A(\sigma)]/4$ lies in

the path therefore lies in $\mathcal F$.
For $\sigma_L<\sigma<\sigma_U$, $c_0=c_e$ and

optimal. Its strict cost and risk inequalities persist in an
open neighborhood, which lies outside $\mathcal F$.
Hence $\mathcal F$ is nonempty and its complement has nonempty
interior in the connected set $\mathcal U$.
Their relative boundary is nonempty. At each boundary point,
continuity gives $V=V_\infty>V_0$, and the restricted optimum is
attained with positive prize and deadline. This proves the precision
boundary assertion and the nonemptiness of the two feedback regions
within $\mathcal W$.
It does not imply a unique boundary, single crossing, or monotonicity.

Finally, for any two attained joint optima at $c_1<c_2$, crossed
optimality inequalities give
$(c_2-c_1)(\alpha_1-\alpha_2)\geq0$.
Thus $\theta_1^*/c_1\geq\theta_2^*/c_2$, without a monotonicity
claim for either the prize or precision itself.}

\emph{Innovation variance and local entry stability.}
Suppose $D$ is twice continuously differentiable near the
positive no-prize optimum $T_0$. If
\[

\qquad \sigma^2T_0\ge\frac7{10}S_0,

then, at $c=2T_0D'(T_0)/\sigma^2$, the prize--deadline Hessian
at $(0,T_0)$ is negative definite for every precision, including
zero precision and full disclosure. The variance condition compares
innovation accumulated without incentives with initial uncertainty;
it involves no jointly optimal design. This is a local statement,
not an exclusion of profitable remote designs.

To prove it, write, in this calculation only,
$b=\sqrt{(S_0+\sigma^2T_0)/(2\pi)}$, $L=u'(b)$,
$q=1+S_0/(\sigma^2T_0)$, $\eta=-bu''(b)/L$, and
$\xi=T_0D''(T_0)/D'(T_0)$. Here $L>0$, $\eta\ge2$,
$\xi\ge0$, and $1<q\le17/7$.
The exact second-order output coefficient established above gives
\[
J_{\theta\theta}=\frac{-\eta+H_\lambda}{bL},
\qquad
H_\lambda=2\sqrt{2\pi}\,q^{3/2}
b_2\!\left(q-1,\lambda\sigma^2T_0^2\right).
\]
The nonnegative second-order kernel is maximized by full disclosure,
and its Gaussian integral representation gives
\[
0\le H_\lambda\le H_\infty
=\frac{2q}{\pi}\int_0^1
\arcsin\!\left(\frac{q-y}{2q-y}\right)dy<\frac q3.
\]
The strict upper bound follows because the argument is below $1/2$
for $y>0$. Differentiating the zero-prize marginal value
$g(T)=u'(b(T))b'(T)$ gives
$g'(T_0)/g(T_0)=-(\eta+1)/(2qT_0)$.
Together with $g(T_0)=D'(T_0)=bL/(2qT_0)$ and
equation~\eqref{eq-prize-entry-derivative}, this yields
\[
J_{\theta T}=\frac{1-(\eta+1)/(2q)}{T_0},
\qquad
J_{TT}=-\frac{bL(\eta+1+2q\xi)}{4q^2T_0^2}<0.
\]
The curvature after allowing the deadline to adjust is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq-local-entry-schur}
bL\left(J_{\theta\theta}-\frac{J_{\theta T}^2}{J_{TT}}\right)
=-\eta+H_\lambda+
\frac{(2q-\eta-1)^2}{\eta+1+2q\xi}
<1-\frac{11q}{3}+\frac{4q^2}{3}
\le-\frac2{49}.
\end{equation}
For the first inequality, replace the denominator by $\eta+1$
and then use $\eta\ge2$. The last quadratic is convex in $q$;
its values at $1$ and $17/7$ are $-4/3$ and $-2/49$.
The negative Schur complement and $J_{TT}<0$ prove the claim.
The second-order expansion is a derivative of the exact objective
at zero prize, not a replacement of output by a truncated policy.

If $D'(0)=0$ is relaxed, the same limitation of a local prize-entry criterion occurs with moderate utility curvature and a
positive no-prize deadline. Set $S_0=\sigma^2=1$, take $u(B)=-1/B$
for $B\ge b_0:=1/\sqrt{2\pi}$, and extend it below $b_0$ as a
twice continuously differentiable, increasing, concave function.
Let $D(T)=dT$, where $d=4\sqrt{2\pi}/(5\sqrt5)$.
The unique no-prize optimum is $T_0=1/4$, with output
$b=\sqrt{5/(8\pi)}$. Its local prize-entry cost is
$c_e=2T_0d=d/2$, and $db=2/5$.
For each $c\in[c_e,c_e+1/(400b)]$, consider
$\theta=c/50$, $T=27/100$, and $\lambda=0$.
Its exposure is $\theta T/[c(1+T)]=27/6350<1/4$,
so the no-signal equilibrium and equation~\eqref{eq-design-zero-candidate}
apply. They give
\[
\frac{\mathcal B(0,c/50,27/100,0;1)}{b}
\ge\sqrt{\frac{127}{125}}\,\frac{6377}{6350}.
\]
At $c=c_e$, its payoff improvement over the best no-prize design is
at least
\[
\frac1b\left[\frac{247}{250}
-\frac{6350}{6377}\sqrt{\frac{125}{127}}\right]
>\frac{1}{10000b}.
\]
Indeed, the difference of the two squared terms in brackets is
$531114161/2541633062500>1/5000$, and their sum is below $2$.
Output depends on $c$ only through $\theta/c=1/50$; raising $c$
within the stated interval therefore reduces this improvement by
at most $1/(20000b)$. The candidate still strictly improves every
no-prize design. Yet equation~\eqref{eq-prize-entry-derivative} gives
the marginal prize return $c_e/c-1<0$ at $T_0$ whenever $c>c_e$.
This example compares exact payoffs; it does not identify the globally
optimal precision.

If $D'(0)=0$ is relaxed, an explicit cost interval is available when marginal delay costs rise
by at most a factor of two: $0<D'(0)\le D'(t)\le2D'(0)$ for all $t\ge0$.
Then $D'(0)t\le D(t)\le2D'(0)t$, so
$2\sqrt{D'(0)E}\le C(E)\le2\sqrt{2D'(0)E}$.
Consequently $R_0^2/[2S_0D'(0)]\le c_F\le R_0^2/[S_0D'(0)]$.
Under the utility and risk conditions of part (ii), the directly
checkable interval
\[
\frac{[\sup u-u(\sqrt{S_0/(2\pi)})]^2}{2S_0D'(0)}
\le c\le
\frac{3[\sup u-u(\sqrt{S_0/(2\pi)})]^2}{2S_0D'(0)}
\]
lies within $[c_F/2,3c_F]$ and therefore ensures global attainment,
a positive prize and deadline, and finite optimal precision.
This additional delay-cost condition is not required for the general
region in part (ii).

If relative utility curvature is also bounded above, the cost interval
can be checked using initial marginal utility alone. Specifically,
suppose, for some $\gamma\ge32$,
\[
\gamma u'(B)\le-Bu''(B)\le\frac{3\gamma-1}{2}u'(B)
\qquad(B\ge\sqrt{S_0/(2\pi)}).
\]
Under the same marginal-delay bound and risk condition, the interval
\begin{equation}\label{eq-primitive-finite-cost-band}
\frac{[u'(\sqrt{S_0/(2\pi)})]^2}
{4\pi(\gamma-1)^2D'(0)}
\le c\le
\frac{[u'(\sqrt{S_0/(2\pi)})]^2}
{3\pi(\gamma-1)^2D'(0)}
\end{equation}
also ensures a global joint optimum with positive prize and deadline
and finite precision. Indeed, integrating the curvature bounds gives
$2b_0u'(b_0)/[3(\gamma-1)]\le R_0\le b_0u'(b_0)/(\gamma-1)$.
The implementation-cost bounds above then imply
\[
\frac{[u'(b_0)]^2}{9\pi(\gamma-1)^2D'(0)}
\le c_F\le
\frac{[u'(b_0)]^2}{2\pi(\gamma-1)^2D'(0)}.
\]
Thus equation~\eqref{eq-primitive-finite-cost-band} lies within
$[c_F/2,3c_F]$. This additional curvature upper bound is not needed
for the general finite-precision region.

This subclass relaxes the common delay-growth bound: its elasticity
$tD'(t)/D(t)$ is at most $2$, rather than at least $\gamma_0\geq33$.
The alternative window in equation~\eqref{eq-full-disclosure-primitive-window}
also excludes this subclass: its delay conditions imply elasticity
at least $3\gamma/4$ at $t=S_0/\sigma^2$.
This lack of coverage does not exclude full disclosure outside the
certified finite-precision region.

\emph{A global positive-prize region without a disclosure ranking.}
Suppose $-Bu''(B)\ge7u'(B)$ for $B\ge\sqrt{S_0/\pi}$.
If $\sigma>0$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq-positive-joint-region}
\frac{19\sigma^2u'(\sqrt{S_0/\pi})}{80\sqrt{\pi S_0}}
\le D'(S_0/\sigma^2)
\le\frac{\sigma^2u'(\sqrt{S_0/\pi})}{4\sqrt{\pi S_0}},
\end{equation}
and
\[
\frac{9\sqrt{S_0}u'(\sqrt{S_0/\pi})}{20\sqrt\pi\,\sigma^2}
\le c<\frac{19\sqrt{S_0}u'(\sqrt{S_0/\pi})}{40\sqrt\pi\,\sigma^2},
\]
the original compactified joint problem attains its global maximum,
and every maximizer has $\theta^*>0$ and $T^*>0$.
There is no restriction on average delay costs, but this result does
not determine whether $\lambda^*$ is finite or infinite.

For the proof, write $\tau=S_0/\sigma^2$,
$K=\sqrt{S_0}u'(\sqrt{S_0/\pi})/(4\sqrt\pi)$,
and $g(T)=u'(b(T))b'(T)$.
The delay window is $19K/20\le\tau D'(\tau)\le K$,
where $g(\tau)=K/\tau$, so $T_0\ge\tau$.
The curvature assumption implies
$g(T)\le g(\tau)[2\tau/(\tau+T)]^4$ for $T\ge\tau$.
Since $(50/51)^4<19/20$, strict concavity of $F_0$ gives
$T_0<26\tau/25$. Set
\[
q=\frac{\tau+T_0}{T_0}\in(51/26,2],\qquad
b_*=b(T_0),\qquad L=u'(b_*),\qquad d=D'(T_0).
\]
The no-prize first-order condition gives
$d=Lb'(T_0)$ and the entry cost $c_e=2T_0d/\sigma^2$.
Convex delay and $T_0\ge\tau$ give $c_e\ge19K/(10\sigma^2)$.
Moreover $Tg(T)$ decreases for $T\ge\tau$, since its logarithmic
derivative is at most $1/T-4/(\tau+T)<0$.
Hence $c_e\le2K/\sigma^2$, and the stated cost interval implies
$c<c_e$ and $c/c_e\ge9/10$.
Equation~\eqref{eq-prize-entry-derivative} now provides a strictly
profitable positive prize at $T_0$ and any fixed positive finite precision.

Let $R=\sup u-u(b_*)$. Integrating the curvature bound gives
$R\le Lb_*/6$. Any design that matches or exceeds the best no-prize
payoff satisfies $\theta+D(T)\le R+D(T_0)$.
The delay-cost tangent therefore implies
\[
\theta+d(T-T_0)\le R.
\]
For $x=T/T_0$ and $w=\theta/(c\sigma^2)$, use
$c_e\sigma^2=Lb_*/q$ and $dT_0=Lb_*/(2q)$ to obtain
\[
0\le w\le\frac{10}{9}\left[\frac q6+\frac{1-x}{2}\right].
\]
Thus $w\le25/27$ and $T<43\tau/25$.
The joint bound, rather than these separate bounds, controls the
no-signal boundary. Define
\[
P_q(x)=\frac{x^2}{2}-(11/40+q/6)x+\frac9{40}(q-1).
\]
The expression $(11/40+q/6)^2-(9/20)(q-1)$ decreases on
$51/26\le q\le2$ and is $-19031/270400<0$ at $q=51/26$.
Consequently $P_q(x)>0$, which yields
\[
\frac{\theta T}{c(S_0+\sigma^2T)}
=\frac{wx}{q-1+x}<\frac14.
\]
At $T=0$ the exposure is zero.
The closed triangle $\theta,T\ge0$,
$\theta+d(T-T_0)\le R$ is compact, satisfies this exposure bound,
and contains every design that could beat the positive-prize candidate.
The no-signal continuity construction above applies on this triangle;
positive-precision continuity and uniform high-precision convergence
extend the objective continuously to its compactified precision domain.
It therefore attains a maximum. The candidate exceeds $F_0(T_0)$,
which in turn exceeds $u(b(0))$, excluding both zero-prize and
zero-deadline maxima. These derived bounds do not restrict the original
feasible domain.

If $D'(0)=0$ is relaxed, the conditions are nonempty even with linear delay: take
$S_0=\sigma^2=1$, $u(B)=-AB^{-6}$ for $B\ge1/\sqrt\pi$,
with $A>0$ and an increasing concave $C^2$ extension below this range,
and set $D(T)=(39/40)g(1)T$.
This verifies existence of the external parameter region, not a
disclosure ranking.

The main finite-precision region is nonempty analytically. For example, take
$S_0=2\pi$, $D(t)=3t^2$, and $u(B)=1-e^{-100(B-1)}$.
Then $-Bu''(B)/u'(B)=100B\geq100$ for $B\geq1$, $R_0=1$,
$t_u=1/3$, and $c_F=4/(9\pi)$. The entire interval
$2/(9\pi)\leq c\leq4/(3\pi)$ therefore satisfies part (ii) for a common
sufficiently small positive innovation risk. No numerical optimization
is used to establish feasibility.
\Halmos
\endproof
