2026-10-07: A precision-uniform first-variation estimate

Status: the first-variation and second-output-coefficient bounds below
are proved directly. They do not control finite-prize higher variations
and do not replace the manuscript's global output estimate.

1. Exact imbalance coefficient

Keep sigma,T,S0 and precision fixed; write alpha=theta/c.
At alpha=0, H=2G_Q and A=0. The first imbalance coefficient is

  A_1(y,t)=-integral_t^T partial_y P_(Q_t-Q_s)(G_(Q_s)^2)(y) ds,
  Q_t=S_t+sigma^2(T-t).

Gaussian multiplication gives, with q=Q_t and v_s=q-Q_s/2,

  A_1(y,t)= y/(2 sqrt(pi)) integral_t^T
                  Q_s^(-1/2) G_(v_s)(y)/v_s ds.           (G1)

Thus A_1 has the sign of y. The estimate below only uses
0<Q_s<=q and Q_s>=sigma^2(T-s), valid at every precision.

2. A sharp Gaussian-relative bound from the Riccati clock

Set z=|y|/sqrt(q), u=sigma^2(T-s)/q, r=Q_s/q, and

  K(r,z)=r^(-1/2)(1-r/2)^(-3/2)
             exp[-z^2 r/(4(1-r/2))].

Since u<=r<=1 and u_t=sigma^2(T-t)/q<=1, (G1) becomes

  |A_1(y,t)|/G_q(y)
     = z/(2 sqrt(pi) sigma^2) integral_0^(u_t) K(r(u),z)du. (G2)

The sign of partial_r log K is the sign of

  N(r)=-2+5r-2r^2-rz^2.

If z>=1, N(r)<=-2(1-r)^2<=0. If 0<z<1, N increases
strictly on [0,1], from a negative to a positive value. Thus K
is either decreasing or decreases and then increases; it has
no interior maximum on any interval [r0,1].

Along a Riccati path, a_s=lambda S_s^2 is either everywhere
at least sigma^2 or everywhere at most sigma^2: S never
crosses its stationary value. Also r'(u)=a_s/sigma^2,
r(0)=r0=S_T/q>0 and r(u_t)=1.

If r'>=1, positivity gives

  integral_0^(u_t) K(r(u),z)du
       <= integral_(r0)^1 K(r,z)dr <= integral_0^1 K(r,z)dr.

If r'<=1, split du=r'du+(1-r')du. Since u_t<=1,

  integral_0^(u_t) K(r(u),z)du
       <= integral_(r0)^1 K(r,z)dr
          +r0 max{K(r0,z),K(1,z)}.                       (G2a)

If K(r0,z)>K(1,z), the no-interior-maximum property implies
K is decreasing on [0,r0], so r0 K(r0,z)<=integral_0^(r0) K.
If K(r0,z)<=K(1,z), then K<=K(1,z) throughout [r0,1], and
the right side of (G2a) is at most K(1,z). The substitution
v=sqrt(u/(2-u)) evaluates the full integral exactly:

  z/(2 sqrt(pi)) integral_0^1 K(u,z)du
     =z sqrt(2/pi) integral_0^1 exp(-z^2 v^2/2)dv
     =2Phi(z)-1 <=1.

This full integral is at least K(1,z), after multiplying by
z/(2 sqrt(pi)): the inequality is
2Phi(z)-1>=sqrt(2/pi) z exp(-z^2/2), obtained by integrating
the decreasing Gaussian density on [0,z]. Hence (G2a) is
also bounded by the full integral. This argument includes
the constant r=1 path at zero precision.

At z=0 both sides of (G1) vanish. Therefore, at every precision,

  |A_1(y,t)| <= [|2Phi(y/sqrt(Q_t))-1|/sigma^2]
                         G_(Q_t)(y).                    (G3)

This includes no signal. In the full-disclosure conditional game,
Q_t=sigma^2(T-t) and the exact expression is
A_1=g(y/sqrt(Q_t))G_Q/sigma^2, attaining the Gaussian-relative
bound in (G3).
Full-disclosure initial output additionally averages the initial
prior; the conditional Q_0 must not be confused with S0+sigma^2T.

3. A single-integral second-output comparison

Let f(w)=B(w)/b(T), w=alpha/sigma^2 and
k=sigma^2T/(S0+sigma^2T). Its first two Taylor coefficients obey

  f_1=k,
  0<=f_2<=(1/pi) integral_0^k
                      asin((1-v)/(2-v))dv <=k/6.         (G4)

Indeed, the first H variation vanishes, and the alpha^2 output
coefficient is integral_0^T P_(Q_0-Q_t)(A_1 beta_y)(0)dt.
Here beta_y=g_Q/2, so the integrand is nonnegative by (G1)
and bounded above by g_Q^2 G_Q/(2sigma^2), by (G3).
Gaussian multiplication and the two-normal sign formula give

  P_(Q0-Q)(g_Q^2 G_Q)(0)
    =(2/pi) G_(Q0)(0) asin((Q0-Q)/(2Q0-Q)).

As Q_t>=sigma^2(T-t), this arcsine is bounded above by
the same expression with Q_t replaced by sigma^2(T-t).
Substituting v=sigma^2(T-t)/Q0 gives (G4). Full disclosure
attains the single-integral upper bound, after initial-prior
averaging. At finite precision S_t>0 makes the arcsine
comparison strict; its integral is strictly below that bound.

At k=1 the integral is
1/3-(sqrt(3)-1)/pi<1/8. Continuity gives f_2<1/8 uniformly
over all precisions when k is sufficiently close to one.

This directly verifies the second-order full-disclosure ranking,
without the double kernel or the stationary-profile calculation.
It does not supply a precision-uniform small-positive-prize
ranking: as lambda tends to infinity, the strict second-order
gap tends to zero, so the higher-order remainder still needs
relative control. The manuscript's high-precision tail argument
cannot be deleted on the strength of (G4) alone.

4. Why (G3) is not a nonlinear Gaussian-norm contraction

It is tempting to infer a finite Gaussian-norm bound for
T(f,g)=integral_t^T partial_y P_(Q_t-Q_s)(f_s g_s)ds
from |f_s|,|g_s|<=G_(Q_s). That inference is false: differentiation
of a positive Gaussian envelope does not preserve its ordering.

At zero precision Q_s=q is constant. Take
f(y)=G_q(y)sin(Ny), g(y)=G_q(y). Both have Gaussian-weighted
supremum norm at most one, but

  |T(f,g)(0)|/G_q(0)=(T-t)N G_q(0) -> infinity.

Even the odd/even symmetry of A,H does not prevent this example.
Thus (G3) is an exact first-coefficient result, not a bilinear
operator bound on arbitrary Gaussian-weighted functions.

5. A simpler finite real-variation research target

Using the coarse f_2<=k/6 in (G4), it would suffice to establish,
uniformly on 0<=w<=.252,

  f is C^3 and f'''(w)<=45.                               (G5)

Taylor's integral formula would then give

  f(w)<=1+k w+(1/6)k w^2+(15/2)w^3.

For 0<w<1/4 this is strictly below (1-4w)^(-1/4), whose
first coefficients are 1,1,5/2,15/2 and whose remaining
coefficients are positive. The scalar payoff comparison would
follow as in ROOT_OUTPUT_ANALYTIC_CERTIFICATE.txt, Section 9.

The third-variation bound (G5) is NOT proved by (G3) or (G4).
The exact expected-source hierarchy in REAL_OUTPUT_VARIATION_AUDIT.txt
remains the appropriate place to try to establish it. Do not
turn (G5) into a manuscript assumption or claim global exclusion
on the basis of finitely many prize derivatives at zero.
