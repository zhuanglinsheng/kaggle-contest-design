## Overall Feedback

**Observation system mapping**

The formulation of the public-signal model transitions between continuous and discrete regimes in ways that alter the implied statistical experiment. Section 2 introduces a continuous-time signal process, $dZ_t=y_tdt+dB_t/\sqrt{\lambda}$, making $Z_t$ an integrated noisy observation of the latent state. However, Section 3 concurrently maps the public leaderboard gap to an empirical derivative $dZ_t=\hat y_tdt$ and treats it as a noisy point observation $\hat y_{t_k}=y_{t_k}+\varepsilon_k$ with covariance $\sigma^2\min(t_a,t_b)+I/(\Delta\lambda)$. Furthermore, Equation (13) brings the observed public gap into the continuous-time filter as a deterministic correction, while the likelihood specifies this same gap as noisy. Readers would benefit from the paper choosing a single, unified observation regime—such as a continuous noisy signal, interval averages, or discrete noisy leaderboard measurements—and allowing the filter, update timing, covariance matrix, and likelihood implementations to flow directly from that choice.

**Information precision and the baseline payoff**

The paper motivates its approach heavily around how contestants dynamically react to noisy public leaderboard feedback via Bayesian updating. Yet, the baseline model substitutes the fully Bayesian terminal payoff $\theta\Phi(\tilde y_T/\sqrt{\bar S})$ with the threshold payoff $\theta\mathbf 1_{\tilde y_T>0}$. Combined with the steady-state restriction $S_t=\bar S$, the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman diffusion term becomes $\lambda\bar S^2=\sigma^2$. Consequently, $\lambda$ drops out of the equilibrium effort policy and aggregate effort comparative statics entirely, a neutrality that Section 2.1 acknowledges. This structural property produces a mismatch with the introduction's framing of signal precision as central to the dynamic strategic behavior. The manuscript might reconcile this by treating $\lambda$ primarily as a measurement and filtering primitive, or by quantifying the approximation loss relative to the smooth Bayesian payoff or a setup with time-varying filtering variance.

**Parameter scale and prior bounds**

There is an apparent discontinuity regarding the scale of the effort submission parameters. Lemma 1 establishes identification conditional on fixing $r$, but the empirical implementation jointly estimates $r$ alongside other parameters via $\tau_i(t)=r m_i(\tilde y_t,t)$. Because $r$ scales latent equilibrium effort into observed rates, it can absorb level differences that would identify absolute cost and ability factors $(c_i,c_j)$. The synthetic recovery tables omit $r$, making it difficult to assess how this scaling ambiguity interacts with likelihood recovery. Additionally, Section 3.3 sets the truncated prior support for $\lambda$ to $[10^{-6},10]$. However, Table 7 reports many posterior means well above 10, with values in the 20s and 30s. The statistical exercises will be clearer once the joint scale identification constraint is reconciled with the estimation setup and the posterior bounds are aligned with the specified prior support.

**Validation construction and separation**

The validation exercises use the private leaderboard to check out-of-sample consistency, but the separation between the estimation sample and validation targets breaks down in certain steps. Section 5.1 isolates focal players strictly through their final private-leaderboard rankings, and the alternative 2nd–3rd pair in Section 6.1 similarly relies on final private ranks. Selecting the sample using the withheld object weakens the out-of-sample claim. Furthermore, the validation benchmarks reflect different levels of structural independence. The $\sigma^{MLE}$ benchmark in Section 5.2.3 subtracts posterior effort trajectories, representing a model-based consistency check rather than a fully external target. Separately, the $\lambda^{MLE}$ benchmark relies on an inverse-quadratic mapping of public-private discrepancies that produces extreme dispersion in Table 7. The validation framework would carry more diagnostic weight if the baseline pair rules used contest-phase public observables, if model-free private-path metrics took precedence, and if the regressions included checks on public data fractions and observation counts.

**Prize-duration effort simulations**

The counterfactual simulations in Section 5.3 generate effort-improvement predictions by fixing the prize and allowing duration to vary across $T\in[0.25T^{obs},3T^{obs}]$. Proposition 1 establishes that predicted total effort is strictly increasing in duration under the model's steady-state approximations. Therefore, the finding that 73 of 73 contests exhibit positive effort-improvement margins is a mechanical implication of extending the contest duration. The alternative prize-saving setup analogously substitutes lower baseline prizes for longer horizons. Neither exercise incorporates the $\beta^T$ time cost specified in Section 2.1 or a platform-side delay cost. Rather than framing the results as discoveries of observed design inefficiency, the section could present these findings strictly as diagnostics tracking the model-implied prize-duration frontier, or strictly bound the counterfactual search space using calibrated delay costs.
